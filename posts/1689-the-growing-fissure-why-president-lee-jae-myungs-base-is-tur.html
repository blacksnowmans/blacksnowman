The Growing Fissure: Why President Lee Jae-myung’s Base is Turning Against Him

October 29, 2026

For many observers of South Korean politics, the current landscape feels increasingly precarious. Recent commentary by the prominent intellectual and former politician Rhyu Si-min, featured on the popular YouTube channel Park Si-hyung TV, has resonated deeply with the public. While he offered no shocking new revelations, his analysis articulated the vague sense of anxiety that has been gnawing at the supporters of the current Democratic Party of Korea (DPK)—the liberal party currently holding the presidency—for months.

We are witnessing more than just a temporary dip in approval ratings; we are seeing a fundamental cracking of the political foundation. Critics argue that President Lee Jae-myung, who rose to power as a champion of progressive reform, is systematically dismantling the very coalition that put him in office. The fear among his own base is that he is repeating the exact mistakes of the imprisoned former president, Yoon Suk-yeol, whose alienation of his own conservative base paved the way for his eventual downfall and impeachment.

The Essence of the "Dismantling" Thesis

Rhyu Si-min’s two-hour long appearance was less about exposing secrets and more about providing a clear framework for the confusion currently felt by the DPK’s base. When official polling data is volatile, supporters crave an interpretation that validates their intuition. Rhyu’s argument was stark: the Lee Jae-myung administration is failing to grasp the gravity of its isolation.

There are those, often within the president’s own inner circle, who dismiss such criticism as the cause of falling approval ratings. This is a dangerous miscalculation. If the stability of an administration can be shaken by a single political commentator’s analysis, it is a testament to how fragile the administration’s governance really is. President Lee currently maintains an approval rating hovering in the 30% range. If this floor collapses, the administration will face a political storm from which it may not recover.

History serves as a stern teacher: leaders who refuse to adjust their governing style in response to the public mood inevitably face a bleak fate. The arrogance of assuming that a leader can dictate to their base, rather than serving the values they were elected to protect, is the quickest path to political obsolescence.

The Trap of "SNS Politics"

The administration’s disconnect from the public is perhaps most visible in President Lee’s recent behavior on social media. In a move that drew widespread criticism, the President retweeted a post from FM Korea (a popular online community known for its often polarizing and anti-progressive discourse) that criticized the government’s policy of prioritizing multicultural families in public postpartum care centers. By thanking the user for their "excellent point," the President effectively signaled his agreement with a narrative that frames vulnerable minorities as a burden on the state.

This is not the behavior expected of a head of state. A leader’s job is to solve problems through policy and systemic reform—not to engage in emotional, reactionary squabbles in online comment sections. By validating divisive rhetoric, the President is feeding the very "us-versus-them" mentality that threatens to tear his own support base apart. His digital obsession is not just a distraction; it is a tactical error that wastes the political capital he was elected to use for meaningful change.

The moment a head of state validates the exclusionary logic of internet communities, the societal damage is immense. A leader is meant to heal social conflicts and design structural solutions, not to act as a ringleader for petty resentment.

The Historical Context of Democratic Support

To understand the current crisis, one must understand how the liberal camp in South Korea functions. Supporters did not back figures like the late Kim Dae-jung or Roh Moo-hyun simply out of a cult-like devotion to their personalities. They backed them because they were seen as the most capable instruments to execute the progressive agenda. Supporters viewed these leaders as tools to achieve shared values, not as masters to be blindly followed.

The current faction around President Lee—often dubbed the "New Lee Jae-myung" group—seems to have forgotten this. They operate under the delusion that they are the owners of the party and that supporters are mere accessories. This arrogance has created a "pro-Lee" exclusionary culture where those who do not strictly adhere to the President's personal line are purged or sidelined. This behavior is already creating deep fractures within the Democratic Party. If this exclusive, factional dominance continues, it will inevitably lead to internal splintering, handing the next election to the conservative opposition, the People Power Party, on a silver platter.

A Strategic Course Correction Needed

The controversy surrounding the postpartum care centers is a microcosm of a larger failure. The policy was designed to support areas where birth infrastructure has collapsed, particularly in rural provinces where multicultural families play a vital role in local demographics. By attacking this policy to curry favor with online populists, the President is undermining his own stated goals of balanced regional development.

So, what should supporters watch next?

  • Key Personnel Departures: Watch for resignations among the President’s inner circle. Recent departures, such as those of high-ranking allies, suggest that even those closest to the President are beginning to realize the unsustainability of his current trajectory.
  • Legislative Autonomy: Observe how the Democratic Party acts in the National Assembly. If voices of dissent—those who prioritize the party's values over the President’s personal whims—begin to gain traction, it may signal a necessary recalibration of the party’s power structure.
  • Policy Tone: Keep an eye on the government’s public rhetoric. If it continues to prioritize divisive "culture war" issues over structural economic reform, the alienation of the core base will become irreversible.

The base remains. The people who stood in the cold to support these candidates are still here, and they are not dolls to be played with. They are sovereign citizens who are prepared to hold their representatives accountable. If the Lee Jae-myung administration continues its current drift, it risks being remembered as a transient, destructive force that squandered a historic opportunity. The path to restoration lies in humility, a return to the values that founded the movement, and a long overdue "digital detox" from the toxicity of online populism.
