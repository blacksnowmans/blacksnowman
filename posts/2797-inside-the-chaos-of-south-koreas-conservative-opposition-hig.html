Inside the Chaos of South Korea's Conservative Opposition: High-Stakes Mutiny Hits the People Power Party

November 27, 2026

The cameras were rolling inside the headquarters of the People Power Party, South Korea's main conservative opposition party, but what unfolded on November 26 was anything but a rehearsed press conference. Instead, top leadership broke into an open screaming match over allegations of a rigged internal disciplinary process targeting former party leader Han Dong-hoon. As insult after insult echoed across the room—ranging from threats of party expulsions to accusations of harboring "moles" from the ruling party—the party's floor leader, Jeong Jeom-sik, threw his hands up in disgust. Muttering "Look at the state of this party," he stormed out mid-meeting, leaving the executive leadership in complete disarray.

For observers of South Korean politics, the spectacle marked a dangerous turning point. What began as quiet factional maneuvering has erupted into a full-scale civil war between the leadership faction surrounding current party chief Jang Dong-hyeok and pro-Han loyalists. With local chapters revolting, senior lawmakers demanding emergency sessions, and rival politicians warning of electoral ruin, the People Power Party faces a crisis of authority at a moment when it can ill afford one.

A Leadership Clash Over "Rigged Discipline" and Party Control

To understand the explosive scene on November 26, one must look at the widening chasm within the main opposition party. Ever since the transfer of power to President Lee Jae-myung and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, the People Power Party has struggled to present a united front. Rather than mounting a coherent opposition strategy, internal warfare has consumed the conservative camp.

At the center of the storm is an ongoing investigation into allegations that internal disciplinary actions against former party leader Han Dong-hoon were fabricated or unlawfully manipulated by party management. The pro-Han faction ("친한계") claims that current leadership orchestrated a "rigged disciplinary process" to purge rival voices and cement control over party machinery. Conversely, the leadership faction ("당권파") around Party Leader Jang Dong-hyeok has pushed back aggressively against these claims, viewing them as an intolerable challenge to party authority.

The tension boiled over after Jang Dong-hyeok took swift action against internal whistleblowers. On the evening of November 26, reports confirmed that Jang had abruptly dismissed Hwang Gyeong-seong, a member of the party’s Ethics Committee who had publicly raised allegations regarding the rigged disciplinary process. Rather than launching a transparent inquiry to address the claims, the leadership chose to remove the official asking the questions—a move that non-mainstream factions saw as confirmation of wrongdoing and a heavy-handed authoritarian maneuver.

A Public Explosion: Walkouts, Purges, and Accusations of "Moles"

The immediate fallout was visible during the November 26 Supreme Council meeting. Woo Jae-jun, a Supreme Council member aligned with the party's critical wing, confronted Party Leader Jang directly on camera, demanding a complete revelation of the facts behind the disciplinary manipulation. The exchange quickly escalated into shouting, with members firing back rhetoric rarely heard in official party proceedings.

During the confrontation, members traded hostile jabs, asking "Are you going to expel me too?" while others shouted that "leaving the party is a good option" or suggesting dissenters should "leave and start a new party." The rhetoric reached a fever pitch when leadership figures began calling for a purge of "Democratic Party moles" within their own ranks. When Jang Dong-hyeok refused to apologize and instead raised the prospect of party departures, Floor Leader Jeong Jeom-sik abruptly walked out, uttering his now-viral condemnation of the party's deplorable state.

"Look at the state of this party..."
— Jeong Jeom-sik, People Power Party Floor Leader, before storming out of the Supreme Council meeting on November 26.

The meltdown immediately triggered backlash beyond the party headquarters:

  • Han Dong-hoon's Counter-Offensive: Former leader Han Dong-hoon publicly signaled that he would mobilize "all available methods"—including potential legal and political measures—to fight what he characterizes as an illegal purge.
  • Grassroots Revolt: Local party members in the central Chungcheong region issued scathing statements criticising the executive bench, openly questioning whether such leadership can legitimately represent South Korea's conservative movement.
  • Senior Lawmaker Interventions: Seeing the executive council collapse, senior party figures immediately rallied to demand an emergency General Assembly of party lawmakers to strip leadership of sole decision-making authority over the scandal.

Echoes of Past Defeats: Conspiracy Theories and Street Politics

The internal fight is further complicated by the party's ideological drift. As leadership attempts to consolidate power internally, it has increasingly leaned into hardline tactics, including street demonstrations and unproven allegations of election fraud. This shift has drawn sharp criticism from across the political spectrum, including from prominent figures within the broader opposition camp.

Lee Jun-seok, a lawmaker from the reformist opposition Reform Party and a former leader of the conservative party himself, issued a stark warning targeting Jang Dong-hyeok on November 26. Lee publicly asked whether Jang was "following the path of Hwang Kyo-ahn"—referring to the former conservative party leader whose reliance on far-right street rallies and election fraud conspiracies led the party to a catastrophic general election defeat in 2020.

Lee warned that by embracing conspiracy theories and resorting to hostile purges—labeling internal rivals as "moles"—the People Power Party risks alienating moderate voters and ensuring another devastating defeat in upcoming national elections. When a major political party substitutes internal verification procedures with accusations of treason and conspiracy, its foundational democratic credibility inevitably erodes.

What to Watch Next: The Upcoming Battlegrounds

With the executive council paralyzed and factional distrust at an all-time high, the focus now shifts from public rhetoric to formal party machinery. Over the coming days, several critical events will determine whether the People Power Party can contain the crisis or head toward a formal party split.

  • The November 27 Floor Strategy Meeting: Following his dramatic walkout, Floor Leader Jeong Jeom-sik is scheduled to chair a Floor Strategy Meeting on November 27. Observers will be watching closely to see if parliamentary leadership attempts to rein in the executive council and establish a distinct line of authority.
  • The Emergency General Assembly (Nov 30 or Dec 2): Senior lawmakers have demanded a gathering of all party parliamentarians. Reports indicate the crucial meeting will take place either on November 30 or December 2. This session will serve as the first major showdown, where lawmakers will formally debate the rigged disciplinary allegations and deliberate on the dismissal of Ethics Committee member Hwang Gyeong-seong.
  • Han Dong-hoon’s Legal and Political Strategy: Having pledged to use "all available methods," former leader Han's next steps—whether filing for court injunctions against party actions or launching an organized factional caucus—will test the leadership's control over the party base.
  • Legislative Vulnerability in Parliament: As the main opposition party consumes itself in internal strife, the ruling Democratic Party under President Lee Jae-myung is pushing forward with key legislative priorities, including special counsel nominations targeting past prosecutorial misconduct. How effectively—or helplessly—the fractured PPP responds to the ruling party's moves will reveal the true political cost of this internal war.
