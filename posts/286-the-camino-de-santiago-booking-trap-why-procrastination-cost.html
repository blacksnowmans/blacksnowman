The Camino de Santiago Booking Trap: Why Procrastination Costs You

September 07, 2026

If you have decided to walk the Camino de Santiago—the ancient pilgrimage route across Spain—you are likely oscillating between intense excitement and a vague, persistent anxiety. The 800-kilometer (about 500-mile) journey is a massive undertaking, and the reality of the logistics often hits the moment you realize that booking your flight was just the prelude to a logistical battle. If you wait until the last minute to secure your transportation or initial accommodation, you are setting yourself up for the worst-case scenario: "price bombs" that drain your budget and the terrifying prospect of having nowhere to sleep.

Veteran pilgrims often offer a singular, urgent piece of advice: "If you don't book this before you leave, you’ll either find everything sold out or face exorbitant prices. Go book it now!" They aren't trying to be alarmist; they are speaking from experience. On this journey, planning isn't just about efficiency—it is a survival strategy. To protect both your sanity and your wallet, here is the essential guide to navigating your way from Paris to the first steps of the Camino.

Navigating the Gateway: Paris to Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port

For those choosing the "French Way" (the most popular route), the first hurdle is getting from Paris to Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, the town nestled at the foot of the Pyrenees where the pilgrimage traditionally begins. Much like airfare, European train and bus prices fluctuate wildly based on demand. If you don’t lock in your tickets early, you could end up paying as much for a single train ride as you did for your flight to Europe.

There are four primary ways to make this trek:

  • TGV High-Speed Train: Departing from Paris Montparnasse, this is the fastest option (about 5 hours). While convenient, it is the most expensive.
  • Overnight Train: Departing from Paris Austerlitz, this takes about 12 hours. It is a favorite among savvy pilgrims because it saves the cost of one night in a hostel, often costing around 100,000 KRW (about $75). Note: These trains do not run daily, so plan your itinerary carefully around the schedule.
  • Bus: Departing from Paris Bercy, this is the cheapest option but takes about 11 hours. Comfort is minimal, and the lack of proper sleeping space makes it a grueling ride.
  • Flying: You can fly into Biarritz or Pau and transfer to Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port. While fast, it is often the most expensive and cumbersome option.

Pro Tips for the Overnight Train

To book the overnight train, use the SNCF Connect app (the official app for the French National Railway). Be careful with your station selection—ensure you are departing from Paris Austerlitz. Prices vary significantly by day of the week, with Fridays being the most expensive and Mondays or Tuesdays being the most budget-friendly.

Crucially, when booking, ensure you select a "Berth" rather than a "Seat." Choosing a standard seat means spending 12 hours upright, which is a recipe for physical exhaustion before you even start walking. In second class, you will find three-tiered bunks. Aim for the top bunk if possible; while the bottom bunks are easier to get into, the clearance is so low you cannot sit up straight. The top bunk offers more headroom and space for your backpack.

Safety Warning: The Hidden Risks of Transit

While overnight travel is romanticized, it is also a hotspot for pickpockets. If you lose your bag on the train from Paris, your pilgrimage is effectively ruined on day one. Always lock your bag to the rack or keep it physically attached to you while you sleep. Keep your valuables, such as your passport and wallet, in a pouch against your skin at all times. A well-planned trip is worthless if you lose your gear to a petty thief.

Roncesvalles: The Non-Negotiable Reservation

The most critical stretch of the pilgrimage for planning is the path from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Roncesvalles and Zubiri. While experienced pilgrims might enjoy the "walk-in" culture of finding beds on the fly, beginners should never attempt this without reservations. Roncesvalles, in particular, is a bottleneck: there is only one albergue (pilgrim hostel) in the entire town.

During peak season, thousands of pilgrims converge on the route. If you arrive without a reservation, you may find yourself with nowhere to sleep in a remote mountain village. Booking your accommodation for the first three days is not an act of cowardice; it is a vital step in maintaining your physical and mental conditioning for the long road ahead.

Conquering the Pyrenees

The trek over the Pyrenees is legendary for its difficulty. Carrying a 25kg (55lb) pack over a mountain range is no small feat. Many pilgrims break this up by staying at the Orisson Refuge. These mountain huts are incredibly popular and often book up 5 to 6 months in advance. Staying here costs about 52 EUR (about $56), which includes dinner and breakfast. While that might seem pricey for a hostel, consider that there are no grocery stores or restaurants in the middle of the Pyrenees. The warm meal and the camaraderie with fellow pilgrims are invaluable.

Is the Effort Worth It?

The Camino de Santiago has evolved beyond its religious roots into a sanctuary for the modern, stressed-out individual. According to 2018 data, about 300,000 pilgrims walked the route, with 60% choosing the French Way. Notably, South Koreans represent the largest group of pilgrims from Asia and the ninth largest globally. This trend reflects a collective desire among our generation to escape the pressures of daily life and find a new direction.

As you prepare, remember that every booking you make—from the overnight train to the hostel in Roncesvalles—is a manifestation of your commitment. In a world where we are accustomed to constant, high-speed competition, the meticulous nature of these preparations is actually the first step of your healing process. It is a way of protecting yourself in a foreign land. Once the bookings are done, the logistics fade, and you are left with only the road and your own two feet.

What to watch next: Keep an eye on the official Camino de Santiago association websites for updates on hostel availability, which can change due to local regulations. If you haven't yet, research how to obtain your "Credential" (pilgrim’s passport), which is necessary to stay in official albergues. Once you have your transport and your first few nights secured, trust your preparation, listen to your body, and enjoy the walk.
