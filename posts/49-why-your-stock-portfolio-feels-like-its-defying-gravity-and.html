Why Your Stock Portfolio Feels Like It’s Defying Gravity (And Why You Should Hold On)

September 24, 2026

If you have been watching the global markets lately, you are likely feeling a strange, nagging dissonance. On one hand, you hear reports of major indices showing resilience; on the other, you look at your own account and wonder why your personal bottom line isn't reflecting that strength. It’s the kind of frustrating gap that makes investors want to laugh—or cry. Whether it's headlines about the KOSPI (Korea’s benchmark stock index) "defending" its levels or the relentless rally in US tech stocks, the reality for the average investor is often far more muted.

You’ve likely seen the urgent cries across social media and financial news: "Don't you dare sell Samsung Electronics or SK Hynix now." But as oil prices swing on a whim of a Donald Trump tweet, and complex analyses about the Federal Reserve's "real" intentions flood your feed, it’s easy to feel like you’re just waiting for the next gut punch. However, beneath the noise of macroeconomic grandstanding, the structural reality of the market is simpler than it appears. Let’s strip away the hype and look at the gears actually turning under the hood.

Geopolitical Theater and the Reality of Oil Prices

The current resilience of the US tech sector is often credited to a cooling in oil prices, but there is a clear political puppet master at work: Donald Trump’s posturing regarding Iran. By deliberately highlighting the risk of conflict—most notably during his UN speeches—Trump created a volatility spike that sent oil prices and bond yields soaring, effectively pouring cold water on the broader market.

For an outside observer, this might look like genuine geopolitical instability. In reality, it is a high-stakes political performance. With mid-term elections approaching, Trump is under pressure to explain why inflation has pinched the average household. By creating a "geopolitical crisis" and then managing the fallout, he shifts the narrative away from his own domestic policy failures. The market knows it’s a show, but as long as the "term premium"—the extra yield investors demand for holding long-term debt—remains in check, investors are willing to play along for the sake of liquidity.

The Artificial Intelligence Capital Expenditure Boom

While geopolitics provides the daily drama, the real story is the tectonic shift in capital expenditure (CAPEX). JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon has signaled that AI-related investments will likely surpass $1 trillion by 2027. This isn't just "big tech" spending for the sake of it; it is a massive, structural flow of capital into the hardware that powers the digital age.

Meta (the parent company of Facebook and Instagram) recently provided a masterclass in this shift. Their move to integrate AI agents directly into their consumer platforms—rather than just selling backend tools to other companies—has changed the game. When a tech giant successfully brings AI to the everyday user, the demand for underlying infrastructure (ASICs, CPUs, NAND flash, and especially High Bandwidth Memory, or HBM) becomes an existential necessity, not just a luxury. For South Korea’s memory giants, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, this translates into a multi-year order book that makes short-term price volatility look like little more than background noise.

Why You Should Keep Your "Samsung-Hynix" Positions

There is a recurring fear that the memory chip cycle is peaking, but the numbers tell a different story. Look at the performance of Micron Technology, the US memory chip maker. Their consistently strong earnings serve as a "litmus test" for the entire industry. If Micron is thriving, the global demand for memory is fundamentally healthy.

Moreover, for investors in Samsung Electronics, there is a secondary safety net: dividends. With quarterly dividend yields approaching 1.5% to 2%, the stock offers a buffer that growth-focused tech stocks in the US often lack. In a world where high interest rates are the new normal, companies that combine massive, AI-driven profit growth with tangible shareholder returns are the only ones you want in your portfolio. You are not holding these shares because of a "story"—you are holding them because they are the "rice" (the essential commodity) of the digital era.

The KOSDAQ Divide: Picking Winners in a High-Rate Environment

While the KOSPI remains bolstered by these heavyweight semiconductor giants, the KOSDAQ (South Korea’s equivalent to the NASDAQ, home to many smaller, growth-oriented companies) is facing a colder reality. The KOSDAQ is hypersensitive to interest rates because these companies rely on borrowing to fund future research. In a high-rate environment, the cost of capital is effectively strangling their growth potential.

However, this doesn't mean the entire market is doomed. It means the "era of easy money" is dead. You cannot simply buy the index and expect a rise. The winning strategy today is "Bottom-Up" investing: focusing on specific sectors like secondary batteries (EV components) and the "So-Bu-Jang" (Materials, Parts, and Equipment) sector. These companies are seeing their earnings targets consistently revised upward, proving that they have the fundamental strength to survive even when the broader market is under pressure.

What to Watch Next

  • US Bond Auctions: Keep a close eye on the results of the 2-year, 5-year, and 7-year Treasury auctions. Any sign of weak demand will trigger immediate market-wide volatility.
  • Dividend Ex-Dates: Watch for the dividend announcement cycles for major Korean blue chips. Institutions often engage in arbitrage around these dates, which can create entry opportunities.
  • AI Integration Pace: Monitor how quickly other tech giants (beyond Meta) launch consumer-facing AI agents. The faster this happens, the higher the demand for Korean-made memory chips becomes.

The Bottom Line: The market is noisy, and political theater will continue to provide short-term scares. But if you own companies that generate real cash, pay real dividends, and provide essential technology that the world cannot function without, your best strategy is to sit tight. The "noise" is temporary; fundamental value is permanent.
