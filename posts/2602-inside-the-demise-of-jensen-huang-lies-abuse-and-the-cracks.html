Inside the Demise of Jensen Huang: Lies, Abuse, and the Cracks in Nvidia’s Empire

November 22, 2026

The boardroom of Nvidia was long regarded as a temple of modern computing, a place where the architecture of the artificial intelligence revolution was drawn up under the iron command of Jensen Huang. Clad in his signature black leather jacket, Huang was not merely a Chief Executive Officer; he was celebrated across global markets as the supreme conductor of the AI era. But behind that orchestrated image lay a reality that finally shattered on November 21, throwing global equity markets and the semiconductor industry into unchartered turmoil.

Huang’s sudden resignation—and the appointment of Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO—was initially framed by casual observers as the fallout of a personal affair. Yet as investigative details spilled out, it became clear that the scandal ran far deeper into corporate governance, systemic deception, and institutional decay. The board did not push Huang out simply over a personal relationship; they ousted him after discovering a year-long campaign of fabricated reports, severe conflicts of interest involving high-end AI microchips, and two decades of horrific verbal abuse inflicted upon his employees.

For international investors and tech observers, the fall of Jensen Huang is more than a celebrity executive downfall. It is a cautionary tale of imperial corporate governance, where record-breaking profits were used to shield extreme behavioral pathology until the enterprise itself began to fracture from within.

Deception, Tyranny, and Nepotism Behind the Leather Jacket

The decisive break between Jensen Huang and Nvidia’s board of directors came down to a total collapse of trust. For over twelve months, as rumors of an extramarital affair swirled, the board conducted internal reviews and requested straightforward explanations from the chief executive. In response, Huang systematically submitted false reports, misleading statements, and engineered cover-ups to hide the scope of the relationship. When the highest decision-making body of a publicly traded firm is deliberately misled by its chief executive for a full year, governance ceases to function.

Worse still, the line between personal relationships and corporate assets was completely erased. Investigations revealed evidence that Nvidia was moving to allocate 40,000 of its prized H200 AI GPUs—the single most sought-after hardware commodity on earth—to a company tied to Huang’s mistress. In an environment where global tech giants beg for chip allocations, funneling cutting-edge hardware for personal gratification struck at the core of fiduciary duty.

Once Huang’s protection vanished, current and former employees broke their silence regarding a workplace culture defined by fear and humiliation. Testimony surfaced showing that Huang routinely hurled shocking insults at staff, using phrases like "your mother should have aborted you" and threatening to "pee on your brain." Far from being isolated outbursts of an eccentric genius, former staff described twenty years of relentless verbal violence, normalized under the guise of Silicon Valley hyper-performance.

In South Korea, where corporate misbehavior is scrutinized through the lens of business ethics, analysts and media quickly drew parallels to the country’s infamous 2014 "Nut Rage" scandal—an incident where a Korean Air heiress delayed a flight over served nuts, coming to symbolize unhinged executive privilege and abusive dynastic control. Insiders exposed a similar culture of nepotism at Nvidia, pointing to the unbridled influence of Huang’s daughter, Madison Huang, within the company, complete with reports of workplace screaming incidents. The illusion that Nvidia operated as a pure meritocracy crumbled, exposing a private family power structure operating inside a public tech titan.

The Fall from Grace: Record earnings and market dominance can obscure executive abuse and moral hazard for a time, but no amount of innovation can indefinitely hide a culture built on fear and deception.

The Washington-Silicon Valley Rift: Trump, Musk, and a Defiant Board

As news of the resignation sent shockwaves through international markets, major political and industry figures rushed to intervene. U.S. President-elect Donald Trump publicly weighed in, arguing that Huang deserved a "second chance" given his pivotal role in American technology. Tesla CEO Elon Musk similarly called for Huang’s reinstatement, arguing that his technical leadership was indispensable if the United States wished to maintain its edge in the global AI race against geopolitical rivals like China.

Huang himself showed no intention of quietly fading away. Leveraging his massive industry network and technical prestige, the former CEO actively signaled his desire to return to the helm, betting that market anxiety would force a turnaround.

Yet Nvidia’s board of directors stood firm against intense external political pressure. In a clear message to Wall Street and Washington, the board explicitly ruled out any possibility of Huang’s return. From the board’s perspective, a year of deliberate lies had severed the executive mandate beyond repair. By rallying around interim CEO Colette Kress, the directors signaled that restoring corporate integrity took priority over yielding to political powerbrokers or the cult of personality.

Accounting Shadows: SEC Probes, Circular Financing, and Market Chaos

The corporate crisis immediately ignited panic across financial markets. Nvidia’s stock plummeted 14% in a single trading session, capping a brutal weekly loss of 23%. The sudden plunge triggered $1.76 billion in forced liquidations across related tech stocks, hardware suppliers like SanDisk, and crypto assets like Bitcoin.

The market panic hit South Korea particularly hard. Korean retail investors—known locally as "Seohak Ants" for their aggressive, army-like purchasing of foreign equities—had poured billions into Nvidia as the cornerstone of their foreign portfolios. As billions in market capitalization evaporated overnight, retail and institutional holders were left reeling.

Feeding the sell-off were ominous accounting questions. Analysts began zeroing in on the mystery of Nvidia’s "missing Singapore revenue," where massive sales routed through the region raised red flags regarding ultimate destination and economic reality.
