The Fed’s Latest Rate Hike: Why Korean Markets Are Holding Their Ground

September 22, 2026

For many investors watching the US Federal Reserve’s latest decision to hike interest rates by 25 basis points, the overriding sentiment wasn’t panic—it was relief. After months of speculation, the "rip the Band-Aid off" mentality has taken hold. If rates were going to rise, the market preferred it happen now rather than dragging out the uncertainty until the next Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting. Watching the overnight charts, it felt as though the market had already steeled itself for the pain, choosing to confront the reality rather than fear the unknown.

Surprisingly, the aftermath of the hike was met with confusion: "The Fed raised rates—why isn't the market crashing?" While the Dow Jones Industrial Average saw a slight dip, the tech-heavy Nasdaq held its own, and Nasdaq futures actually rebounded in post-market trading. This resilience suggests that the market had already "priced in" the news weeks ago, and more importantly, it proves that the South Korean stock market possesses a structural stamina that shouldn’t be underestimated. Even while buried under a mountain of headwinds, the major indices are carving out a solid floor.

The Fed, Oil Prices, and the Hidden Variables

The real story of this FOMC meeting isn't the 25-basis-point hike itself, but the market's defensive strength. Even with the Dow correcting, growth sectors in South Korea—specifically semiconductor materials, parts, and equipment (often referred to as “Sobujang”), aerospace, and robotics—remained unfazed.

While some of these companies may not be churning out massive cash dividends this quarter, they represent the future. Despite rumors of a "slowdown" in AI development, industry leaders like Sam Altman continue to signal aggressive rollouts, confirming that the AI revolution has crossed a point of no return. Investors have grown more discerning, moving past the knee-jerk fear reactions of the past to identify companies with actual, sustainable competitive advantages.

However, we must remain grounded. Historically, rapid rate hikes by the Fed act as a ticking time bomb for the real economy. We have already seen the fallout in the U.S. with bankruptcies among companies with weak balance sheets and retailers like Bed Bath & Beyond succumbing to credit crunches. Even if the Korean markets seem sturdy today, the long-term pressure of global tightening will eventually filter down to the real economy.

The hidden hero of today’s market stability might actually be oil. Almost immediately following the rate hike, news broke that Saudi Arabia reported no issues with oil supply, causing crude prices to tumble by over 3%. Given that oil prices are the primary driver of inflation and consumer price indices (CPI) globally, this stability is a breath of fresh air. In the U.S., where reliance on personal vehicles is absolute, high oil prices function as a de facto "inflation tax" that crushes consumption. If oil prices can stabilize—or at least stop their aggressive climb—it gives the Fed the breathing room it needs to pause its tightening cycle.

Trading in a "Stagnant" Market: Where the Money is Going

After the euphoric rallies seen in May and June, the current sideways movement of the KOSPI (the benchmark index for the Korea Exchange) feels agonizingly slow. We are in a period of "energy accumulation," where the market is consolidating before its next move. With total trading volume—the amount of money changing hands—down to around 20 trillion won (about $15 billion) across the KOSPI and KOSDAQ, the market has become a smaller, more competitive pond.

In this environment, chasing fleeting trends is a recipe for disaster. The smartest way to read the market’s true intention is to look at the portfolio allocations of "active" ETFs (Exchange Traded Funds) managed by top Korean asset management firms. A look at the top holdings of these funds reveals a clear consensus: semiconductor Sobujang companies.

These firms aren't just betting on the "big names" like Samsung Electronics; they are focusing on the essential players who supply the parts and equipment that make the semiconductor industry run. Even with concerns about next year's growth base effects, these mid-sized players are showing stronger year-over-year profit growth than many of the massive IDMs (Integrated Device Manufacturers).

Three Key Takeaways for Navigating the Current Market:

  • Focus on Relative Strength: When the market takes a hit, look for the stocks that fall the least and recover the fastest. Companies like PSK Holdings, which are deeply tied to High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) infrastructure, are prime examples of this "relative strength" in action.
  • The AI Bottleneck is Infrastructure: AI isn't just about software; it’s about power. A massive, often overlooked, bottleneck in the AI era is electricity supply. Companies involved in power plant construction, high-voltage transmission, and energy storage systems (ESS) are the silent winners of this cycle.
  • Volume is the Truth: With overall trading volume significantly lower than in years past, liquidity is scarce. This is not the time to gamble on obscure themes. Stick to the "main" sectors where institutional money is parked, even if it feels boring.

What to Watch Next

The domestic market currently sits at a strange crossroads: it has the physical strength to withstand bad news, but it lacks the liquidity to break out into a new rally. The "main event" moving forward will be the battle between oil price stabilization and the ability of AI infrastructure companies to turn their massive capital investment into actual, bottom-line earnings.

For investors, the most important rule right now is to ignore the temptation of panic-buying or chasing volatile "hot" stocks. In a market where volume is thin, the fastest way to shrink your account is to be impatient. Keep your eyes on the "majors"—the companies building the physical infrastructure for the AI future—and be prepared to play the long game. In an environment covered in fog, the ones who look the furthest ahead are the ones who will ultimately come out on top.
