How South Korea's Most Famous Intellectual Defused a $70M Wealth Rumor and Bought His First Stock

October 29, 2026

For years, a persistent whisper circulated through the darker corners of Korean political YouTube and online forums: You Si-min, one of the country's most prominent progressive intellectuals, was sitting on a secret fortune of roughly 100 billion won (about $70 million). To critics of the left, the alleged sum served as a neat narrative device—proof that a man who spent decades lecturing the public about social equality and the struggles of ordinary people was, in reality, a hidden multi-billionaire.

In South Korea's polarized political ecosystem, where public figures are routinely targeted by character assassinations disguised as fringe gossip, such rumors carry heavy weight. But in a recent appearance on Park Si-young TV, a popular progressive political commentary channel run by commentator Park Si-young, You tackled the allegations head-on. Not only did he deny the nine-figure sum, but he also revealed that until he retired from formal politics at age 55, he had virtually no savings at all. The fortune—which he clarifies amounts to less than 5 billion won (about $3.5 million)—was built the hard way: by sitting down every single year after leaving politics and writing a bestselling book.

The broadcast marked You's return to the airwaves about five weeks after an appearance with investigative journalist Chang In-soo. Wearing a leather jacket that prompted lighthearted studio jokes about being on a "war footing," You used the platform to not only clear his name but also to reflect on the heavy toll of political commentary, his shifting approach to analyzing the state of the nation under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, and—surprisingly—his first-ever foray into the stock market.

The Burden of Commentary and the Shift to an 'Alien' Perspective

For decades, You Si-min has been an inescapable figure in South Korean public discourse. To his supporters, he is a sagacious guide whose historical and political insights cut straight to the core of complex societal issues. To his detractors, he is an acerbic partisan whose sharp tongue brooks no dissent. Yet, during his recent media appearances, You expressed a deep discomfort with the lofty titles bestowed upon him by fans and media alike, waving off terms like "the sage of our era" with a tired laugh.

That discomfort stems from a deeper self-reflection. You admitted that he has spent much of his career engaging in what he calls "normative evaluations"—constantly telling the public what is good or bad, beautiful or vulgar, and predicting success or ruin based on his own ethical framework. Looking back, he realizes that this approach often alienated those who did not share his baseline assumptions. As an ardent supporter of the current administration under President Lee Jae-myung—the progressive leader who took office following the historic upheaval that saw former leader Yoon Seok-youl impeached and imprisoned—You worried that his passionate commentary might inadvertently box the government in or distort public debate.

His solution? To adopt what he terms the "alien's perspective." Moving forward, You aims to strip away raw emotion and observe South Korean politics with the detached, neutral curiosity of an extraterrestrial visitor watching earthlings. While partisan firebrands on both sides thrive on emotional outrage, You's pivot toward cold, observational analysis reflects an intellectual trying to evolve past the toxic binary of modern political fandom.

Busting the Multi-Million Dollar Myth: A Life Without Savings Until 55

The centerpiece of You's recent media tour, however, was his blunt correction of the financial rumors that have dogged him for years. Addressing the claims that he held anywhere from 10 billion to 100 billion won, You chuckled at the absurdity. "It's not 100 billion won, it's not 50 billion won, and it's not even 10 billion won," he stated plainly.

The reality of his financial history is far more mundane than the conspiracy theories suggest. When You officially retired from electoral politics in 2013 at the age of 55, he had accumulated virtually no savings. He acknowledged that throughout his active political career—serving as a reform-minded lawmaker and later as a cabinet minister—building personal wealth was simply not a priority, leaving him with an empty bank account at an age when most Koreans are deep into retirement planning.

It was only after leaving politics behind that financial survival and the desire to fund his children's education forced him to treat writing as a commercial enterprise. "I figured I needed to make some money and start saving, so I decided to write one book every year," he recalled. Those annual releases—most notably his deeply researched and widely read "European City Tour" travelogue series, including recent volumes on Madrid and Barcelona—turned him into one of the country's most reliable commercial authors. The royalties from those books form the entirety of his legitimate, transparently earned asset base, which sits comfortably below the 5-billion-won mark.

Trusting the Presidential Vision: From Zero Savings to ETF Investing

Perhaps the most unexpected revelation from You's recent appearances was his late-in-life conversion into a retail investor. Having famously never owned a single stock for most of his life, You decided to crack open his savings account and dive into the Korean stock market last year.

The catalyst? President Lee Jae-myung's ambitious campaign pledge to push the benchmark Korea Composite Stock Price Index (KOSPI) to the 5,000-point mark. At a time when the market was hovering below 3,000 points and economic uncertainty loomed heavy, You decided to put his money where his mouth was. Trusting the economic roadmap laid out by the Lee administration, he began purchasing exchange-traded funds (ETFs) along with shares in renewable energy and semiconductor firms.

For a veteran political figure to tie a portion of his personal wealth to the economic targets of a sitting president is a rare move. It signals a personal financial stake in the future of the nation's key industries—semiconductors and green energy—that aligns directly with the government's industrial policy. Whether the KOSPI eventually reaches 5,000 remains to be seen, but You's participation gives him a grounded, firsthand perspective on the anxieties and aspirations of ordinary retail investors.

What to Watch Next

  • The 'Alien' Experiment in Action: Watch whether You's new, emotion-free analytical style actually dampens the partisan vitriol he typically receives, or if his critics will simply find new ways to twist his neutral observations.
  • The KOSPI 5000 Trajectory: Monitor the performance of the semiconductor and renewable energy sectors in Korea, which serve as the primary litmus test for President Lee Jae-myung's economic growth promises—and the health of You's maiden stock portfolio.
  • Upcoming Literary Projects: Given his self-imposed rule of publishing one book a year to secure his livelihood, look for news on whether his next travelogue or political essay collection will hit shelves as scheduled.
