A House Divided: Inside the High-Stakes Factional War Rocking South Korea’s Ruling Party

November 16, 2026

Within a span of twelve hours on November 15, over 110 news articles inundated South Korean media, capturing a ruling party spiraling into open factional warfare. What began as a leaked recording of a closed-door meeting has mushroomed into a full-blown existential crisis for the Democratic Party of Korea (DP). By nightfall, the party leadership had referred one of its highest-ranking figures to the ethics tribunal, while a powerful block of freshman lawmakers huddled in an emergency late-night Zoom conference that dragged past 11:00 PM.

At the epicenter of the storm is Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee, whose stray remarks regarding President Lee Jae-myung triggered a ferocious backlash from presidential loyalists. But rather than retreating, Choi launched a dramatic counter-offensive, slinging allegations of religious collusion against her internal opponents. As Party Leader Kim Min-seok approaches his 100th day at the helm, the ruling party finds itself paralyzed by internal strife, raising urgent questions about whether its leadership can maintain control of the National Assembly.

The Spark: A Leaked Tape and a President’s Pain

To understand why a single audio recording caused such widespread panic, one must understand the political sanctity of President Lee Jae-myung’s record within his own party. During the turbulent political saga that led to the impeachment and removal of former president Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—who currently serves a prison sentence—President Lee stood as the central figure rallying the opposition base. Demonstrating unwavering commitment to that constitutional reckoning is a prerequisite for legitimacy inside today's ruling Democratic Party.

The crisis erupted when a private recording of Choi Min-hee was leaked to the press. In the audio, Choi claimed that during the height of the impeachment movement against Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, President Lee Jae-myung had actually opposed impeaching him. The revelation sent shockwaves through the party, framing one of its most prominent leaders as publicly casting doubt on the president's revolutionary credentials.

On the morning of November 15, at 11:16 AM, Choi issued a formal public apology. She bowed her head, stating she was deeply sorry for causing distress to the president due to a "lapse in memory." However, her apology failed to douse the flames. Instead, the situation reached a boiling point when President Lee Jae-myung personally voiced his distress over the affair, remarking bitterly that "the hardest thing to endure is an arrow flying from behind."

President Lee's public expression of betrayal galvanized the party's pro-Lee faction (known as the Chin-Myung faction). A prominent pro-Lee grassroots organization, the Democratic Gyeonggi Innovation Conference, released an official statement demanding Choi’s immediate expulsion from the party. Party loyalists argued that falsely accusing the sitting president of opposing the historic removal of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol was an unforgivable act of political sabotage that could not be pardoned by a simple apology.

The Counterstrike: Weaponizing Cult Allegations

Faced with career-ending pressure from the party’s dominant faction, Choi did not back down. Around noon on November 15, just forty-four minutes after apologizing for her misstatement, she launched a high-stakes counter-attack through multiple media outlets.

Choi insisted that while her memory error was regrettable, it was entirely separate from what she alleged to be a corrupt internal conspiracy. She called on the party leadership to open a formal investigation into a suspected secret "Three-Party Alliance" involving anti-Lee factions, political splitters, and Shincheonji—a controversial fringe religious group that has long been a lightning rod in South Korean political scandals. By framing herself as the victim of an illegal wiretap, Choi sought to shift the spotlight onto her accusers.

The counter-offensive immediately split the party along regional and factional fault lines:

  • Local Support: Five municipal and provincial council members from Choi’s constituency in Namyangju, Gyeonggi Province, issued a joint statement defending her, demanding an immediate end to "unfair political attacks" and urging party unity.
  • Leadership Backlash: Lawmaker Kang Deok-koo, a figure aligned with the party leadership, harshly dismissed Choi’s claims. Kang publicly scoffed at the idea that Party Leader Kim Min-seok would rely on a religious sect for political leverage, calling her accusations absurd fabrications.
  • Factional Retaliation: Supporters of senior lawmaker Jung Cheong-rae (the Chin-Chung faction) and other non-mainstream party groups rallied against the pro-Lee hardliners, launching a campaign for party reform and even submitting a formal disciplinary petition against Party Leader Kim Min-seok for taking action against Choi.

Leadership Under Fire: Three Days to Ethics Referral

With the ruling party consuming itself in public warfare, Party Leader Kim Min-seok faced the sternest test of his tenure. Approaching his 100th day in office—a milestone typically reserved for touting legislative achievements and economic policy—Kim was forced to act decisively to prevent a total breakdown of party discipline.

At 6:00 PM on November 15, just three days after ordering an emergency internal investigation, Kim received the Ethics Inspectorate’s report and immediately referred Choi to the Central Party Ethics Tribunal. The rapid turnaround signaled that the leadership was treating the matter not as a minor rhetorical slip, but as a severe breach of party order. The decision effectively put Choi’s political fate in the hands of the tribunal, where potential penalties range from official reprimands to total expulsion.

Simultaneously, the party’s influential caucus of first-term lawmakers—known collectively as Deomincho—convened an emergency virtual meeting on Sunday evening. As the backbone of the party's legislative force, the reactions of these newly elected lawmakers were seen as a bellwether for wider parliamentary sentiment.

During the intense, hours-long discussion that lasted past 11:00 PM, hardline members argued for forceful measures, suggesting the caucus demand Choi’s voluntary resignation or urge the tribunal to hand down the maximum possible penalty. While the caucus reached a consensus that the situation was "extremely grave," the debate was so contested that they refrained from issuing an immediate midnight statement, electing instead to draft a refined position the following morning.

What Lies Ahead: A Ruling Party at a Flashpoint

The turmoil within the Democratic Party comes at a precarious moment. While the ruling party maintains control of the executive under President Lee Jae-myung and holds a formidable presence in the National Assembly, deep-seated factional mistrust threatens to derail its legislative agenda. External forces are already capitalizing on the chaos: civic groups have filed criminal complaints against Choi with the police, while the main opposition People Power Party (PPP) has seized on her statements to demand a Special Counsel investigation into the alleged Shincheonji ties.

In the coming days, political observers should monitor several critical developments:

  • The Ethics Tribunal's Verdict: The severity of the tribunal's ruling on Choi Min-hee will determine the party's immediate trajectory. A light penalty risks enraging the pro-Lee base, while an expulsion could deepen the rift with non-mainstream factions.
  • Kim Min-seok’s 100-Day Milestone: How Party Leader Kim navigates his upcoming landmark address will reveal whether he can reassert authority over competing internal factions or remain bogged down by party infighting.
  • The Opposition's Special Counsel Push: Watch how the opposition People Power Party leverages Choi's cult-related accusations in the National Assembly to disrupt the ruling party's legislative momentum.
  • Disciplinary Petitions Against Leadership: Counter-petitions filed against Party Leader Kim by rival faction members could create unprecedented procedural headaches within the party's executive bodies.

What started as a covertly recorded conversation has exposed the fragile fault lines within South Korea's ruling coalition. As the Central Party Ethics Tribunal prepares its ruling, the Democratic Party faces a decisive choice between enforcing strict discipline or plunging deeper into an all-out factional war.
