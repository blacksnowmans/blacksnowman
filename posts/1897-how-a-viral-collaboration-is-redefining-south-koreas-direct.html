How a Viral Collaboration is Redefining South Korea's Direct-to-Consumer Knitwear Market

November 03, 2026

Every autumn, wardrobes across South Korea undergo a familiar ritual of panic: the sudden realization that despite racks full of clothes, there is nothing quite right to wear for the chilly transition between seasons. It is an annual wardrobe crisis that has spawned an entire sub-industry of fast fashion aimed at quick fixes. But a new entrant in the Korean direct-to-consumer apparel space is taking a radically different approach. The newly launched knit jacket developed in collaboration with Design Owol, a boutique apparel brand led by CEO Lee Jin-ah, has captured massive online attention not merely for its celebrity-adjacent marketing, but because of an obsessive focus on structural integrity and practical utility.

For international observers watching South Korea's vibrant digital commerce ecosystem, this project offers a fascinating case study in how domestic creators and independent manufacturers can leverage media influence to build high-end apparel at a fraction of traditional luxury costs. By bridging the gap between the relaxed comfort of knitwear and the sharp silhouette of a structured outerwear jacket, the project aims to solve a very specific seasonal dilemma for Korean consumers: how to stay warm and look polished from early autumn straight through to early spring.

Engineering Structure Into Soft Materials

The primary challenge in designing a proper knit jacket lies in the fundamental physics of the yarn. Pure knits have a tendency to sag, stretch out at the elbows, and lose their form over the course of a busy day. According to Lee Jin-ah, transforming the soft advantages of knit into a garment that maintains a crisp, blazer-like composure required extensive trial and error in the studio.

Early drafts leaned toward minimalist solid colors, but the final production favored subtle contrast detailing. Changes in weave patterns around the elbows and sleeves, alongside contrasting inner panels, ensure that the jacket retains visual depth even when hung up on a coat rack. More importantly, the production process involves meticulous hand-finishing—specifically interlocking and sewing the internal facings by hand—to ensure durability. While luxury fashion houses charge a heavy premium for this level of craftsmanship, this project has managed to price the garment at roughly one-fifth of high-end equivalents, targeting what Korean consumers call *gasimbi*—value for the heart and emotional satisfaction.

Practicality was baked into the blueprint from the start. Eschewing purely decorative tailoring, the design incorporates a clever dual-pocket layout. Split vertically and horizontally, these pockets allow wearers to stow smartphones, wallets, and car keys without ruining the clean exterior lines of the jacket.

Styling the Cut: Short Versatility Versus Long Elegance

To cater to different body types and lifestyle needs, the lineup is split into distinct short and long versions, each offering a fundamentally different silhouette.

The women's short version is cut to hit right at the hip bone, maximizing mobility. For urban commuters navigating crowded subways or driving frequently, the cropped length prevents the bunching and restriction common with heavier coats. Paired with a crisp button-down shirt underneath, it projects a smart-casual office vibe; thrown over a simple t-shirt, it achieves an effortless, relaxed look.

Conversely, the long version provides generous coverage past the hips, serving as an effective tool for body-proportion styling. Its defining feature is an internal waist drawstring. Pulling the string tight cinches the waist for a distinctly feminine, tailored silhouette, while leaving it loose creates a straight, minimalist drape. Available in a diverse color palette ranging from khaki and ivory to black, mustard, and navy, the long version bridges the gap between casual comfort and professional office attire.

Sizing for All Bodies and the Art of the Fit

One of the most notable aspects of the release is its aggressive approach to sizing. Designed from the ground up as unisex, the men's range stretches from a diminutive 85 (XXS) up to a roomy 110 (XL), while the women's line covers sizes 85 to 100. The largest size is cut generously enough to comfortably fit individuals who might normally require a 120, making it an attractive option for fans of the oversized streetwear aesthetic.

When women opt for the men's cut, the standard styling advice is to drop down one size for a cleaner fit, though those favoring a looser silhouette can stick to their usual measurements. The underlying philosophy emphasizes that clothes should flatter the body's natural lines rather than swallow them. Choosing a size where the shoulder seam rests precisely on the body allows the garment's hand-finished structure to shine, preventing the sloppy look that often plagues oversized knitwear.

What to Watch Next

  • Market Reception and Resock Cycles: Watch whether this collaborative model expands into other staple wardrobe items, as initial production runs for independent Korean designer collaborations frequently sell out within hours.
  • Durability Reviews: Keep an eye on consumer feedback regarding how the merino wool blend and hand-finished seams hold up against pilling and stretching after months of heavy winter layering.
  • Direct-to-Consumer Pricing Trends: Observe if traditional retail brands begin adjusting their margins as independent media figures continue to prove that consumers will snap up luxury-grade finishing at a fraction of the cost.

As South Korea's retail landscape increasingly shifts toward personality-driven, highly digitized commerce, projects like this demonstrate that modern consumers are no longer willing to choose between comfort and structural elegance. For those navigating the shifting temperatures of the coming months, the real test will be whether these independent labels can scale their manufacturing without sacrificing the meticulous hand-detailing that made them viral in the first place.
