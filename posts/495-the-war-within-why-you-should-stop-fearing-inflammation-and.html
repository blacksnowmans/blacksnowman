The War Within: Why You Should Stop Fearing Inflammation and Start Managing It

September 30, 2026

We often treat inflammation like a common cold—a nuisance to be suppressed as quickly as possible. When we feel a sore throat or see a high inflammation marker on a health checkup report, our instinct is to reach for anti-inflammatory medication or start panic-buying supplements. However, according to Professor Lee Seung-hoon of Seoul National University Hospital (a leading institution in South Korea), this mindset is fundamentally flawed. Inflammation is not an enemy attacking your body; it is a vital, self-sacrificing defense system fighting to keep you alive.

Treating inflammation as an object to be eradicated is akin to firing the firefighters who are currently trying to save your house from burning down. The real danger isn't the inflammation itself, but the "chronic overreaction" of a body that has become confused. Before you fill your cabinet with health supplements, it is time to understand the true nature of the 24-hour war being waged inside you.

Misconceptions About Inflammation: Why Your Body Isn't the Enemy

In medical terms, inflammation is part of the "innate immune system," the first line of defense against pathogens. When you get a cold and your throat swells, it isn't the virus causing the swelling—it is your own macrophages and neutrophils, the "protagonists" of your immune system, turning your throat into a battlefield to eliminate the invaders. Just as firefighters drench a house in water to stop a fire from spreading to the neighbor, your body inflicts local damage to neutralize a larger threat.

The problem arises from "collateral damage." Immune cells can be like blinkered racehorses—they focus solely on the objective at the cost of everything else. If the area of infection is large, the "battlefield" expands, leading to the destruction of healthy tissue. This is the physiological cost of survival. Even in modern cancer treatments like immune checkpoint inhibitors, we see that reactivating immune cells to attack tumors can inadvertently trigger an overactive immune response against healthy organs. This proves that modulating inflammation requires precision, and that our immune system possesses a destructive power that must be managed, not just ignored.

The distinction between acute and chronic inflammation is critical. Acute inflammation ends once the threat is neutralized—the firefighters pack up and leave. But chronic inflammation is a state of perpetual, misdirected conflict. When the immune system attacks for years without a clear cause, the organ in question is eventually destroyed from within. This is the "dreaded chain reaction" that leads to cancer, atherosclerosis, and stroke.

The Silent Warning Signs: Why Your Blood Work Matters

Most of us focus on cholesterol or liver enzyme levels during our annual physicals. However, there is a much more sophisticated signal that often goes ignored: the hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein) test. Popularized by researchers like Dr. Paul Ridker, this test is the canary in the coal mine for chronic inflammation.

A reading above 0.2 mg/dL is widely considered a signal that a low-level war is burning somewhere in the body. The American Heart Association has officially recognized chronic inflammation as a formal disease risk factor. Even if you have no symptoms, a reading of 0.4 mg/dL suggests that a "silent war" is happening in your blood vessels or organs. The recent recognition of colchicine, a traditional gout medication, as a new treatment for cardiovascular disease is a testament to the fact that controlling inflammation is the key to preventing heart attacks and strokes.

Similarly, we must monitor our liver enzymes (AST and ALT). While a temporary spike after a night of drinking is a result of acute stress, a consistent reading of 50–60 is a terrifying signal that fatty liver disease has progressed into a chronic inflammatory state. The liver is famously called the "silent organ," but blood tests do not lie. When these numbers rise, it is an urgent distress signal that your metabolic system has hit its limit.

The Metabolic Trap: Why "Healthy" Habits Can Backfire

Many Koreans follow a "low-carb" trend, eating mostly banchan (side dishes) while cutting out rice. However, this often leads to a hidden trap. Korean side dishes are often seasoned with significant amounts of sugar, salt, and condiments. These flavors stimulate the appetite, leading to overeating, which is a direct path to obesity and chronic inflammation.

We must also dispel the myth that carbohydrates are inherently evil. Carbohydrates are the body's preferred fuel source. The problem is "refined carbohydrates"—white rice and bread—which are processed to be absorbed instantly, causing blood glucose levels to skyrocket. When the body has an excess of fuel, it stores it as visceral fat. The secret is not to eliminate carbs, but to change how we consume them. Strategies like "reverse eating" (consuming fiber first to slow glucose absorption) are highly effective ways to prevent blood sugar spikes.

Protecting Your Future: The Glymphatic System and Muscle Maintenance

We often think of dementia as a sudden onset, but it is a slow, creeping process. Fortunately, our brains have a remarkable cleaning system called the glymphatic system. Think of it as the brain’s sewer system. It only works when we are in deep sleep: brain cells shrink, expanding the spaces between them, allowing cerebrospinal fluid to wash away the toxins and metabolic byproducts (like ATP waste) that accumulated during the day. Sleep deprivation is not just about being tired; it is the act of leaving your "brain trash" uncollected.

Finally, we must address sarcopenia, or muscle loss. Muscle isn't just for aesthetics; it is a metabolic asset. Once lost in old age, it is incredibly difficult to regain. Protein intake is the primary defense mechanism against the breakdown of muscle. For those over 60, protein requirements are significantly higher than for the young, yet many seniors rely on noodle-based diets due to dental or digestive issues. This is why we must embrace high-quality protein supplementation as a key to "healthspan."

What to Watch Next

  • Request an hs-CRP test: During your next physical, ask your doctor specifically to include an hs-CRP test to get a baseline for your chronic inflammation level.
  • Monitor your liver: Don't dismiss mildly elevated AST/ALT levels. If they persist, investigate your metabolic health rather than assuming it's just "a bad week."
  • Prioritize protein: If you are over 50, re-evaluate your protein intake. Are you getting enough to prevent muscle wastage, or are you filling your stomach with refined carbs and seasonings?
  • Sleep hygiene: Treat your sleep as a non-negotiable health intervention. You aren't just "resting"—you are letting your brain perform its nightly deep-clean.

Inflammation is not a villain to be defeated, but a desperate, life-saving struggle. Managing that struggle so it doesn't lead to disastrous outcomes is the ultimate key to a healthy life. Before you reach for the next "miracle" supplement, start by cleaning up your metabolic house.
