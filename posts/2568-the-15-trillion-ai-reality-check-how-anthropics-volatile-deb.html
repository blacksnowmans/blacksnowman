The $1.5 Trillion AI Reality Check: How Anthropic’s Volatile Debut Shook Global Markets from Wall Street to Seoul

November 21, 2026

For a brief moment on November 20, the global financial architecture seemed to revolve entirely around a single ticker. Anthropic, the high-flying artificial intelligence developer behind the Claude model family, achieved what many thought impossible: a public debut on Nasdaq at a eye-watering $1.5 trillion valuation. As orders flooded in at seven times the available allocation, the stock surged 17% during regular trading and breached 20% in off-hours dealing. Financial commentators quickly proclaimed the dawn of a $2 trillion AI era, celebrating the first time a frontier model creator had its value tested directly by public equity markets rather than private venture syndicates.

Then came sunrise on day two. Before regular trading even opened, pre-market sell-offs pulled the stock down 9%, setting up a brutal 10% regular-session plunge. In a matter of hours, the historic IPO turned from a celebration into a trigger for a global tech selloff. The contagion spread rapidly across borders and asset classes, knocking Japan's Nikkei 225 down to its lowest level since April and sparking a 5% drop in South Korea’s benchmark Kospi index, breaking below the critical 5,000-point threshold. For investors who believed the AI boom was an unstoppable engine of endless market gains, Anthropic’s two-day roller coaster served as a harsh awakening: the market has officially pivoted from buying AI potential to demanding immediate, cold-hard monetization.

The Anatomy of a $1.5 Trillion Debut and Its Immediate Reversal

To understand the sudden reversal, one must look at how uniquely stretched expectations were heading into November 20. Before Anthropic's debut, frontier AI labs operated inside a protective cocoon of private funding rounds, where valuations were set by venture capitalists and corporate strategists eager to secure access to next-generation foundation models. Going public changed the game entirely. By opening its books and equity to public capital markets, Anthropic provided the first real price-discovery mechanism for the entire generative AI sector.

Initial demand was overwhelming. Retail and institutional investors who had missed out on private funding rounds rushed to buy, propelling the stock 17% higher on day one. But that price action created an unsustainable valuation hurdle. By the second morning, the mood transformed. The shift in market narrative was swift and decisive, moving away from tech optimism toward a rigorous audit of capital efficiency.

Key Market Data Points:

  • IPO Oversubscription: 7x demand relative to available shares.
  • Day 1 Performance: +17% regular session close, topping +20% in after-hours trading.
  • Day 2 Performance: Opened down 6% in early pre-market, dropping to -9% pre-market, and closing down 10% in main trading.
  • Initial Market Cap: $1.5 trillion peak valuation achieved on debut.

The swift day-two selloff revealed that public markets are far less forgiving than venture capitalists. Investors began confronting difficult questions about the immense capital expenditures required to train and run foundation models relative to current commercial revenues. The realization that even a industry leader like Anthropic could see its listing premium evaporate overnight triggered a wave of profit-taking across the entire AI ecosystem.

Staggering Internal Wealth, Cash-Outs, and the Ethics of Revocable Pledges

Even as secondary market investors wrestled with the day-two drawdown, the listing created unprecedented private wealth for Anthropic’s internal team. Silicon Valley has long used equity compensation to attract top talent, but the scale of wealth generated by this IPO broke historical benchmarks. Thousands of researchers, engineers, and early employees became instant millionaires, with average individual equity values estimated between 58 billion and 68 billion Korean won (approximately $43 million to $51 million).

This massive wealth creation contributed to a broader global trend, where the rapid expansion of the AI sector has minted 45 new billionaires in just one year. However, this windfall also highlighted a sharp asymmetry: while internal staff locked in life-changing wealth, retail investors who chased the stock on day one were left holding substantial paper losses by day two.

More controversial were the actions of the leadership team. Anthropic’s founder reached a personal net worth of roughly 30 trillion won (around $22 billion), while seven co-founders each cashed out $280 million around the listing. Public scrutiny intensified when reports confirmed that an initial commitment to donate 80% of their equity to public-interest causes was structured in a way that remains revocable.

The Core Governance Dilemma: For an enterprise that built its brand on AI safety, public trust, and ethical governance, the sight of founders liquidating hundreds of millions of dollars while retaining the option to walk back their charitable commitments raised uncomfortable questions about corporate governance and alignment with public shareholders.

How Wall Street’s AI Rebound Broke South Korea’s Kospi and Hit SK Telecom

The fallout from Anthropic’s listing was felt acutely in South Korea, demonstrating how global capital shifts can destabilize international markets. Despite the hype surrounding the listing, South Korea's main stock market, the Kospi, experienced a dramatic 5% plunge, breaching the 5,000-point psychological benchmark and reaching its lowest level in 10 months. Rather than acting as a catalyst for local tech equities, the mega-IPO acted as a liquidity magnet, drawing foreign capital out of Seoul and reallocating it toward US dollar assets.

The most dramatic single-stock story played out in SK Telecom, South Korea’s largest mobile network operator. In the months leading up to the IPO, SK Telecom had been highlighted as a primary beneficiary of the deal, thanks to its strategic early equity investment in Anthropic, which was projected to yield a massive 5 trillion won (about $3.7 billion) windfall. Yet when the listing actually took place, SK Telecom’s stock fell by 12% in a single session.

Why South Korean Assets Suffered:

  • Sell-the-News Dynamics: Expectations of SK Telecom’s 5 trillion won valuation gain were already priced in; once the IPO occurred, investors rushed to lock in profits.
  • Foreign Portfolio Rebalancing: Foreign institutional investors dumped Korean large-cap stocks to raise cash and offset risk across their global portfolios.
  • Disconnect from Cash Flow: Investors calculated that unrealized paper gains on overseas tech holdings do not translate directly into immediate dividend increases or core telecom earnings.
  • Disappointing ETF Exposure: Six domestic exchange-traded funds (ETFs) in Korea incorporated Anthropic on day one, but their structural weighting was too low for retail investors to capture meaningful upside. Conversely, specialized vehicles like Woori Asset Management’s US short-term bond IPO fund successfully secured direct share allocations.

Global Semiconductor Contagion vs. a $3 Trillion Long-Term Bet

The tremor originating from Nasdaq quickly turned into a broader tech selloff across global markets. As funds rebalanced out of semiconductor and hardware providers to digest the Anthropic debut—and amid renewed concerns over US chip export controls to China—major technology stocks plummeted worldwide. In Japan, tech conglomerate SoftBank dropped 16%, pulling down the Nikkei index. In the US, memory supplier Micron fell 5%, processor developer AMD dropped 7%, and market bellwether Nvidia faced persistent selling pressure.

Yet, amidst this wave of short-term liquidations, powerful institutional money took the opposing side of the trade. Prominent investment firm Altimeter doubled down on the sector, executing a massive $5 billion position in Anthropic (trading under ticker ANT) while publicly forecasting that the company’s market capitalization would surge to $3 trillion within three years.

This sharp division illustrates the tug-of-war currently defining the technology sector: short-term traders reacting to immediate profit-taking and valuation risks, versus long-term mega-funds betting that generative AI platforms will dominate the future digital economy.

What to Watch Next

As the initial dust settles on the largest IPO in financial history, market participants should closely monitor several critical indicators over the coming quarters:

  • Anthropic's First Public Earnings Report: The market will scrutinize quarterly revenue growth and enterprise adoption rates for Claude to judge whether a valuation above $1 trillion is sustainable.
  • Lockup Expirations and Insider Supply: With thousands of newly minted employee millionaires holding massive equity stakes, any upcoming lockup expirations could introduce significant secondary selling pressure.
  • South Korean Capital Flows: Watch whether foreign investors return to the Kospi after the historic index breach below 5,000, or if capital flight toward US tech equities continues to suppress Korean valuations.
  • Corporate Governance Actions: Pay close attention to whether Anthropic's founding team formalizes their 80% donation commitment or further amends governance structures to reassure institutional shareholders.
