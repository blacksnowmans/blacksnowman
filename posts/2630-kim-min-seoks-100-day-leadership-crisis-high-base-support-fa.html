Kim Min-seok’s 100-Day Leadership Crisis: High Base Support, Falling Polls, and Factional Warfare in South Korea’s Ruling Party

November 23, 2026

Just two days ahead of his 100th day in office, Kim Min-seok, the leader of the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK)—South Korea’s ruling party—finds himself standing on remarkably shaky ground. What was meant to be a milestone reflecting steady governance alongside the administration of President Lee Jae-myung has instead turned into a rigorous test of political survival. A sudden five-percentage-point drop in the ruling party’s approval ratings, combined with escalating factional warfare within party ranks, has complicated the leadership’s strategic math and raised sharp questions about its long-term direction.

For observers watching South Korean politics from abroad, the current moment highlights a classic dilemma in parliamentary party management. As the ruling party, the Democratic Party bears the dual responsibility of coordinating smooth legislative policy with the presidential office while keeping its own legislative caucus united. Yet, as internal squabbles eclipse practical policy accomplishments, Kim’s leadership is being squeezed between the demands of a fiercely loyal party base and an increasingly disillusioned general public.

A 100-Day Milestone Defined by Divided Loyalty and Falling Polls

The latest polling figures surrounding the 100-day mark send a stark warning to the Democratic Party leadership. The five-percentage-point decline in party support represents a sharp reversal from the upward momentum Kim had promised upon taking the helm. Media coverage and political analysts in Seoul have pointed out that this downward curve is particularly concerning for a ruling party tasked with driving national policy and maintaining legislative momentum behind President Lee Jae-myung’s broader reform agenda.

Critiques from both inside and outside the party suggest that this drop is the direct consequence of wasted political energy. Rather than delivering tangible economic and social policy achievements that improve everyday public livelihoods—a core focus known locally as minsaeng— party leadership has allowed itself to be drawn into prolonged internal disputes. For moderate and unaligned voters who expected stability and competent governance from the governing party, the persistent noise of party infighting has eroded confidence.

Key Takeaway from Recent Polling: According to a poll conducted by local news outlet Kuki News, Kim Min-seok maintains a firm hold on first place among Democratic Party supporters as the preferred party leader and top prospective presidential candidate for the progressive camp, even as the party's overall public approval rating has fallen by 5 percentage points.

This stark divergence highlights the double-edged nature of Kim’s current standing. On one hand, maintaining overwhelming support among core party loyalists provides a formidable shield against immediate internal challenges. On the other hand, an over-reliance on core partisans underscores a clear failure to expand outreach into the center. While enthusiasm among hardline supporters keeps internal opponents at bay, it does little to rebuild the broader public coalition required to sustain effective legislative momentum.

Factional Fracture: The Choe Min-hee Controversy and Resurgent Heavyweights

The primary catalyst exposing the leadership’s internal weaknesses has been the contentious disciplinary process involving DPK lawmaker Choe Min-hee. What began as a routine internal disciplinary matter before the party’s ethics committee quickly escalated into a high-stakes proxy war between rival internal factions. Reports of deep communication breakdowns within the caucus—including lawmakers actively dodging one another’s phone calls—have laid bare how fractured the party's internal dynamics have become.

Kim’s inability to deliver a decisive resolution or establish clear guidelines early in the Choe Min-hee dispute allowed party infighting to play out publicly in the media. By allowing factional rivalries to simmer unchecked, the leadership demonstrated a hesitation that critics argue has severely undermined its authority. In party politics, a leadership team that appears incapable of maintaining internal order quickly risks losing the trust of its own rank-and-file members.

Exploiting this visible authority gap, senior party heavyweights have begun stepping back into the spotlight to assert their own influence. The vocal public interventions by heavyweight lawmaker Choo Mi-ae, alongside strategic maneuvers by lawmaker Jung Chung-rae, have added layers of complexity to the party’s internal power structure. Political analysts view the resurgence of these influential figures as a direct response to perceived weakness at the top.

This dynamic has intensified what party insiders refer to as the "Myeong-Cheong battle"—a sharp confrontation between rival party factions competing for dominant influence over the ruling party's direction. With senior figures pursuing independent political trajectories that directly clash with official leadership messaging, Kim’s capacity to manage the party caucus and coordinate effectively with the executive branch is facing its sternest test yet.

Hardline Gambits and Rhetorical Youth Outreach

Faced with falling poll numbers and deepening internal divisions, the Democratic Party leadership has attempted to shift the political narrative by turning outward. Most notably, leadership figures officially raised the prospect of impeaching Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae. This aggressive stance against the head of the judicial branch represents a classic tactical drive: creating a high-profile external confrontation to unify progressive loyalists and distract from internal factional friction.

However, resorting to such extreme legislative measures carries substantial risks. While aggressive rhetoric against the judiciary may temporarily energize the party’s core base, it risks creating a severe backlash among moderate voters who view attempts to impeach the Chief Justice as an overreach that destabilizes judicial independence and the rule of law. Relying on external battles to enforce internal unity is at best a short-term fix that leaves underlying party fractures unaddressed.

Concurrently, the leadership has attempted to broaden its public appeal by focusing on younger demographics. Kim publicly acknowledged the issue, stating that "one of the Democratic Party's biggest concerns is the youth," and launched several initiatives aimed at opening dialogue with younger voters. Yet, these efforts have faced criticism for lacking concrete policy substance.

The alienation of younger voters stems from deep-seated structural pressures, including housing affordability, job market competition, and persistent inflation. Rhetorical declarations of concern and headline-grabbing public events are unlikely to turn the tide among young citizens without practical legislative solutions that deliver tangible economic relief.

Legislative Coordination vs. Internal Chaos: What to Watch Next

Despite its internal troubles, Kim’s leadership has demonstrated functional competence in one critical area: executive-legislative coordination. Over his first 100 days, the party leadership maintained close working relationships with President Lee Jae-myung’s presidential office and executive cabinet ministers, helping to shepherd key legislative agenda items through the National Assembly. This reflects a paradoxical reality: the ruling party leadership functions relatively smoothly when managing external policy alignment with the government, but struggles significantly when managing its own internal organization.

As Kim Min-seok passes the 100-day mark, his leadership enters a decisive phase. Restoring public trust and securing his position as a primary leader within the progressive camp will require moving beyond political rhetoric and demonstrating concrete results. To navigate the road ahead, international observers should monitor several key indicators:

  • Resolution of Internal Discipline: How the leadership handles the final outcome of lawmaker Choe Min-hee’s disciplinary process will serve as a bellwether for whether Kim can re-establish control over rival factions and rein in independent maneuvers by senior heavyweights like Choo Mi-ae and Jung Chung-rae.
  • The Trajectory of Judicial Confrontation: Whether the ruling party formally pursues the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae or dials back its hardline rhetoric will indicate whether the leadership chooses to double down on base mobilization or pivot back toward capturing moderate voters.
  • Legislative Pivot to Economic Policy: Watch whether the Democratic Party can shift Assembly focus away from internal political battles and toward tangible economic and minsaeng (livelihood) legislation designed to address housing and employment concerns for young voters.

The true measure of a ruling party leadership is rarely tested during quiet times; it is revealed during moments of compounded crisis. Whether Kim Min-seok can reconcile core base expectations with broader national governance will determine not only the stability of South Korea's governing party, but also his own political future.
