The Opposition’s Strategy: Inside Jung Jeom-sik’s Offensive Against the Lee Jae-myung Administration

November 14, 2026

For those watching South Korean politics from afar, the current landscape often looks like a chaotic tug-of-war. However, on November 13, the People Power Party (PPP)—South Korea’s main conservative opposition party—provided a masterclass in how a minority party attempts to dictate the national agenda. Jung Jeom-sik, the PPP’s floor leader, took center stage, deploying a multi-pronged offensive that touched on the national budget, high-stakes special investigations, and regional development.

To understand why these moves matter, one must recognize that the PPP is currently operating under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, who leads the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK). In this polarized environment, the opposition's survival strategy relies on identifying the government’s vulnerabilities—whether they be policy missteps or internal party friction—and turning them into political wedges. Jung’s actions on November 13 were not merely administrative directives; they were a blueprint for how the opposition intends to claw back influence in the legislative process.

A Frontal Assault on the Budget and Economic Policy

The most immediate flashpoint is the national budget. Jung Jeom-sik issued a stern directive to all standing committees of the National Assembly: the opposition must immediately withdraw all requests for increasing the "Future Response Fund."

For the uninitiated, the "Future Response Fund" is a government-led financial vehicle that the PPP characterizes as a "deceptive budget" designed to circumvent the National Assembly’s constitutional power to review and approve spending. By ordering his party members to pull their own local project-related funding requests from this pool, Jung is making a calculated bet. He is signaling that the PPP is willing to sacrifice local pork-barrel spending to seize the moral high ground, framing the government’s budgetary process as a fundamental threat to parliamentary oversight.

Beyond the immediate spending, Jung doubled down on economic criticism. He attacked the ruling DPK over recent vacillations regarding the Comprehensive Real Estate Tax, labeling a brief moment of internal dissent within the ruling party as a "failed rebellion." By pivoting to a demand for a 1% point reduction in corporate tax rates, Jung is attempting to shift the narrative from mere criticism of the Lee Jae-myung administration to a proactive push for a conservative economic agenda, aiming to keep the government on the defensive regarding its fiscal and property policies.

Weaponizing Internal Conflict: The Shincheonji Special Counsel

Perhaps the most aggressive move of the day was Jung’s call for a Special Counsel investigation into the "Shincheonji allegations" involving the ruling Democratic Party. Shincheonji, a religious movement that became a focal point of public scrutiny during the COVID-19 pandemic, is a sensitive topic in Korean society. By demanding an independent investigation, the PPP is not merely raising concerns about the religious group; they are exploiting the current internal turmoil within the ruling party.

The DPK has recently been plagued by reports of internal fissures and allegations regarding the unauthorized viewing of sensitive materials. By demanding a special investigation, Jung is effectively weaponizing these grievances. He is framing the issue as a failure of governance that cannot be resolved through internal apologies behind closed doors. This is a classic "political counter-punch": by demanding that the ruling party be subjected to the same level of scrutiny it has often applied to its rivals, the PPP seeks to erode the moral authority of the Lee Jae-myung administration and force a public reckoning that could distract the government from its core legislative goals.

Regional Strategy and the Administrative Capital

While waging war on national issues, the PPP also kept a sharp eye on regional voter sentiment. Jung met with representatives from the party’s Sejong City chapter, who have been lobbying for the "Special Act on the Administrative Capital."

Sejong City was designed to function as the country's de facto administrative hub. By pledging that the PPP would "take full responsibility" for the passage of the Special Act, Jung is performing a dual function. First, he is shoring up support in a region where the conservative base needs to remain engaged. Second, he is shifting the burden onto the ruling DPK. Because the DPK holds a majority, any failure to pass this legislation can now be framed by the opposition as a failure of the ruling party to follow through on its promises, effectively using a local issue to build a national narrative of government obstructionism.

Additionally, Jung’s attendance at the inauguration of a youth provincial councilor association underscored a broader attempt to revitalize the party’s base. His refrain that "the answers to politics lie in the field" is a direct appeal to voters who are disillusioned with the centralized, often disconnected politics of Seoul.

What to Watch Next

The events of November 13 paint a clear picture: the PPP, under Jung Jeom-sik’s leadership, is moving away from passive opposition and into a phase of active confrontation. As the legislative session intensifies, keep an eye on the following:

  • Budget Standoff: Whether the PPP’s move to withdraw from the "Future Response Fund" leads to a legislative deadlock or forces the government to restructure its spending plans.
  • The Special Counsel Momentum: Watch to see if the demand for an investigation into the Shincheonji allegations gains traction in the media or if it remains a tool for political posturing.
  • Legislative Maneuvering in Sejong: How the DPK responds to the "Special Act" challenge. If they block it, the PPP will frame it as a betrayal of the region; if they support it, the PPP will claim credit for the initiative.

Jung Jeom-sik’s strategy is clear: he is betting that by keeping the government in a state of constant, high-pressure defense, the PPP can regain the initiative in the national conversation. Whether this high-octane approach will result in tangible political gains or merely deepen the existing legislative gridlock remains the central question for the coming weeks.
