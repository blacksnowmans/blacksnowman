The Shadow War Inside the 2025 Democratic Party Convention

October 28, 2026

In July 2025, the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK)—the current ruling party under President Lee Jae-myung—faced a leadership convention that felt less like an internal democratic process and more like a battlefield. To the outside observer, it appeared that the party’s two primary pillars, Park Chan-dae and Jung Chung-rae, were locked in a vicious, fratricidal struggle. But to those watching closely, the chaos was not a natural evolution of politics. It was the result of a coordinated, malicious infiltration designed to tear the party apart from the inside.

The Manufactured Demonization of Jung Chung-rae

During the summer of 2025, Jung Chung-rae and Park Chan-dae were widely seen as the party’s "two-horse chariot," responsible for steering the future of the Democratic Party. Jung, who had served as the Judiciary Committee chair during the impeachment of the disgraced former president Yoon Seok-yeol (now incarcerated), was a symbol of reform. Park was deeply committed to the success of the Lee Jae-myung administration.

Despite their shared goals, an aggressive campaign began to circulate online, labeling Jung a "watermelon"—a derogatory Korean political slang term used to accuse a liberal politician of being a conservative hidden in a liberal's shell. For party members, the sight of this veteran reformist being attacked by his own supposed base was baffling. It was clear that this was not standard campaign rhetoric; it was a deliberate attempt to alienate voters and fracture the party's cohesion by painting one of its most loyal members as an enemy.

The conflict was entirely artificial. Publicly, Jung and Park remained staunch allies. On July 21, 2025, Park took to social media to shut down the rumors. "Jung Chung-rae is a great politician, and more importantly, a friend and comrade," Park wrote. "If they call Jung a 'watermelon' for being an enemy within, then call me one too." It was a plea for his supporters to stop the baseless attacks, recognizing that the friction was being stoked by forces whose only goal was to weaken the momentum of the Lee Jae-myung government.

The Exposure of External Infiltration

The situation escalated on July 30, 2025, when Park Chan-dae held a press conference at the National Assembly to reveal the disturbing truth: the conflict was being stoked by people entirely outside the party. Park presented evidence that individuals associated with a pro-Yoon Seok-yeol group on the social media platform Naver Band were posing as DPK members to spread disinformation and fan the flames of internal resentment.

Park’s decision to pursue legal action against these provocateurs was a turning point. It proved that the instability within the DPK was not spontaneous, but a calculated "divide and conquer" strategy. Why were these external forces so obsessed with the convention? Because by sowing discord, they aimed to sabotage the DPK’s legislative agenda and paralyze the reform efforts of the current administration.

This atmosphere was exacerbated by the rise of a toxic framing term known as "Moon-Jo-Teol-Re-Yu"—a shorthand used to attack a collection of prominent liberal figures, including former President Moon Jae-in and others. By grouping figures with vastly different political histories into one "enemy" category, external agitators effectively turned supporters against each other, replacing healthy debate with a culture of suspicion. The party was effectively being gaslit into fighting itself.

The Unresolved Mystery of Outside Interference

The 2025 convention was further muddied by allegations involving the Shincheonji Church, a controversial religious group. Party members Kim Min-seok and Park Seon-won publicly raised concerns about organized infiltration by the group to sway the leadership outcome. While the accusations were explosive, the subsequent lack of transparent verification left many party members frustrated.

The core issue here transcends mere politics—it is a matter of democratic sovereignty. If an external religious organization is systematically attempting to influence the decision-making structure of a major political party, it constitutes a grave threat to the republic. The DPK’s struggle to address these infiltrations highlights a vulnerability in South Korea’s digital-age political system, where identity verification and the integrity of online polls remain susceptible to manipulation.

Moving Forward: A Lesson in Resilience

The events of 2025 serve as a stark reminder that the greatest threat to a reformist government often comes from its own susceptibility to division. The DPK learned the hard way that "division equals defeat." The path forward requires more than just calling for unity; it requires the construction of robust, modern systems to verify party membership and protect internal discourse from digital sabotage.

As the party continues to navigate the post-impeachment era under President Lee Jae-myung, the challenge is to cultivate a culture where internal debate is welcomed but external manipulation is ruthlessly filtered out. The "watermelon" smears and the "Moon-Jo-Teol-Re-Yu" frameworks were not political opinions—they were weapons of war against the party's cohesion.

What to watch next: The DPK is currently in the process of auditing its digital infrastructure and membership systems. The focus remains on whether the party can implement more sophisticated "digital defenses" to prevent the infiltration of fake members in future elections. Furthermore, party members continue to demand full transparency regarding the investigations into the external groups that attempted to hijack the 2025 convention. For the Democratic Party, the goal is clear: ensure that the party remains a house for its members, not a playground for those who wish to see it burn from the outside.
