Inside the Democratic Party's Civil War: How a Single Insult Sparked a Factional Showdown

November 19, 2026

Picture a political party holding the reins of power, ostensibly united behind a president from its own ranks, only to tear itself apart over a single, carelessly chosen metaphor. In South Korean politics, words carry immense weight, and the phrase "a pack of hyenas" has just blown open a bitter factional war within the Democratic Party, the liberal ruling party currently backing the administration of President Lee Jae-myung.

What began as a controversy over an aggressive remark by a senior lawmaker has rapidly metastasized into an all-out power struggle involving the party's heaviest hitters. As key legislative bodies are abruptly disbanded and party heavyweights trade public accusations of internal sabotage, the ruling camp is facing its most severe test of discipline and cohesion since taking power.

The Fall of the Media Publicness TF and the Fallout of the 'Hyena' Remark

On November 18, the Democratic Party abruptly decided to disband its Media Publicness Task Force, a policy body that had been led by Representative Choi Min-hee. While the leadership framed the move as the natural conclusion of the body's activities, it was widely read as a panicked preemptive strike to contain the political damage caused by Choi's recent behavior. Specifically, Choi had lashed out at critics and colleagues with the explosive description of "a pack of hyenas," a slur that instantly boomeranged.

Rather than quelling the storm, the dissolution of the task force sent shockwaves through the party. Progressive supporters felt a deep sense of demoralization, watching a key institutional pillar of their media reform agenda sacrificed overnight. Critics both inside and outside the party argued that Choi's toxic language dragged down the party's dignity and alienated moderate voters. Yet, the leadership's heavy-handed decision to dissolve the TF only invited fierce backlash, transforming an individual disciplinary issue into an institutional crisis.

Chung Sung-rae Breaks Silence to Counterattack Leader Kim Min-seok

As the pressure on Choi mounted, Representative Chung Sung-rae—a heavyweight within the party—broke several days of absolute silence to launch a furious defense. Declaring his fierce opposition to any disciplinary action against Choi, Chung argued that punishing her would achieve nothing constructive and would merely fracture the party.

Crucially, Chung's intervention went far beyond merely shielding a colleague. He openly questioned the timing of the leaked transcripts that brought Choi's remarks to light, implying that the push for disciplinary action was a targeted political hit job orchestrated by elements within the leadership. By taking direct aim at the political judgment of party leader Kim Min-seok, Chung elevated a local personnel dispute into an open rebellion against the party's top brass. To compound the counterattack, Chung even turned the tables, suggesting that domestic political manipulation within party lines constituted a far greater act of betrayal—invoking controversial political maneuvering phrases to frame Choi's attackers as the true culprits.

Choo Mi-ae and Woo Won-shik Enter the Fray

The rift widened further when former Justice Minister and party heavyweight Representative Choo Mi-ae joined the fray, throwing her immense political weight behind the anti-leadership faction. Choo argued publicly that the party’s Ethics Tribunal should first judge the statements and conduct of party leader Kim Min-seok himself, alleging that Kim's handling of the affair was driving the division rather than healing it. Her fiery intervention effectively redefined the crisis from an effort to save Choi Min-hee into a direct referendum on Kim Min-seok's leadership.

Amidst the escalating crossfire, senior lawmaker Woo Won-shik attempted to restore calm, cautioning that the Democratic Party does not belong to any single faction or individual like Kim or Chung. Woo urged restraint and called on party elders to stop stepping on each other's toes. However, his appeals fell largely on deaf ears. In an atmosphere poisoned by deep-seated factionalism, even appeals for unity were viewed with suspicion, highlighting a party where prominent figures appear to view the organization as personal fiefdoms.

The Disciplinary Dilemma and the Trap of Internal Warfare

The immediate operational crisis now rests on the desk of the party's Ethics Tribunal, which faces a no-win scenario. If the tribunal slaps Choi with heavy disciplinary sanctions, it will trigger open warfare with powerful heavyweights like Chung Sung-rae and Choo Mi-ae, alongside the party's aggressive core base. Conversely, if it lets her off with a light reprimand or clears her entirely, it will face intense accusations from a bloc of 55 first-term lawmakers who have formally demanded strict punishment, as well as from alienated moderate voters.

This localized bloodletting has completely derailed the ruling party's legislative focus. While parliamentarians spend their days exchanging blows over internal discipline and media task force demobilization, critical public policy initiatives are sidelined. Factional warfare never benefits those in power, and as the infighting deepens, public fatigue is visibly setting in.

The Sword Aimed Inward
When a political party's disciplinary mechanism is deployed against its own members in the midst of bitter factional strife, the blade invariably cuts backward into the party's own foundation. What the Democratic Party desperately needs right now is not a public execution of words, but a genuine restoration of collective leadership.

What to Watch Next

  • The Ethics Tribunal Ruling: The ultimate verdict on Representative Choi Min-hee will serve as the litmus test for who holds the upper hand within the ruling camp. Watch for whether the leadership stands firm or caves to heavy-hitter pressure.
  • First-Term Lawmaker Mobilization: Keep an eye on the 55 freshman legislators who demanded strict discipline. Their willingness to stand up to—or fold under—party heavyweights will signal the balance of power between the party's old guard and its newer voices.
  • Legislative Recovery: Monitor whether the Democratic Party can pivot back to crucial livelihood and economic legislation, or if the distraction of internal warfare continues to dominate the nation's political agenda.
