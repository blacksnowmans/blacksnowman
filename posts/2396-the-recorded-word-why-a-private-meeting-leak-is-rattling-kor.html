The Recorded Word: Why a Private Meeting Leak is Rattling Korea’s Ruling Party

November 16, 2026

In the high-stakes theater of South Korean politics, a private room is rarely truly private. On October 16, inside a closed-door meeting intended to soothe internal frictions among party faithful, Choi Min-hee—the Chief Supreme Council member of the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK)—spoke with the candidness of a leader among friends. She was there to discuss the future of the party and the strategic maneuvers of the current administration under President Lee Jae-myung. But months later, a transcript of that meeting has surfaced, igniting a firestorm that threatens to reshape the leadership of the country's ruling party and exposing deep-seated cultural rifts between its factions.

For an outsider, the controversy might look like a typical political spat over a "hot mic" moment. However, in the context of South Korea’s current political landscape—where the DPK holds the presidency and the main opposition, the People Power Party, is reeling from the impeachment and imprisonment of former leader Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—this is more than a gossip item. It is a battle over the "correct" interpretation of the President's past decisions and a test of how the party handles its most vocal, and sometimes most toxic, supporters.

The Transcript That Shook the Supreme Council

The core of the crisis lies in a leaked recording from that October 16 meeting. In the audio, Choi Min-hee is heard discussing the strategic choices made by President Lee Jae-myung during his time as party leader, particularly during the chaotic period surrounding the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. Choi’s remarks, as initially reported, seemed to suggest that President Lee had once been hesitant to push for full impeachment, preferring instead a path of resignation or compromise to win over centrist voters.

Specifically, Choi was recorded saying that even a President's political judgment "isn't always right" and that he had been "wrong" to initially favor a cooperative stance with figures like Han Duck-soo, who served as Prime Minister during the previous era. To the "Chin-seok" faction—supporters of Kim Min-seok, the DPK leader who has since ordered an ethics investigation into Choi—these words were nothing short of heresy. They viewed it as a direct attack on the President’s authority and a distortion of history.

The fallout was immediate. Kim Min-seok initiated a "power of office" ethics audit, a move that signals a potential for severe disciplinary action. Within the DPK, groups like "Deomincho" (a circle of first-term lawmakers) and "Deomin-hyeok" have called for strict accountability. For Choi, the stakes are existential: as an elected member of the Supreme Council—the DPK’s highest decision-making body—even a mid-level disciplinary measure could strip her of her leadership post, effectively overturning the will of the party members who voted for her.

Context vs. Content: Was It a Critique or a Lesson?

However, as is often the case with leaked transcripts, the surrounding context provides a very different narrative. Analysis from independent media outlets, such as the YouTube channel "Kim Seung-hyun’s Thesis" and Sisa Tapa, suggests that the leaked snippet was a "clipped" version of a much longer, more nuanced conversation. According to these reports, Choi was not attacking the President, but rather defending a strategic debate regarding the impeachment of Cho Hee-dae, the Chief Justice of the Supreme Court.

In the full context, a party member had asked Choi why the DPK was hesitant to impeach Justice Cho. Choi explained the legal and political risks—the fear that the Constitutional Court might reject the move—but then used President Lee as an example of a leader who evolves. Her point was that even a leader as capable as Lee Jae-myung can start with one political intuition (such as seeking a centrist compromise) and then, through internal party debate and a changing landscape, arrive at a more decisive "correct" conclusion. In her view, she was praising the President’s ability to listen and adapt, rather than mocking his past "mistakes."

The confusion was further muddied by a verbal slip. Choi admitted to accidentally saying "Yoon Suk-yeol" when she meant "Han Duck-soo" during part of the explanation, a "memory lapse" for which she has since issued a public apology. While her critics see this as a sign of incompetence or hidden malice, her defenders argue it is a minor human error being weaponized by internal rivals to stage a "leadership coup."

The 'Kill Switch' in the DPK Disciplinary Code

To understand why this matters to the future of the Lee Jae-myung administration, one must look at the DPK's internal rulebook. Disciplinary actions in the party follow a four-step ladder: a Warning, Suspension of Party Duties, Suspension of Party Membership, and finally, Expulsion.

For a rank-and-file member, a "Suspension of Party Duties" is often a slap on the wrist. But for someone in Choi’s position, it is a political kill switch. Because she is a "Supreme Council member," her position is tied directly to her status as a party official. If her "party duties" are suspended, she is immediately removed from the council. Unlike an expulsion, which requires a high bar of evidence and a lengthy appeal, a suspension of duties can be enacted more swiftly, allowing the party leadership to effectively "reorganize" the top tier without a new election.

This has led to accusations of a double standard. Critics of the audit point out that other party figures have made public gaffes—such as insulting citizens during protests or making unsubstantiated claims during elections—without facing the "power of office" audit. By targeting Choi for a private comment that was arguably taken out of context, the DPK leadership risks alienating the grassroots base that values Choi’s outspoken nature. Park Kyu-hwan, a former DPK Supreme Council member, noted that this isn't just about one person; it's about the legitimacy of elected leadership versus the power of the party apparatus.

The Dark Underbelly of the Digital Base

Adding a layer of toxicity to the dispute is the role of "hardcore" supporters. The South Korean political internet is a battlefield of nicknames and slurs. On one side are the "Gae-ttal" (a term for fierce supporters of the President), and on the other are those who use derogatory terms like "Jyo-myeong-i" to mock the President. These labels have become shorthand for a political culture that often prioritizes loyalty over logic.

The controversy took a darker turn with a "Sisa Tapa" report concerning a private KakaoTalk group chat. This wasn't a public forum, but an invitation-only group requiring real-name verification, reportedly attended by several high-ranking party figures and influential supporters. Leaked screenshots from this group showed members using violent language against Choi Min-hee, including calls to "kill her off" and various sexual insults.

While some participants reportedly tried to restrain the language, the fact that such vitriol existed in a space shared by political insiders has shocked many. It highlights a glaring hypocrisy: while Choi is being audited for her *words* regarding political history in a private meeting, those within the party's inner circle who used *violent and abusive language* against a female colleague have largely escaped formal scrutiny. If the DPK applies a "zero tolerance" policy to Choi's analytical remarks but ignores the "kill rooms" of its digital base, it faces a profound crisis of moral authority.

What to Watch Next

The resolution of the Choi Min-hee affair will be a bellwether for the internal health of the Democratic Party of Korea as it navigates the Lee Jae-myung presidency. Here is what to monitor in the coming weeks:

  • The Ethics Committee Decision: The specific level of discipline handed to Choi will reveal the current leadership's true intentions. A "Suspension of Duties" will be seen as a declaration of war against Choi's faction and her supporters, likely leading to a fracture in the party's unity.
  • The "Group Chat" Fallout: As names of participants in the controversial KakaoTalk group are confirmed or denied—including those of current lawmakers—the party may be forced to launch a second, broader investigation into its internal culture. If they don't, the "double standard" narrative will only grow.
  • Presidential Silence: President Lee Jae-myung has, so far, remained largely above the fray. Whether he steps in to mediate or allows the audit to run its course will signal how much he relies on the "Chin-seok" faction to maintain order within the party.

Ultimately, this isn't just about a recording or a slip of the tongue. It is about whether Korea's ruling party can handle internal dissent and historical analysis without resorting to the "politics of elimination." In the shadow of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s failed legacy, the DPK's greatest challenge may not be the opposition, but its own internal mirrors.
