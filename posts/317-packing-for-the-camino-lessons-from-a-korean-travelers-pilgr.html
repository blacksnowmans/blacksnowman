Packing for the Camino: Lessons from a Korean Traveler's Pilgrimage Prep

September 07, 2026

The Santiago de Compostela pilgrimage—the Camino de Santiago—is often romanticized as a life-changing, spiritual journey. But for one Korean YouTuber, twelve days before departure, the reality felt less like a spiritual awakening and more like a tactical, high-stakes military operation. As they laid out gear on the floor, muttering, "I’m the type of person who can't focus if even one small thing is uncomfortable," it became clear: the pilgrimage doesn't start at the airport; it starts with the grueling, anxiety-filled labor of packing.

The Physics of Survival: Gear and Clothing

For many, the biggest mistake is treating the Camino like a fashion show. The weather in Spain between March and April is notoriously volatile, often cycling through all four seasons in a single week. The goal here is utility, not style. The YouTuber’s strategy focused on a "walk vs. rest" dichotomy: high-function hiking pants and moisture-wicking shirts for the trail, and a single, well-worn pair of shorts for the albergue (pilgrim hostels).

The choice of footwear is perhaps the most critical decision a pilgrim makes. Choosing the Hoka Kaha 2—a premium hiking boot retailing for about $230 (300,000 KRW)—suggests that when it comes to long-distance walking, there is no room for budget compromises. Combined with Injinji toe socks and a generous application of Vaseline, the setup is designed to prevent the pilgrimage-ending nightmare of blisters before they even form. In the world of the Camino, your feet are your primary engine; if they fail, the journey ends.

The Digital Pilgrim: Managing Tech in a Modern Age

One of the most relatable hurdles for the modern Korean traveler is navigating the evolving regulations regarding lithium-ion batteries. Due to strict aviation safety laws, all power banks and camera batteries must be carried in carry-on luggage, neatly organized in transparent zipper bags to satisfy airport security.

Beyond the legalities, there is an interesting tension between the digital and the analog. While the YouTuber is packing advanced gear like action cameras and smartwatches, they also made the deliberate choice to carry wired earphones as a fail-safe against battery drainage. It is a reminder that while technology facilitates our connection to the outside world, a pilgrim must be prepared for when that connection inevitably fails. Packing is essentially an exercise in maintaining autonomy—ensuring that when you are on the trail, you are not defined by the limitations of your devices.

Hygiene and the "Soul" of Korean Comfort

Packing for a month on the road requires ruthless efficiency. Replacing bulky liquid shampoo and body wash with two simple bars of all-in-one soap and solid toothpaste tablets is a masterclass in weight reduction. These aren't just minor adjustments; they are necessary sacrifices to keep the backpack under the "Golden Ratio" of weight.

However, no Korean traveler is truly ready without a taste of home. Including two tubes of gochujang (Korean chili paste) and six packets of instant ramen soup base is a psychological anchor. While critics might argue that one should embrace the local diet, these items serve as a "moral boost." When the bread and cheese of rural Spain become repetitive, that familiar, spicy broth serves as a vital bridge back to one's roots, turning a lonely meal in a hostel into a moment of communal comfort.

The Weight of the Pack and the Weight of Expectations

The YouTuber’s final haul tipped the scales at nearly 12kg (including a secondary bag), a weight that will feel exponentially heavier when ascending the Pyrenees. There is a common trap here: the "just in case" mentality. Every item packed to satisfy a hypothetical "what if" scenario—what if it’s cold, what if I’m hungry, what if I can’t wash my clothes—eventually becomes a physical burden that degrades the quality of the journey.

Ultimately, the Camino is a condensed version of life. It forces you to ask: What can I afford to leave behind? The YouTuber’s decision to document this process is more than just content creation; it is a way of processing the anxiety of a major life transition. For many young Koreans today, the pilgrimage is an escape from the suffocating pressures of the job market, housing costs, and the relentless pace of life in Seoul. It is a space to verify one’s own existence outside the constraints of society.

What to Watch Next

As the departure date nears, the real test will be the "ruthless edit." Most pilgrims end up mailing home or discarding items within the first week of the Camino. Watch to see if this traveler stays committed to the 10kg goal, or if they choose to sacrifice their comfort to keep their pack light. The success of the journey won't be measured by the photos taken or the miles covered, but by the ability to shed the unnecessary weight—both inside the backpack and within the mind.
