The Cult Allegations Tearing South Korea’s Ruling Party Apart

November 11, 2026

On the morning of November 10, the political atmosphere within South Korea’s ruling party, the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), reached a breaking point. After reviewing the findings of a specialized task force (TF) meant to investigate allegations of infiltration by the Shincheonji Church of Jesus—a controversial religious group often accused of political meddling—Representative Lee Sung-yoon emerged with a blunt assessment. He described the report as "devoid of any real content" and openly accused the party’s leader, Kim Min-seok, of fabricating a crisis to suit his own political agenda. By the afternoon, Kim had abruptly canceled all scheduled public appearances, retreating into a silence that has signaled a widening, volatile schism within the party.

To an outside observer, the scale of this infighting might seem disproportionate. Yet, in the context of South Korean politics—where the DPK currently holds the presidency under Lee Jae-myung—this internal civil war is threatening the party’s cohesion and its ability to govern. The controversy, which began as a whisper about religious infiltration, has transformed into a high-stakes power struggle that pits faction against faction, leaving the party’s leadership in a state of paralysis.

The Investigation That Backfired

The core of the dispute lies in the legitimacy of the DPK’s internal investigation. For weeks, Kim Min-seok and his allies had suggested that Shincheonji followers had systematically joined the party to influence internal primaries. However, once the official data was finally unsealed, it revealed that the suspected individuals accounted for a mere 0.4% of total voters.

Representative Lee Sung-yoon’s reaction was visceral. By publicizing his disappointment, he effectively characterized the investigation as a "post-facto alibi"—a desperate attempt by the leadership to provide evidence for an accusation they had already leveled without proof. The fallout has been immediate: the investigation, which was intended to project strength and vigilance, has instead become a symbol of internal dishonesty. As the data is now being interpreted in diametrically opposed ways—some see a dangerous infiltration, others see a statistically insignificant coincidence—it is clear that the party has moved past objective analysis and into a full-blown "frame war" where data is merely a weapon for factional advantage.

The Crisis of Leadership

Kim Min-seok’s decision to cancel his schedule and vanish from the public eye has only served to fuel speculation. In a political culture where the leader’s presence is seen as the anchor of stability, this "blackout" suggests that Kim is struggling to maintain control. The demand for an apology from Representative Lee, echoed by other senior figures, is more than a request for words; it is a political demand for the leadership to acknowledge an error in judgment.

The silence from the top is creating a vacuum, quickly filled by infighting. While some members are calling for urgent emergency meetings to reconcile, the emotional divide appears to have grown too deep. If the leader remains unable to resolve the conflict he helped initiate, his authority will continue to crater, potentially hindering the DPK’s ability to pass the legislative agenda of the Lee Jae-myung administration.

The Grassroots Uprising

Perhaps the most significant development is the intervention of the "Core Party Members" (rank-and-file, dues-paying members). On the evening of November 10, these members formally submitted a petition for the disciplinary action of Kim Min-seok. They argue that by relying on unsubstantiated rumors of a "secret alliance" with religious groups, the party leader has compromised the party’s integrity and fueled unnecessary division.

This is a warning shot from the party’s base to its elite. In the current political landscape, where the memory of the previous conservative administration—led by the now-imprisoned Yun Seok-youl—still serves as a rallying point for DPK unity, the party is particularly sensitive to accusations of being "undemocratic" or "conspiratorial." The petition shows that the party’s base is no longer willing to tolerate internal warfare that sacrifices party cohesion for the sake of petty factionalism.

The Opposition’s Opportunity

Predictably, the conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), has moved in to exploit the chaos. The PPP is now aggressively pushing for a special investigation into the Shincheonji allegations, arguing that the DPK’s own internal inquiry is insufficient. By calling for mandatory, state-led investigations, the opposition is effectively forcing the DPK to either continue defending a flawed internal report or to submit to an external investigation that could further damage its public image.

What started as a paranoid fear of religious meddling has transformed into a strategic vulnerability. The DPK is currently trapped in a "Shincheonji quagmire," where every attempt to move forward only traps them deeper. As the public watches this internal struggle unfold, the party risks losing the trust of neutral voters, who are increasingly frustrated by the lack of focus on policy and the economy.

What to watch next: The immediate focus will be on whether Kim Min-seok issues a formal apology. Without a clear path to reconciliation, the disciplinary petition by party members could gain steam, forcing a leadership transition or a total reshuffle of the party hierarchy. For the Lee Jae-myung administration, the goal is to steer the conversation back toward national policy, but as long as the "0.4% controversy" continues to dominate the headlines, the party’s momentum will remain stalled.
