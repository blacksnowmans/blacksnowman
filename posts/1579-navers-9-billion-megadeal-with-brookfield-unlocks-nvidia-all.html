Naver’s $9 Billion Megadeal with Brookfield Unlocks NVIDIA Alliance in East Asia’s AI Race

October 28, 2026

Imagine a technology titan whose dominance was historically built on domestic search queries and online shopping, suddenly committing capital on the scale of nation-states. In South Korea, Naver—the country’s leading internet portal and cloud services conglomerate—has just finalized one of the largest infrastructure deals in Asian tech history. By signing a binding $9 billion (approximately 12 trillion South Korean won) contract with global private equity giant Brookfield Asset Management, Naver is not merely expanding server racks. It is launching a 1-gigawatt (1GW) "AI Factory" project designed to serve as a central computing engine for the region's artificial intelligence ecosystem.

The deal represents a pivotal moment in the global AI boom, where the primary battleground has shifted from clever software algorithms to raw physical infrastructure: power grids, real estate, cooling systems, and advanced silicon. More crucially, securing this massive infrastructure partnership fulfills the final prerequisite for NVIDIA to execute a 1.5 trillion won (roughly $1.0 billion to $1.3 billion) equity investment in Naver, positioning the Santa Clara chipmaker as Naver’s third-largest shareholder.

A $9 Billion Bet: Inside Naver and Brookfield’s Infrastructure Pact

Executing a 12 trillion won capital commitment during an era of elevated interest rates and global macroeconomic caution is no minor undertaking. Naver, often described to foreign observers as the "Google of South Korea," has long controlled domestic search, digital payments, webtoons, and e-commerce. However, as frontier AI models demand astronomical computational capacity, Naver realized that maintaining leadership required securing infrastructure at an unprecedented scale.

Enter Brookfield Asset Management, one of the world's largest alternative asset managers with deep expertise in real assets, renewable energy, and infrastructure finance. Brookfield’s decision to commit $9 billion to a partnership in South Korea signals strong international confidence in the country’s tech capability and geographic relevance as an Asian data hub. Foreign institutional investors rarely place bets of this magnitude without stringent risk controls; Brookfield’s backing reflects a belief that Naver’s operational track record and Asia-wide enterprise footprint make it a prime candidate to host next-generation computing nodes.

This massive transaction covers every layer of heavy tech infrastructure: land acquisition, specialized data center construction, power supply arrangements, and high-density computing equipment integration. Rather than building piecemeal data centers every few years, Naver is locking in a multi-year pipeline of capital to shield itself from the hardware bottlenecks currently crippling tech firms worldwide.

The Silicon Alliance: How NVIDIA Becomes Naver’s Third-Largest Shareholder

The strategic heart of this deal lies in its domino effect on Naver’s balance sheet and cap table. Before committing more than a billion dollars of its own capital, NVIDIA demanded clear evidence that Naver had secured the physical infrastructure and power capacity required to deploy high-end graphics processing units (GPUs) efficiently.

With the Brookfield contract signed, that key condition is officially satisfied. NVIDIA is now set to inject approximately 1.5 trillion won ($1.0–$1.3 billion) into Naver through a targeted rights offering, making the semiconductor giant the third-largest shareholder in South Korea's flagship internet platform.

This transaction creates a deeply intertwined strategic alliance:

  • For NVIDIA: CEO Jensen Huang secures a massive, long-term anchor customer capable of operating a 1-gigawatt footprint in East Asia, guaranteeing a steady pipeline for NVIDIA's high-performance enterprise hardware.
  • For Naver: Gaining NVIDIA as a core shareholder virtually guarantees priority access to next-generation GPU allocations, supply chain preference, and deep co-engineering support at a time when global chip shortages remain a constant threat.
  • For the Market: The market valuation of Naver transitions from that of a domestic consumer portal to an essential player in the global AI supply chain.

Building the 1-Gigawatt ‘AI Factory’ and Redefining South Korea’s Tech Titan

Central to Naver's blueprint is the concept of the 1GW "AI Factory." To put 1 gigawatt in perspective, it is roughly equivalent to the total electrical output of a commercial nuclear reactor. Modern generative AI models and real-time inferencing engines consume vast amounts of electricity, rendering traditional corporate data centers obsolete.

Naver’s concept of an "AI Factory" signals a structural shift in how data centers operate. Rather than acting as passive storage warehouses for data, these facilities operate as heavy industrial computing plants. Raw data enters the facility, is processed through thousands of interconnected GPUs running complex neural networks, and emerges as continuous intelligence, customized AI models, and B2B services.

Key Takeaway: The AI battle is no longer won solely in code repositories; it is won on the factory floor of high-density data processing, where access to gigawatts of power and gigabytes of memory dictates market survival.

This infrastructure expansion allows Naver to pivot its business model. Facing fierce competition in domestic search and e-commerce from global giants like Google and aggressive cross-border platforms such as AliExpress and Temu, Naver is building a defensive moat as an enterprise AI infrastructure provider. Through its AI Factory, Naver can lease high-performance computing clusters and sovereign cloud environments to corporations, research institutes, and government bodies across Asia that lack the capital to build their own hardware facilities.

Sovereign AI, Foreign Capital, and the Geopolitical Balancing Act

This massive influx of infrastructure and capital lands squarely in the middle of a shifting global political landscape. As the United States and China lock horns over technological supremacy, smaller tech-forward nations face a dilemma: how to build advanced AI capabilities without surrendering their digital sovereignty or sensitive national data to foreign hyperscalers.

South Korea has positioned itself as a leading advocate for "Sovereign AI"—the principle that nations should develop their own localized foundational models, hosted on domestic infrastructure, to preserve their cultural context, language nuances, and regulatory oversight. Under the current administration of President Lee Jae-myung, national policy has heavily prioritized digital security, data sovereignty, and robust domestic computing networks as matters of national interest.

This private sector milestone offers a welcome contrast to past administrative instability. During Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure, South Korea's technology landscape suffered from policy fluctuations and controversial cuts to public research and development budgets. Naver’s ability to orchestrate a trilateral alliance spanning domestic operational expertise, North American asset management capital (Brookfield), and primary silicon architecture (NVIDIA) demonstrates how private enterprise can fortify national infrastructure despite prior macroeconomic turbulence.

Yet, this alliance comes with distinct risks that Korean policymakers and market analysts will scrutinize closely:

  • Dependency Risks: By tying its technological backbone so closely to NVIDIA’s architecture, Naver risks over-reliance on a single hardware vendor's proprietary ecosystem.
  • Capital Obligations: Managing a 12 trillion won infrastructure liability requires maintaining consistently high data center capacity utilization to service debt and asset obligations.
  • Sovereignty vs. Foreign Influence: While building domestic compute facilities supports national Sovereign AI initiatives, hosting major foreign institutional shareholders requires careful management to ensure strategic autonomy remains intact.

What to Watch Next

As this multi-billion dollar framework transitions from legal agreements to physical deployment, key milestones will determine whether Naver can successfully execute its ambitious pivot:

  • Equity Execution: Watch for the formal completion of Naver’s capital increase and the closing of NVIDIA’s 1.5 trillion won share purchase to confirm board governance arrangements.
  • Power Grid Allocations: Track how Naver secures municipal energy permits and high-capacity electrical grid access in South Korea to power its initial 1GW infrastructure phases.
  • Enterprise B2B Onboarding: Monitor major contract wins from enterprise clients and public sector institutions opting for Naver’s Sovereign AI and AI Factory hosting services over rival offerings from foreign cloud providers.
