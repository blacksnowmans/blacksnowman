Is the Long Slump for South Korean Semiconductor Giants Finally Over?

September 20, 2026

For the past two months, investors in South Korea have been trapped in a grueling holding pattern. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix—the twin pillars of the Korean stock market—have moved sideways, exhausting the patience of retail and institutional investors alike. However, a shift in the global landscape is beginning to stir the stagnant waters of the KOSPI.

As the U.S. semiconductor indices finally break through their long-standing resistance levels, anticipation is building that the domestic "box-range" slump is nearing its end. The global flow of capital is currently surging into three massive, interconnected engines of growth: semiconductors, power infrastructure, and optical communications. While many investors are fretting over the upcoming Chuseok holiday—the Korean autumn harvest festival often associated with market lethargy—the reality is that we are at a significant inflection point that demands more than just a "wait-and-see" approach.

The Myth of the Holiday Slump

Every year, a persistent market superstition surfaces: "You must trim your portfolio before a major holiday." Investors often interpret the typical decline in trading volume before the Chuseok break as a signal to close their wallets or liquidate positions. It is true that, historically, the market has seen minor dips of around 1% in the days leading up to the holiday. However, relying on calendar-based statistics to predict market direction is a dangerously short-sighted strategy.

The market does not move according to the dates on a calendar; it moves according to corporate earnings and the flow of capital. The industrial cycle and the current macroeconomic environment are far more meaningful than any cultural tradition. Those who sell perfectly healthy stocks simply because a holiday is approaching often find themselves left behind when the market resumes its momentum. Rather than reacting to short-term holiday fluctuations, savvy investors should look at the fundamental "stamina" of the market, which is currently far more robust than the general sentiment suggests.

Surviving the Triple-Witchings and High Interest Rates

The U.S. market recently navigated a massive "triple-witching" day—the simultaneous expiration of stock options, index futures, and stock futures—involving some $7 trillion in contracts. Despite intense volatility, technology stocks emerged as the clear leaders, with companies like Nvidia, Broadcom, and Micron posting significant gains. This resilience is telling: when market volatility rises, the "smart money" clearly distinguishes between companies with genuine, underlying strength and those without.

This strength persists even under the pressure of the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield, which has been hovering near the psychologically significant 5% mark (specifically 4.996%). In 2022, such high rates triggered a massive sell-off in the Nasdaq. However, the landscape in 2023 and beyond is different. Despite the high cost of capital, the AI and semiconductor sectors are generating such massive profits that they are successfully offsetting valuation pressures. The "5% threat" is no longer a guaranteed death sentence for equities, provided that corporate earnings growth can outpace the rising cost of debt.

The TSMC Signal and the Domestic Semiconductor Supply Chain

The true power of the current semiconductor rally is best illustrated by TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company), the world’s largest contract chipmaker. By announcing a broad price hike to its major clients, TSMC has sent a clear message: demand is so overwhelming that the market will absorb higher costs without hesitation. When the "boss" of the foundry industry raises prices, it creates a cascading effect that forces major chip designers like Intel and AMD to follow suit, ultimately bolstering margins across the entire supply chain.

This trend is now rippling into the Korean market. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are showing signs of reclaiming the 60-day moving average—a key technical indicator that often signals a shift from a downtrend to a recovery phase. Beyond these giants, suppliers of "so-bu-jang" (the Korean industry term for materials, parts, and equipment) are also heating up. Companies specializing in the equipment for front-end and back-end processes, such as HPSP or various HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) testing firms, are gearing up for a strong second half of the year as capacity expansion becomes a priority for data center operators.

Follow the Money: Power and Infrastructure

While semiconductors are the brain of the AI revolution, power infrastructure is its lifeblood. The explosive growth of AI data centers has created a global power shortage, making the electricity and transformer sector an "unstoppable trend." Companies like LS ELECTRIC and various cable manufacturers are seeing direct benefits as the world scrambles to upgrade its aging electrical grids to accommodate the massive energy appetite of new AI supercomputers.

In contrast, sectors like steel and traditional raw materials continue to struggle with lackluster earnings guidance, reminding us that not all cyclical sectors are created equal. Investors should remain disciplined: focus on the sectors where real, structural growth is being proven by data—semiconductors, power infrastructure, and optical communications—rather than getting distracted by speculative thematic stocks in sectors like cyber security, where the domestic competitive advantage remains unproven.

What to Watch Next

  • Semiconductor Re-entry: Watch for Samsung and SK Hynix to sustain their position above the 60-day moving average. A successful hold here confirms the end of the two-month stagnation.
  • Power Infrastructure Momentum: Keep a close eye on transformer and cable manufacturers; their order books are effectively a leading indicator of global AI data center construction.
  • Holiday Noise: Expect low volume during the upcoming Chuseok holiday. Treat any artificial dips caused by low liquidity as potential buying opportunities for high-conviction, fundamental leaders.

The market is currently offering a clear roadmap. The "worst of the worst" is behind us, and the companies capable of riding the AI and infrastructure wave are poised to benefit. The key is to look beyond the noise of the calendar and stay invested in the sectors that the capital is silently, but steadily, flowing into.
