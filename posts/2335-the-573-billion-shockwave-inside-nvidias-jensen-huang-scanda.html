The $573 Billion Shockwave: Inside Nvidia’s Jensen Huang Scandal, a Forged Mutiny, and the Fallout in South Korea

November 15, 2026

On the morning of November 14, panic tore through global financial centers and technology hubs. A dramatic report hit trading desks claiming that Nvidia’s top executive team had delivered an ultimatum to the board: restore co-founder Jensen Huang as Chief Executive Officer, or watch the leadership team resign en masse to join Elon Musk’s SpaceX. For a few frantic hours, the prospect of an executive exodus to SpaceX sent semiconductor markets into a tailspin. Yet before the trading day ended, the alleged mutiny fell apart completely. It was a hoax—a forged letter lifted almost word-for-word from the emergency petition OpenAI staff sent during Sam Altman’s brief ouster in late 2023.

While the SpaceX exodus was fabricated, the crisis that spawned it was undeniably real. The market manipulation scheme preyed on an unprecedented power vacuum at the world's most dominant chipmaker. Jensen Huang was forced out following revelations that he had repeatedly misled Nvidia’s executive leadership regarding an improper personal relationship with an individual named Alice Huang. For over a year, Huang had assured his top lieutenants that his contacts with Alice Huang were routine business meetings and customer communications. When internal audits and external inquiries exposed those claims as outright deceptions, the board was left with no choice but to terminate the figurehead who had built Nvidia over three decades.

The speed of the collapse has been catastrophic. In just seven days, Nvidia saw $573 billion in market capitalization evaporate—the largest single-week wipeout of corporate value in history. Beyond Wall Street, the aftershocks are reverberating through the global artificial intelligence supply chain, exposing critical vulnerabilities in South Korea’s technology sector, threatening a $510 billion joint AI venture, and reigniting geopolitical frictions between Washington and Beijing.

A Forged Mutiny and a Divided Workforce

The hoax letter circulated on November 14 was crafted to exploit the sheer shock of Huang’s departure. Whoever orchestrated the scheme copied the structural layout, emotional phrasing, and signature blocks of the famous OpenAI employee petition. By substituting corporate names and inserting a sensational claim about a mass migration to SpaceX, the perpetrators briefly convinced investors that Nvidia’s executive core was willing to burn the company down to bring Huang back. While half a day was enough for investigators to confirm the document was fraudulent, the damage to market stability was immediate.

That such an obvious forgery gained temporary credibility speaks volumes about the vulnerability of Nvidia’s post-Huang governance. For thirty years, the company operated under the absolute authority of its co-founder. Without his centralizing presence, the organization proved easily swayed by unverified rumors.

Inside Nvidia's headquarters, the psychological rift is deepening. On the company’s internal discussion boards, a post demanding Huang’s immediate reinstatement sparked a fierce debate, accumulating more than 1,600 comments. The workforce is sharply divided:

  • Huang Loyalists: A large faction of employees argues that losing the visionary leader who propelled Nvidia to the apex of the global technology pyramid guarantees corporate decline.
  • Ethics Advocates: A vocal group of workers expresses disillusionment, arguing that Huang's ethical lapses and sustained deception of senior management inflicted unpardonable damage on the firm's credibility.

This internal friction threatens to undermine organizational stability just as the company faces its most daunting operational challenge in decades.

Deception at the Top and a Chaotic Succession

The core catalyst of the scandal was not merely the existence of an improper personal relationship, but the systemic breach of trust that followed. When questions regarding Alice Huang first surfaced last year, Huang systematically assured senior leadership that the interactions were strictly professional. By framing personal indiscretions as normal commercial engagements, Huang compromised the integrity of executive governance.

For years, Huang’s iconic black leather jacket symbolized the triumph of aggressive engineering and unyielding ambition. However, when the board established that their CEO had lied to his closest corporate confidants to conceal private affairs, his standing collapsed. Nvidia's corporate culture had long celebrated absolute internal alignment. Once the founder’s personal dishonesty unspooled that alignment, the board had no ethical or legal defense to keep him at the helm.

The Governance Deficit: When an unchecked founder misleads executive leadership, the financial damages are rarely confined to the C-suite. Stockholders and ecosystem partners ultimately bear the cost of compromised corporate governance.

Filling the executive void is proving exceptionally difficult. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress has emerged as the frontrunner for the leadership role. Kress has historically represented a disciplined, conservative force within Nvidia, frequently acting as a counterweight to Huang’s high-risk infrastructure bets and startup capital outlays. A transition to Kress would likely mark a pivot toward financial defense, expense control, and balance-sheet preservation, potentially slowing Nvidia’s aggressive hardware development schedules.

Reports also surfaced that Nvidia’s board attempted to recruit René Haas, CEO of the British semiconductor design giant Arm. However, Nvidia officially denied any formal contact with Haas. With external recruitment rumors shot down and internal promotions signaling a defensive shift, the company’s long-term product roadmap remains shrouded in uncertainty.

Financial Carnage and the Threatened AI Ecosystem

Wall Street’s reaction to Huang’s departure was immediate and unforgiving. The loss of $573 billion in corporate value within one week reflects more than just anxiety over executive turnover; it signals a fundamental repricing of the entire AI hardware sector. Institutional investors who justified Nvidia's premium valuation based on Huang's singular execution velocity initiated aggressive selling sprees.

The panic is spreading to the broader financial architecture supporting AI hardware deployment. Under Huang, Nvidia pioneered a circular financing model, deploying roughly $500 billion in financial support networks, credit lines, and direct investments to help emerging AI software startups acquire expensive graphics processing units (GPUs). With Huang gone, institutional capital backing this $500 billion credit ecosystem is threatening to pull back.

This liquidity crunch poses an existential risk to cash-burning AI startups reliant on Nvidia's capital support. If this credit mechanism unravels, software developers unable to achieve independent profitability face severe solvency pressure. Meanwhile, rival chipmakers are moving aggressively to exploit the opening:

  • AMD & Broadcom: Accelerating production timelines and pitching enterprise customers seeking to hedge against Nvidia's supply uncertainty.
  • Google: Pushing its proprietary Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) to cloud hyperscalers looking to reduce single-vendor dependency.

The Asian Shockwave: South Korea’s $510 Billion Joint Venture in Limbo

The fallout from Santa Clara has hit South Korea with acute force. Prior to the scandal, South Korean tech initiatives were heavily intertwined with Nvidia, headlined by a prospective $510 billion joint AI project designed to establish regional processing infrastructure. Huang’s unexpected exit has plunged this nationwide initiative into deep uncertainty, forcing state officials and corporate executives to reassess their commitments.

In response, the Presidential Committee on National AI Strategy under President Lee Jae-myung’s administration convened an emergency meeting to review supply chain risks and project continuity. High-level government officials and industry experts gathered to evaluate potential disruptions to hardware allocation schedules, but concrete solutions remain elusive given the leadership vacuum in Silicon Valley. The episode has laid bare the geopolitical risk of anchoring national technology strategies to a single corporate figure.

South Korea’s semiconductor titans, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, are facing an immediate test of their strategic resilience. Both companies serve as vital suppliers of High Bandwidth Memory (HBM)—the ultra-fast memory chips essential for Nvidia's AI accelerators. Under Huang, the supply partnership between South Korean memory makers and Nvidia was exceptionally tight. Now, with Nvidia's purchasing timelines disrupted and leadership shifting toward spending restraint, Samsung and SK Hynix face revenue exposure, intensifying their push to diversify their client bases and build standalone technological self-reliance.

Compounding these operational headaches is a renewed geopolitical flare-up between Washington and Beijing. Following Huang’s resignation, critics in the U.S. political sphere alleged that Nvidia’s oversight regarding semiconductor export restrictions to China had been overly lax during his tenure. As Washington signals tighter enforcement and potential regulatory crackdowns, Chinese trade officials responded by threatening retaliatory export controls on critical rare-earth elements. What began as an executive misconduct scandal in Silicon Valley has quickly amplified resource-nationalism rhetoric between the world's two largest economies.

What to Watch Next

As the market absorbs the loss of Nvidia's long-time leader, several key developments will determine whether the semiconductor landscape stabilizes or fractures further:

  • The CFO Transition: Watch whether Colette Kress secures the permanent CEO title and how quickly she adjusts Nvidia’s capital expenditure and aggressive R&D allocations.
  • South Korea's Policy Response: Monitor updates from the Lee Jae-myung administration's Presidential Committee on National AI Strategy regarding the restructuring or downsizing of the $510 billion joint AI initiative.
  • HBM Memory Realignment: Track how Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix reallocate their HBM production capacity to competing accelerator platforms like AMD and Google TPUs.
  • U.S.-China Tech Sanctions: Observe whether U.S. regulators formally launch investigations into Nvidia’s historic trade compliance, and whether Beijing enacts concrete export restrictions on rare-earth materials.
