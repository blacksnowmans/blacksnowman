The Collapse of a Cabinet Nominee: Why South Korea’s Progressive Bloc is Turning on Itself

September 13, 2026

The South Korean political landscape currently feels like a chaotic ecosystem, trapped in a cycle of internecine warfare. Despite a change in government, the prevailing atmosphere remains dominated by the same polarized tribalism that has plagued the country for years. The recent, swift resignation of Yong Hye-in, the nominee for Minister of Gender Equality and Family, serves as a poignant window into the structural crises facing President Lee Jae-myung’s administration. This was not a one-off mishap; it was a symptom of a deeper rot, highlighted by a bitter internal revolt and the emergence of a stinging new political term: “mercenary critics.”

The Fourteen-Day Downfall: A Symptom of Systemic Failure

Yong Hye-in, a figure from a minor progressive party who was tapped for the cabinet, resigned just two weeks after her nomination. While she framed her departure at the National Assembly’s press center as a "decision to uphold the spirit of solidarity within the progressive bloc," the reality was far more pragmatic. Facing intense pressure from the Democratic Party of Korea (the main opposition party, now the ruling power under Lee), which signaled that her attempt to hold both her seat as a proportional representative and her cabinet post was politically untenable, she was forced to step down before even facing a confirmation hearing.

The writing had been on the wall from the start. In those two weeks, a cascade of scandals emerged: questions regarding her dual roles, controversies over crowdfunding for her honeymoon, allegations of speculative real estate trading in Ansan, unauthorized use of VIP facilities at Gimpo Airport during family travel, and issues regarding the use of research grants. The list of liabilities was so exhaustive that even the ruling party found it impossible to shield her.

Yong’s downfall marks the fourth cabinet nominee to fail in the first 15 months of the Lee Jae-myung administration. All four—Lee Jin-sook, Kang Sun-woo, Lee Hye-eun, and now Yong—are women, fueling a growing debate about the inadequacy of the administration’s vetting system. More importantly, Yong’s exit exposed the widening rift within the progressive movement. In her final remarks, she lamented that "the words shaking us from within hurt more than the enemy outside," citing online vitriol directed at prominent progressive voices like writer Rhyu Si-min. When the President himself was found to have retweeted a post targeting Rhyu, the internal hegemony struggle—the fight for control over the party and the administration’s soul—burst into the open.

The Speed of Decline: Comparing Administrations

Statistically, the Lee administration’s personnel struggles are unprecedented. Losing four nominees in 15 months far outpaces the early records of the previous Moon Jae-in (three) or Yoon Suk-yeol, Park Geun-hye, and Lee Myung-bak administrations (two each). While all governments face teething pains, the speed of this collapse is alarming. Unlike previous nominees who had weeks to defend themselves during formal hearings, Yong was effectively neutralized by her own side within a fortnight.

The reason is clear: the administration’s once-robust approval ratings have dipped to record lows. The government no longer has the political capital to withstand controversy. In the past, they might have pushed a nominee through a hearing, but today, even the "concrete" core supporters are standing with their arms crossed in skepticism. As popular media critic Kim Ou-joon has noted, the problem is not a loss of moderates; it is a cooling of the core base. When the base stops defending the administration, even minor issues are amplified into political tsunamis.

🔥 Self-Destructive Conflict Driven by Poor Vetting

President Lee’s personnel style remains doggedly insular. The administration repeatedly insists on nominees who fall far short of public standards, only abandoning them when public opinion becomes toxic. Despite promises of innovation and unity, the result has been a focus on protecting "my people" rather than national governance.

Rhyu Si-min and the Rise of 'Mercenary Critics'

At the heart of this turmoil is a public clash between President Lee and Rhyu Si-min, a highly influential intellectual figure within the progressive movement. Rhyu recently criticized President Lee’s governance as resembling a "monarchy or aristocracy" rather than a republic, pointing out that the President exercises excessive influence over party leadership. Rhyu warned that this arrogance is alienating the public and that the administration is rapidly losing its way.

The administration’s reaction was not one of reflection, but of hostility. Pro-Lee figures and online influencers immediately branded Rhyu a traitor, painting his calls for more thoughtful, serious criticism as "internal stabbing." This signals a total paralysis of healthy debate within the progressive bloc. The movement that once rose to power by criticizing the arrogance of the conservative right now seems to be mirroring the very traits it once condemned.

Rhyu coined a term for this phenomenon: “mercenary criticism” (yong-yeok-bi-pyeong). He argues that the media environment is skewed because a group of insiders—failed local candidates, ambitious young political aspirants, and party officials—constantly supply "energy" to the system. These people do not offer genuine critique; they offer "mercenary services." They attack anyone who disagrees with the President in hopes of securing a position or favor. They are, in Rhyu’s view, the political equivalent of "juvenile delinquents" who enjoy the protection of the authorities while shirking all responsibility for their words.

What to Watch Next

The core issue is that the Lee administration has become allergic to dissent. By purging those who offer "bitter medicine" and surrounding itself with "mercenary critics" who only provide praise, the government is isolating itself from reality.

Looking ahead, watch for these three indicators:

  • Cabinet Reshuffle: Will the President replace his gatekeepers, or will he continue to prioritize loyalty over competence?
  • Core Supporter Sentiment: Keep an eye on progressive news channels and community boards. If the "arms-crossed" skepticism turns into open hostility, the administration will lose its last line of defense.
  • Local Election Preparations: The next regional elections will be the ultimate referendum on whether the "mercenary" strategy can hold against a dissatisfied public.

If the administration continues to view every critique as an act of treason, it will not only fail to implement its reforms—it may find that it has burnt the very foundation of the progressive movement it sought to lead.
