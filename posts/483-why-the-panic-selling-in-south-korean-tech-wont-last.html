Why the Panic Selling in South Korean Tech Won’t Last

September 09, 2026

There is a recurring scene in the Korean stock market that feels almost scripted. When the market dips, the Fear of Missing Out (FOMO) kicks in, and when it finally rebounds, investors who just sold their holdings at the bottom are left staring at a mirage. In a recent appearance on "3ProTV," a prominent Korean financial analysis channel, market analyst Ha Chang-wan addressed the psychological toll this takes on individual investors. He deconstructed the "panic at 2:00 PM"—a common phenomenon where the market tends to bleed in the afternoon—and urged investors to look past the day-to-day noise to identify where the market is actually heading.

Recent volatility was sparked by Houthi attacks on Saudi oil facilities and renewed fears regarding the "yen carry trade," where investors borrow low-interest Japanese yen to buy higher-yield assets. But do these geopolitical risks truly undermine the fundamentals of a massive industry like semiconductors? The answer is a resounding no. In fact, the current trend of foreign and institutional investors quietly accumulating stock while retail investors panic-sell is a significant signal that the market is preparing for a shift. It is time to stop fixating on daily fluctuations and start reading the structural narrative.

Geopolitical Noise vs. Industrial Reality

When the stock market slips, there is always a convenient headline to blame. The recent afternoon sell-off was pinned on Middle Eastern instability and the unwinding of the yen carry trade. However, analysts suggest that the current market environment is fundamentally different from the "Black August" of 2024. In that crash, a surprise interest rate hike by the Bank of Japan (BOJ) and weak U.S. employment data caused the Japan-U.S. interest rate gap to narrow by 1% in just three months, triggering a massive sell-off in the Nikkei 225 and Korea’s own KOSPI and KOSDAQ indices.

Today, the speculative short positions on the yen are roughly half of what they were during that previous crisis, and the possibility of further rate hikes by the BOJ in September is already fully priced into the market. Even after the geopolitical tension spiked, semiconductor stocks showed resilience, proving that the market’s internal health is much stronger than it appears. As Ha argues, temporary geopolitical shocks cannot erode the core value of a booming industry; instead, they should be viewed as buying opportunities.

The Undeniable Strength of Memory Chips

When the market shakes, looking at the performance of heavyweights like SK Hynix (a global leader in high-bandwidth memory) and Samsung Electronics (the world’s largest memory chipmaker) reveals who is truly in charge. Despite concerns that SK Hynix would fall back into a stagnant trading range, the stock has repeatedly tested and broken through previous resistance levels.

The supply-demand data is even more compelling. According to market intelligence firm TrendForce, the average inventory level for global DRAM manufacturers heading into the final quarter of the year is just 2.7 weeks. For companies like SK Hynix and Micron, that number is closer to a razor-thin two weeks. Samsung Electronics is holding about four weeks of inventory, still well below the "stable" range of six to eight weeks. Because global big tech companies—NVIDIA, Google, and Microsoft—are effectively lining up to secure memory for their AI data centers, chips are being shipped as fast as they are produced. In this environment, panic-selling is illogical; if you believe in the long-term AI demand, these dips are effectively the last "clearance sale" before the next leg up.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • Geopolitical risks like Houthi attacks are short-term noise already factored into prices.
  • Global DRAM inventory is at an all-time low, creating a "bidding war" among AI tech giants.
  • Fundamentals for Korean chip giants remain rock-solid; retail panic-selling only serves the "smart money."

Why Retail Selling is the Ultimate "Buy" Signal

The market’s flow of funds often reveals a simple truth: when individual investors, exhausted by volatility, dump their shares, foreign and institutional investors are there to scoop them up. In recent weeks, as billions of dollars (translated from billions of won) left the market, retail investors suffered significant losses. However, analysts interpret this exit as a positive sign. Because retail investors often move based on emotion, their departure "lightens the load" for the market. Once the shares are in stronger, more strategic hands, the stock is free to rise much faster when external variables stabilize.

The crucial difference lies in strategy. Many retail investors engage in "water-down" (or "averaging down")—buying more as a stock falls until they run out of cash, leaving them helpless when the price drops further. True "fire-building" (or aggressive buying) involves adding to positions based on the conviction that a company's fundamentals remain unchanged despite market noise. Those who lack a clear asset allocation plan are the ones who consistently fall into the trap of selling low and trying to chase the rebound high.

Watching the Rotation: Infrastructure and Energy

As the semiconductor giants catch their breath, market energy is rotating toward sectors supported by policy and concrete demand. The explosion of AI data centers has created an unavoidable energy crisis, making nuclear power, power grid equipment, and optical communications sectors the new leaders.

Companies like Gaon Cable, KEPCO E&C (the engineering arm of the state-run power company), and Hyundai Engineering & Construction are seeing sustained interest. Similarly, the "small and mid-cap" (SoBuJang) sector—which focuses on materials, parts, and equipment—is showing signs of a strong comeback, particularly in glass substrates and semiconductor packaging.

Ultimately, the market is a relay race. When the big tech stocks rest, the infrastructure and component sectors take the baton. For the investor, the goal is not to predict the next single-day gain, but to hold a position in an industry with a clear, upward-trending horizon. As the major obstacles that have weighed on the market since the 9,000-point level begin to dissipate, those who maintained their conviction will be the ones reaping the rewards. The market will always have waves—the question is whether you will be the one drowning in them, or the one using them to move forward.
