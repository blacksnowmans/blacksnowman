A Scandalous Notepad Leak Exposes South Korea's Cutthroat Ruling-Party Power Struggles

September 10, 2026

Picture the scene inside the grand, high-ceilinged chamber of the National Assembly in Seoul. Lawmakers sit in rows, cameras zoom in on solemn faces, and the day's legislative business unfolds under the weight of national expectation. Yet, behind the veneer of parliamentary dignity, a high-stakes psychological game is being played out in plain sight. In South Korean politics, few things are ever truly accidental — a reality underlined when a stray camera lens recently caught a glimpse of a senior politician's open notepad, laying bare the raw, unfiltered mechanics of ambition and factional warfare.

The incident centers on Kim Min-seok, a senior figure and representative of the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country's primary liberal opposition party. During a plenary session, Kim was photographed holding a notepad bearing striking scribbles: targeted slogans directed at hardline supporters, and the name of a major internal rival floated as a potential candidate for the upcoming Seoul mayoral race. What began as a visual gaffe quickly detonated into a full-scale political crisis, offering foreign observers a rare, unvarnished look at the cynical calculations that drive Korean party politics behind closed doors.

The Notepad Leak: Calculated Stunt or Clumsy Accident?

To understand the weight of the leaked notes, one must look closely at how the political establishment reacted. Shin Joo-ho, a former senior spokesperson for the conservative People Power Party (PPP)—South Korea's main conservative political force—appeared on a local radio broadcast to dissect the memo. Shin argued with absolute conviction that the exposure of the notepad was no mistake. He pointed out that while the rest of the pages were curiously sparse, the page bearing the name of potential Seoul mayoral contenders and harsh phrases aimed at political opponents was positioned precisely for maximum camera visibility.

Political analysts have dubbed this a classic "two birds with one stone" strategy. On one side of the ledger, the notes featured aggressive rallying cries directed at the party's fervent base. On the other, floating the name of a prominent internal heavyweight signaled a calculated bid to project leadership clout. Whether the public viewed the memo as an aggressive stance against political rivals or a bold vision for local elections, the leadership stood to gain visibility either way. While Kim's camp scrambled to dismiss the incident as an oversight, public skepticism remains high. In a high-security legislative environment where aides meticulously manage optics, political notebooks do not simply fall open to sensitive strategy pages by sheer coincidence.

The notes also targeted high-profile figures from the conservative bloc, including Han Dong-hoon, a prominent conservative lawmaker and former justice minister, alongside senior official Lee Jin-suk, with the stark demand to "out" them. Han fired back publicly via social media, questioning whether the DPK leadership intended to push for formal expulsions. The episode revealed a leadership under pressure, resorting to extreme rhetoric on paper to appease an impatient, vocal base when legislative momentum faltered.

Internal Friction and the Trap of the Seoul Mayoral Race

The fallout from the memo quickly triggered an explosive reaction from one of the DPK's most formidable internal heavyweights: Jung Cheong-rae. Jung's name had appeared in Kim’s notes as a prospective candidate for the Seoul mayoral election—a prospect that drew an immediate, furious public rebuke rather than gratitude.

Jung took to social media to vent his frustration, asking pointedly why leadership figures continued to target him even after the conclusion of internal party leadership races. The public clash exposed a fracture within the DPK that has passed the point of easy repair. Jung correctly interpreted his inclusion on the notepad not as an honor, but as a political trap. Given the current political terrain and struggling approval ratings, the upcoming Seoul mayoral race is a notoriously difficult battleground for the liberal camp.

Pushing a major party heavyweight like Jung into a difficult mayoral race looks less like a bid for victory and more like a tactical maneuver to neutralize a political rival. By drafting Jung out of his secure local constituency in Mapo, Seoul, to fight an uphill mayoral battle, party leaders could weaken his political standing while clearing the way to install a loyalist in his former district. It is a textbook example of factional engineering, designed to reshape internal party geography under the guise of civic duty.

Reshuffling the Ranks and Sideline Purges

The notebook controversy is merely a symptom of a much broader, systematic consolidation of power currently underway within the DPK. Observers note that the sudden return of Choi Jae-sung—a former three-term lawmaker who had previously stepped away from frontline politics—and his appointment as a special advisor under Kim Min-seok follows a similar strategic logic.

In modern Korean politics, such returns are rarely accidental. Choi's maneuvering within local networks and the marginalization of figures aligned with rival factions point to a calculated effort to build a dedicated loyalist framework. While the party publicly champions reform and unity, the day-to-day reality of party management increasingly prioritizes internal alignment and the systematic sidelining of potential challengers.

Critics argue that this inward-looking factionalism distracts the party from broader governance and legislative goals. Even as the DPK commands substantial legislative leverage, public frustration mounts when political energy is diverted into internal turf wars rather than pressing economic and social issues.

What to Watch Next

As the dust settles on the notepad scandal, the political landscape in Seoul is bracing for several critical developments:

  • Factional Showdowns: Keep an eye on how Jung Cheong-rae and his allies respond to ongoing pressures regarding regional nominations and candidate selections ahead of upcoming local elections.
  • Mayoral Candidate Dynamics: Watch whether the push to draft high-profile figures into difficult mayoral races gains traction or collapses under the weight of internal resistance.
  • Leadership Stability: Monitor Kim Min-seok's standing within the DPK; further missteps or perceived overreaches could trigger formal demands from rank-and-file members for emergency leadership overhauls.

Ultimately, the notebook leak serves as a vivid reminder of the ruthless undercurrents in South Korean politics. When public office becomes a canvas for tactical maneuvering rather than public service, even a simple slip of the pen can trigger a political earthquake.
