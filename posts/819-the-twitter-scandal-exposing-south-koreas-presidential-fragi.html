The Twitter Scandal Exposing South Korea’s Presidential Fragility

September 19, 2026

Watching South Korean politics lately has become an exercise in frustration. Just as the administration of President Lee Jae-myung—the leader of the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the primary liberal opposition-turned-governing force—sinks into a period of historic unpopularity, a single social media activity has set the political arena ablaze. It was an impulsive flick of a finger, but it has spiraled into an unprecedented crisis that highlights the deep, uncomfortable fractures within the current government.

The incident is simple yet damning: while President Lee spent his daylight hours offering public apologies for his administration’s lack of communication and sensitivity, he spent his night on social media "retweeting" content from fringe online communities. These were not mere links; they were attacks on his own party members and former political allies, using derogatory language against critics and even targeting former President Moon Jae-in. This glaring contradiction—the humble public servant by day and the vengeful partisan by night—has left even his staunchest supporters asking: Who is truly running the country, and what is their vision for its future?

The Contradiction of Apologies and Digital Vendettas

The controversy broke just as Lee’s administration hit a "dead cross"—a critical juncture where negative public opinion ratings (48%) surpassed his approval ratings (43%) for the first time since his inauguration, according to the National Barometer Survey (NBS). A separate poll by Gallup Korea confirmed the shift, showing disapproval ratings exceeding 50% for the first time. In response, the President went on record, bowing his head and admitting, "I have been lacking in consideration, delicacy, and communication."

However, the sincerity of that apology evaporated within hours. By retweeting posts that viciously attacked key figures like political analyst Park Si-young and Governor Choo Mi-ae, and by sharing content that labeled moderate party figures as part of "Teol-cheonji" (a derogatory term implying a cult-like fanaticism surrounding specific political figures), the President signaled that his apology was likely nothing more than a tactical performance. The President’s own comment on one of these retweets—"This is very comforting. Let's reform more so we can have a worse world..."—revealed a leader more interested in the validation of radical echo chambers than in the difficult work of governance.

Externalizing Blame: A Classic Path to Failure

The warning signs from the public are clear. Polls indicate that frustration over real estate policy is the primary driver of disapproval, followed by significant dissatisfaction with controversial cabinet nominations like Minister of Justice candidate Kim Seung-won and Gender Equality Minister candidate Ryu Ho-jeong. Despite these specific, policy-oriented grievances, the President appears convinced that his plummeting numbers are not the result of his own policy failures or "arrogant" communication style, but rather the result of "incitement" by internal critics and media figures like Rhyu Si-min.

In a recent press conference, Lee dismissed criticism of his stance on prosecution reform as mere attacks by political adversaries. However, when prominent party stalwarts like Jung Chung-rae and former DPK leader Cho Kuk begin warning that the "cleansing" of internal dissenters will lead to a catastrophe for the governing coalition, it is a sign that the rot is internal. By treating genuine feedback as "internal strife," the President is effectively insulating himself from the very reality he needs to manage to remain viable.

The ‘Theory of Total Defeat’ and the Fading Coalition

Rhyu Si-min, a high-profile writer and liberal intellectual, recently offered a stinging critique, warning that "there is no such thing as eternal comradeship." He compared Lee’s heavy-handed interference in party affairs—specifically the process of selecting party leaders—to the behavior of a monarch or a nobility-led regime. Rhyu warned that just as the previous conservative administration disintegrated after driving out moderate voices, President Lee is effectively dismantling his own governing coalition.

The concern is that the administration is trapping itself in a "fortress of the faithful." By catering only to the most extreme segment of his base, Lee is alienating the centrist voters who were essential to his electoral victory. The current situation echoes the lyrics of a song Rhyu suggested for the Blue House (the South Korean presidential office): "Bust Your Windows" by Jazmine Sullivan. It captures the betrayal felt by a base that poured their hearts into creating this government, only to see their trust squandered by a leader who plays with their political capital for the sake of petty, digital retribution.

A Call for Reality: Moving Beyond Blind Loyalty

The time has come for the President’s supporters to face a cold truth: this administration, in its current state, is on a trajectory of inevitable failure. The tendency to wrap every policy and personality in unconditional support has only emboldened the President’s worst impulses. To save the progressive agenda, those who believe in reform must move away from blind loyalty toward independent critical thought.

The "dead cross" in the polls is not a temporary blip; it is an accumulation of deep-seated distrust. If the President’s inner circle continues to focus on "hunting down internal traitors" rather than addressing the economic and social anxieties of the public, the administration will descend into a prolonged "lame duck" phase far earlier than anyone anticipated. History suggests that regimes that ignore the ocean of public sentiment to build walls of partisan purity eventually find themselves alone when the tide turns.

What to watch next: Keep an eye on the upcoming parliamentary sessions and the President’s response to further cabinet confirmation hearings. If Lee continues to prioritize the endorsement of radical online voices over legislative compromise, expect a further erosion of support among independent voters—the group that will ultimately decide whether his administration recovers or remains a historical cautionary tale of self-inflicted decline.
