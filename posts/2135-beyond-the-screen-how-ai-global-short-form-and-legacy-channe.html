Beyond the Screen: How AI, Global Short-Form, and Legacy Channels Are Reshaping Korean Live Commerce

November 09, 2026

Tune into a South Korean live shopping stream late on a Tuesday evening, and the boundaries between traditional retail, digital broadcast, and automation blur almost entirely. In the middle of the night, artificial intelligence tools field inbound viewer queries and recommend personalized product bundles on the fly, while virtual hosts step in during off-peak hours to keep the checkout carts rolling. Meanwhile, behind the bright studio lighting and seamless mobile interfaces, South Korea's retail powerhouses are fighting an existential battle over where consumers will spend their money next—and how much it will cost to keep them watching.

For years, South Korea has served as an advanced test bed for video-driven retail, commonly known as live commerce. What began as an agile, smartphone-based evolution of traditional TV home shopping networks has now entered a far more complex phase. Domestic demand has tightened amid high inflation, consumers have become intensely discerning, and cut-rate discount tactics no longer guarantee loyalty. Retailers are realizing that survival requires rethinking everything from legacy broadcast operations and artificial intelligence integration to high-stakes overseas expansion on platforms like Amazon and TikTok Shop.

The Home Shopping Divide: Legacy Cash Cows vs. Mobile Acceleration

To understand the current tension in Korean retail, one must look at the country's historic home shopping conglomerates. For decades, companies like GS Shop (operated by retail group GS Retail) and CJ OnStyle (the commerce arm of entertainment and media giant CJ ENM) dominated living room consumer spending. Today, their divergent corporate strategies have produced sharply contrasting financial fortunes.

Recent earnings reveal a surprising lesson: abandoning traditional television too quickly carries heavy risks. While GS Shop chose to reinforce its core linear television broadcasting—focusing on operational efficiency and defending its traditional broadcast base—it managed to defend its bottom line despite persistent macroeconomic headwinds. In contrast, CJ OnStyle embarked on an aggressive "de-TV" push, rapidly reallocating capital toward mobile feeds, short-form video content, and smartphone-native live commerce. Yet that transition has proved financially punishing in the short term, dampened by heavy early marketing outlays and the gap between viewer acquisition costs and immediate cart checkouts.

The structural reality facing these giants is demographic. South Korea's demographic cohort aged 50 and older—often referred to locally as the 5060 generation—holds substantial collective purchasing power, and their habitual viewing remains anchored to traditional cable and IPTV channels. Abandoning linear broadcasts without an airtight omnichannel funnel risks ditching steady cash-generating operations before mobile live commerce achieves sustainable profitability. The emergent consensus among industry strategists is that pure-play "de-TV" is too volatile; the winning formula requires a hybrid model where TV maintains cash flow while modular short-form clips pull older, wealthier buyers gradually into mobile apps.

Corporate reshuffles reflect this drive toward operational discipline. At retail conglomerate Shinsegae Group, veteran executive Lee Seok-goo—who previously led Shinsegae Live Shopping, Starbucks Coffee Korea, and the group's hospitality arm, Chosun Hotels & Resorts—has been reassigned to anchor the department store division as the group prepares for legal affiliate separation. The move signals a concerted effort to graft live-shopping broadcast methodologies directly into physical department store retail, using curated luxury live feeds to build consumer fandom and stabilize revenue without relying on sheer discounting.

Looking Abroad: K-Beauty Eyes Amazon and TikTok Shop

With domestic market saturation looming, Korean commerce operators are setting their sights overseas. The most prominent example is Kurly, the pioneer of South Korea's dawn grocery delivery service, which is now leveraging its proprietary private-label (PB) cosmetics brand to make an aggressive dual entrance onto Amazon and TikTok Shop.

The push represents a tactical leap. Rather than relying solely on traditional search-based e-commerce listings, Kurly's embrace of TikTok Shop targets North American and Southeast Asian younger demographics through social live commerce. Discovery-driven short-form video turns passive scrolling into immediate checkout impulses, with user engagement times on discovery-based shopping apps running an estimated 2.5 times longer than on standard catalog-style storefronts. By pairing Amazon's established cross-border logistics backbone with TikTok's viral broadcast algorithms, Korean brands aim to insulate themselves from domestic economic stagnation.

Simultaneously, media titan CJ ENM is restructuring its commercial apparatus into an integrated advertising and content pipeline. Rather than treating shopping broadcasts as isolated infomercials, the strategy embeds interactive live commerce directly into entertainment programming, enabling global viewers to purchase featured Korean lifestyle goods with minimal clicks. This pivot coincides with wider international shifts. Analysts watching the trajectory of major retail events like China's Singles' Day (Guanggun Jie) note that the era of unsustainable discount wars and predatory dumping is giving way to operational sophistication, genuine brand narratives, and real-time customer service. For Korean exporters, the goal is no longer winning a race to the bottom on price, but delivering a polished broadcast experience backed by verified product quality.

Algorithmic Commerce from Studios to Smart Farms

Underpinning this operational evolution is the aggressive deployment of artificial intelligence. In South Korean live commerce, AI has moved far past automated chatbots. Platforms are rolling out real-time recommendation engines that bypass traditional search bars altogether, interpreting viewer behavioral history and viewing habits to surface personalized broadcast channels and custom product bundles that drive conversion rates higher.

Behind the camera, automated infrastructure is actively lowering overhead. Natural language processing tools sift through thousands of real-time viewer comments in live chat feeds, instantaneously flagging the most critical sizing, ingredient, or shipping inquiries directly onto the host's teleprompter. During late-night and dawn hours, synthetic virtual show hosts maintain broadcast continuity without requiring studio crews, ensuring that platforms capture off-peak purchases that previously went unserviced.

Importantly, this technological shift is not restricted to Seoul corporate studios; it has penetrated primary industries across provincial South Korea. In Seongju, North Gyeongsang Province—a region renowned for fruit production—grower Kim Mi-jeong recently earned the official title of "Agricultural Meister" by integrating smart-farm AI climate automation with direct-to-consumer mobile streaming. By using smart-farm sensors to stabilize crop consistency and presenting real-time freshness directly to urban shoppers over platforms like Naver Shopping Live, regional producers are bypassing complex wholesale distribution layers. Consumers no longer rely on static packaging labels; they evaluate harvests on-screen, chatting directly with growers before placing orders.

The Squeeze Behind the Spotlight: 34% Take Rates and Seller Margins

Yet the technical brilliance of contemporary live commerce conceals severe friction across the merchant supply chain. While seasonal promotions generate eye-catching gross merchandise volumes—such as recent early-bird winter apparel broadcasts by outdoor brands like National Geographic Kids, Shinsegae Men's Collection, and Guy Laroche—small and mid-sized merchants face punishing platform fees.

Commission and platform fees across certain domestic and international e-commerce channels have climbed past 34 percent of order value. When sellers factor in obligatory shipping overhead, digital marketing costs, agency fees, and studio production expenses, top-line sales figures often yield zero operating profit. High commission structures threaten to undermine the entire merchant ecosystem, forcing suppliers to either compromise on raw material quality or increase baseline retail prices to stay solvent.

The Merchant Burden: When platform commissions surpass 34 percent before logistics and production expenses are deducted, high-volume live broadcasts risk becoming empty vanity metrics rather than sustainable commercial engines for independent producers.

This dynamic has prompted a growing push toward direct-to-consumer (D2C) channels and regional cooperative platforms. To survive, smaller merchants are attempting to build proprietary live capabilities on their own websites, diversifying across lower-commission regional services rather than remaining beholden to dominant digital landlords. Without structural adjustments to platform take rates, the rapid technological growth of live commerce risks hollowing out the very merchant base that produces the products.

What to Watch Next

As South Korea's retail landscape moves toward its next operational cycle, several concrete indicators will show how these tensions resolve:

  • Home Shopping Channel Re-allocations: Watch upcoming quarterly filings from CJ ENM and GS Retail to see whether CJ modifies its aggressive TV divestment pace, and whether GS can maintain operating margins once broadcast transmission fees (paid to pay-TV providers) are renegotiated.
  • TikTok Shop Conversion Metrics for K-Beauty: Monitor Kurly's international sales milestones on TikTok Shop and Amazon throughout upcoming seasonal promotional windows, particularly regarding return rates and short-form viewer-to-buyer conversion ratios in North America.
  • Platform Commission Scrutiny: Track whether regulatory discussions emerge within the Ministry of SMEs and Startups or the Fair Trade Commission regarding fee transparency, as independent seller associations push back against aggregate platform takes exceeding 30 percent.
  • Synthetic Host Retention Rates: Observe viewer retention curves on Naver and independent commerce apps during midnight-to-6:00 a.m. slots utilizing virtual AI hosts, testing whether automated streaming converts into actual repeat sales or merely serves as placeholder novelty.
