The Erosion of Truth: How South Korea’s Progressive Sphere Turned on Itself

October 06, 2026

If you have been monitoring South Korean political YouTube channels lately, you might find yourself baffled by a scene that defies all logic. The once-robust tradition of debate within the progressive camp—a movement built on challenging authority and championing policy—seems to have vanished. In its place, we see a rise in vitriolic, internal infighting that mimics the very tactics used by the far-right. The recent controversy surrounding the YouTube channel Genre-man Yeouido (a program focusing on political commentary) and the baseless attacks on attorney Lee Jong-geun, husband of lawmaker Park Eun-jung, is a sobering window into this decline.

The False Accusations Against Attorney Lee Jong-geun

The controversy centers on remarks made by attorney Roh Young-hee during an appearance on Genre-man Yeouido. Roh claimed that in November 2020, while Lee Jong-geun was a prosecutor, he was the mastermind behind the decision to clear prosecutors involved in the “Lime Asset Management scandal” of drinking and bribery allegations without a proper investigation. She further alleged that he intentionally suppressed a complaint regarding the obstruction of a search warrant execution against former prosecutor Han Dong-hoon.

However, these claims are not just distortions; they are chronologically impossible. The decision not to indict the prosecutors involved in the Lime scandal was made on December 8, 2020, by the Seoul Southern District Prosecutors’ Office. By that time, Lee had already been serving as the head of the Supreme Prosecutors’ Office’s Criminal Department since August 7, 2020. Because the Lime case did not fall under the jurisdiction of the Criminal Department, it was factually impossible for Lee to have been in the chain of command, let alone the "mastermind" of the decision.

Attorney Lee’s side has responded firmly, stating, "Lee Jong-geun was already serving as the head of the Criminal Department at the Supreme Prosecutors’ Office by August 7, 2020; he had no involvement in the non-indictment of the prosecutors in the Lime scandal, nor did he have the authority to oversee it as it was not a Criminal Department case." Regarding the Han Dong-hoon warrant incident, it was the Seoul High Prosecutors’ Office that investigated and indicted the prosecutor involved, not Lee. The lack of basic fact-checking in such high-profile broadcasts raises a chilling question: what is the true motive behind these irresponsible, defamatory outbursts?

From Independent Voice to Partisan Echo Chamber

The transformation of attorney Roh Young-hee is perhaps the most striking aspect of this saga. Years ago, Roh was known as a principled commentator who was unafraid to speak truth to power, regardless of the political camp. When President Lee Jae-myung—then a political figure—posted excessively on social media, it was Roh who criticized him, warning that "too much is as bad as too little." She was also a vocal critic of the practice of appointing former prosecutors or lawyers from Kim & Chang, South Korea’s largest law firm, to senior government positions.

Back then, she was respected for maintaining a balanced perspective. However, that changed after she received what she described as a "warning" from a powerful figure in the political establishment. According to her own public remarks, she received a phone call from a high-ranking official—widely speculated to be Prime Minister Kim Min-seok—who warned her, "Young-hee, you’re going to get hurt."

Instead of standing her ground, Roh seemed to pivot. The critique of "Kim & Chang" appointees vanished. In its place came a complete alignment with the mainstream of the Democratic Party of Korea (the current ruling party). Critics now mockingly suggest she is chasing "extra points" or a government position by acting as a partisan mouthpiece. This shift highlights a depressing reality: in the current landscape, it is far more profitable for media personalities to fall in line with the power center than to offer the independent, critical analysis they once built their reputations on.

The Rise of "Internal Infighting" and the Targeting of Park Eun-jung

Since President Lee Jae-myung ascended to the highest office, the internal culture of the Democratic Party has shifted. When a governing party gains control of massive resources, the "desire" for influence can corrupt organizational culture. We are seeing a pattern where anyone who does not perfectly align with the party’s current leadership is branded an enemy, and those who "prove their loyalty" by attacking dissenters are rewarded with platform prominence.

The attacks on attorney Lee Jong-geun are a perfect example of this. The goal is to frame Lee as being out of favor with the President, thereby "proving" the loyalty of those launching the attack. It is a form of political self-harm that erodes the moral standing of the entire progressive movement. By adopting the "witch hunt" tactics once used by the ultra-conservative "Ilbe" (a notorious online community) and far-right YouTubers, these progressive voices are systematically destroying the democratic values they once claimed to protect.

This is not advocacy; it is the desperate scramble of hyenas fighting over scraps of power. When the progressive movement trades its intellectual integrity for the short-term thrill of attacking its own, it forfeits its right to claim the moral high ground.

What to Watch Next

As this situation unfolds, there are three key areas to observe:

  • Legal Accountability: Lee Jong-geun has initiated legal action against these false claims. If the courts deliver a heavy verdict, it may serve as a much-needed check on the "cyber-wreckers" (YouTubers who profit from defamation) currently poisoning the discourse.
  • Democratic Party Internal Response: Will the party leadership maintain silence while these internal attacks continue, or will they take steps to curb the toxic behavior of their own "supporter-led" media channels?
  • Public Sentiment: Are the viewers, who have been consuming this vitriol, beginning to tire of the misinformation? A shift in public support away from these extremist channels is the only way to force a return to healthy debate.

The progressive movement in South Korea stands at a crossroads. If it continues to prioritize power and loyalty over truth and justice, it will mirror the very authoritarianism it once fought to replace. The "progressive" label is becoming a hollow shell; restoring its substance will require a painful, but necessary, reckoning with these internal demons.
