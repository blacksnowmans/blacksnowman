A Crisis of Words and a Battle for Accountability: The Democratic Party’s Tightrope Walk

October 20, 2026

The atmosphere inside the Supreme Council chamber of the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) on October 19 was heavy with the weight of both mourning and political damage control. As the ruling party under President Lee Jae-myung, the DPK finds itself in a position of "infinite responsibility," a phrase often used in Korean politics to describe the burden of the party in power. The session began not with a fiery political speech, but with a somber moment of silence for Fire Lieutenant Kim Jin-seok, who lost his life battling a blaze at a factory in Bucheon, a major satellite city west of Seoul.

Yet, this gesture of respect was complicated by a self-inflicted wound. Only days prior, Choo Mi-ae—a heavyweight lawmaker, former Justice Minister, and former Gyeonggi Province Governor—had sparked a firestorm of her own. She used the metaphor of a "straw" to describe how fire service finances were being handled, implying a parasitic relationship with the budget. Coming just before a national tragedy involving a fallen hero, the remark was seen as a callous insult to the very people the state is sworn to protect. For the DPK, the day was defined by a dual mission: scrubbing the stain of Choo’s rhetoric while simultaneously turning the heat on the opposition over a burgeoning scandal in the Seoul Metropolitan Government.

The Choo Mi-ae Controversy and the Politics of Firefighting

In South Korean political discourse, the "slip of the tongue" (seol-hwa) can be fatal. Choo Mi-ae is no stranger to controversy, but her description of fire service funding as a "straw" (ppal-dae)—a term suggesting someone is unfairly sucking resources out of a system—backfired spectacularly. The timing could not have been worse. When the news broke of the Bucheon factory fire and the death of Lieutenant Kim, the public’s grief quickly turned into anger toward Choo’s perceived lack of empathy.

The DPK leadership, recognizing that this could erode the party's moral standing as the ruling power, moved quickly to distance itself. Kim Min-seok, a senior leader within the DPK, took the unusual step of publicly demanding an apology from Choo. During the Supreme Council meeting, Kim emphasized that the party had been urging Choo to apologize since the initial outcry, stating that the priority must be "to work until the hearts of the people are eased." Another prominent lawmaker, Jung Sung-ho, went further, using a traditional Korean idiom to describe the situation: "The mouth is a disaster."

This internal disciplining is about more than just optics. Since President Lee Jae-myung took office, the DPK has sought to frame itself as the party of "livelihood politics" (minsaeng). To achieve this, it must maintain a image of competence and compassion. However, the controversy also highlighted a deeper policy issue: the chronic underfunding and regional disparity in the fire services. In Korea, firefighting was traditionally a local government responsibility, leading to massive gaps in equipment and manpower between wealthy Seoul and poorer provinces. While some steps have been taken to nationalize the service, the DPK used this moment to promise a "limitless state responsibility" for "heroes in uniform." They pledged to bridge the budget gap by significantly increasing national tax support, ensuring that a firefighter's safety does not depend on the tax base of the specific city they serve.

The 'Hangang Bus Gate' and the Attack on Seoul City Hall

While playing defense on the Choo Mi-ae front, the DPK went on a full-scale offensive regarding the "Hangang Bus" project. This is a flagship initiative of Oh Se-hoon, the Mayor of Seoul and a prominent figure in the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition party. The DPK has labeled the controversy "Hangang Bus Gate," a term meant to evoke the gravity of a major corruption scandal.

At the heart of the "Gate" are allegations of illegal rebates and kickbacks. Han Byung-do, the DPK’s floor leader, used a National Assembly audit session to demand answers. According to the DPK, the Seoul Metropolitan Government discovered evidence of rebates worth hundreds of millions of won (several hundred thousand dollars) but intentionally delayed referring the matter to the police for investigation.

"It is incomprehensible that a serious corruption case involving taxpayer money was withheld from a formal investigation," Han Byung-do stated during the audit. "We need to know if Mayor Oh was briefed on this and why the city stayed silent."

The drama intensified when the Seoul Mayor’s Chief of Staff claimed during testimony that he had not briefed the Mayor on the request for a police investigation. To the DPK, this sounds like a classic cover-up. The "Hangang Bus" project involves the deployment of high-speed water taxis and buses on the Han River, a multi-million dollar project that Oh Se-hoon has staked his reputation on. By framing this as a "Gate," the DPK is attempting to paint the PPP-led Seoul administration as opaque and corrupt, mirroring the aggressive audit tactics the opposition used back when the DPK held the mayoralty. For the DPK, hitting Oh Se-hoon is a strategic move to weaken one of the PPP’s most viable future presidential contenders.

Impeachment and the Battle for Judicial Reform

The political theater of October 19 also extended to the judiciary. The Rebuilding Korea Party, a smaller progressive party led by Cho Kuk that often aligns with the DPK, officially unveiled a draft for the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. They are calling on the DPK to join a joint task force to push the impeachment through the National Assembly.

The rationale behind the impeachment push is a perceived lack of impartiality in recent high-profile rulings. Within the DPK, figures like Lee Seong-yun have begun to echo these calls, arguing that the judiciary has lost the public’s trust. This is a high-stakes gamble. Impeaching a Chief Justice is a rare and extreme measure in South Korean history. Critics argue it threatens the separation of powers, while proponents say it is a necessary check on a "runaway" judicial branch that they believe is still influenced by remnants of the previous administration.

Polling cited by the DPK leadership suggests that a majority of the public supports judicial reform, which the party is using to justify its consideration of the impeachment motion. However, the DPK leadership remains somewhat cautious, balancing the desire of its "hardcore" base for a total overhaul of the courts with the need to appear as a stable, governing party to moderate voters. This tension reflects the broader struggle within the DPK: how to be a revolutionary force for reform while managing the day-to-day affairs of the state under the Lee Jae-myung presidency.

Sovereignty, Crypto Taxes, and Urban Safety

The DPK’s agenda for the day wasn’t limited to domestic scandals. On the diplomatic front, Representative Park Sun-won took aim at General Xavier Brunson, the commander of United States Forces Korea (USFK). Park criticized comments made by the General regarding the maritime boundaries and the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ), characterizing them as an infringement on South Korean territorial sovereignty.

While the alliance with the U.S. remains the bedrock of Korean security—especially following the turbulent years of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s administration—the DPK has consistently pushed for a "sovereignty-first" approach. Park’s demand for an apology from the USFK commander serves as a reminder that even under a pro-alliance DPK government, the party will not tolerate what it perceives as American overreach into sensitive territorial matters.

Finally, the party addressed two issues that hit closer to home for the average citizen: cryptocurrency and urban infrastructure.

  • The Crypto Tax: With a new tax on virtual assets set to take effect in about 70 days, the DPK is reviewing the threshold. The current plan imposes a 22% tax on gains exceeding 2.5 million won (about $1,800) per year. Many young investors, a key demographic for the DPK, view this threshold as too low compared to stock market taxes. The party is currently "fine-tuning" its stance to balance fiscal responsibility with the concerns of retail investors.
  • The "Permeable Block" Scandal: Representative Heo Young attacked the Korea Land and Housing Corporation (LH), a state-owned enterprise, over the poor quality of "permeable blocks" used in urban sidewalks. These blocks are designed to absorb rainwater and prevent the flash flooding that has plagued Seoul in recent years. Heo revealed that performance tests were being faked or ignored, putting city dwellers at risk of more "urban drownings" during the monsoon season.

What to Watch Next

The events of October 19 show a party trying to lead while simultaneously fighting off ghosts. The DPK is attempting to maintain the momentum of the Lee Jae-myung presidency by delivering concrete results in "livelihood politics," but it is constantly distracted by internal gaffes like Choo Mi-ae’s and the aggressive push for judicial impeachment.

In the coming weeks, keep a close eye on the following:

  • The Choo Mi-ae Fallout: Will the party take formal disciplinary action, or will the "apology" be enough? If the public remains unsatisfied, it could hurt the DPK’s standing in upcoming local by-elections.
  • The Hangang Bus Investigation: Watch for whether the police launch a formal raid on Seoul City Hall. If evidence of a cover-up by Mayor Oh Se-hoon emerges, it will be the DPK’s biggest political victory of the year.
  • The Impeachment Motion: If the DPK officially adopts the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae as a party platform, expect a total freeze in relations between the executive and judicial branches, potentially leading to a constitutional crisis.
  • Crypto Tax Adjustments: Look for a potential legislative move to raise the tax-free threshold for crypto gains, a clear sign the DPK is worried about losing the "Gen Z" and "Millennial" vote.

The DPK currently holds the levers of power, but as the events of a single day show, the line between a "limitless responsibility" and a "limitless disaster" is incredibly thin.
