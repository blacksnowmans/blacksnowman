Why Donald Trump’s Late-Stage Midterm Meltdown Matters for Seoul and Korean Industry

October 31, 2026

With only a handful of days remaining before the United States midterm elections, the high-stakes battle for political dominance in Washington has taken an extraordinary and erratic turn. At 80 years old, Donald Trump has abruptly cancelled scheduled in-person campaign rallies in crucial conservative bastions, opting instead to retreat behind screens for virtual events. At the same time, furious outbursts against journalists, bizarre claims regarding state voting systems, and aggressive challenges to polling data have signaled a deep sense of instability surrounding his campaign and the White House.

For observers in South Korea, this is far more than a distant political drama unfolding on American television screens. The outcome of these midterms will directly shape Washington’s foreign trade policies, Washington's commitment to industrial subsidies, and the global economic climate in which Korean corporations operate. Under the administration of South Korean President Lee Jae-myung, Seoul is carefully calibrating its diplomatic and economic strategies to navigate whatever shift in power emerges from Washington. To understand the stakes for Korean technology, automotive, and battery sectors, one must look closely at the chaos defining the final stretch of the American campaign trail.

The Strategy of Retreat: Cancelled Rallies and a Shaking Red Wall

The decision to pull back from live, in-person campaign events just three days before voters head to the polls caught political analysts and party insiders by surprise. Normally, the final days before an election demand intense physical presence, direct handshakes, and high-energy rallies aimed at galvanizing the base. Instead, Trump’s campaign announced a sudden pivot to remote, virtual rallies. While campaign representatives defended the move as a strategic effort to reach a broader audience more efficiently, political observers and media outlets suspect physical exhaustion and waning internal momentum played a primary role.

Reports from inside Washington describe a somber and isolated atmosphere within Trump’s inner circle. Surrounded largely by strict loyalists, advisers are allegedly reluctant to deliver candid assessments regarding suburban voter fatigue. Rumors have even surfaced that the leadership spends hours consuming short-form video content rather than reviewing critical briefing papers, highlighting growing concerns over campaign leadership and decision-making.

This organizational drift comes at a perilous time for the Republican base. Texas, which has functioned as an unshakeable "Red Wall" for conservative politics for more than three decades, is showing rare signs of vulnerability. Recent polling indicates tight contests or shifting margins in areas previously considered completely safe. In response to these unsettling figures, Trump rushed to address Texan audiences, offering extreme rhetoric—including controversial proposals such as turning Venezuela into the 51st U.S. state. Rather than reassuring moderate conservative voters who feel exhausted by confrontational politics, such volatile statements risk accelerating the voter defections they were intended to stop.

Personal Attacks and Sabotaging Election Legitimacy

As press scrutiny over the cancelled rallies intensified, tensions boiled over during a formal exchange with reporters. When a journalist pressed Trump on whether his age—80 years old—or health concerns were behind the sudden shift away from live campaigning, the response was an explosive, nearly minute-long personal attack. Trump told the reporter that they were "disgusting," instructed them to "take care of your own body," and launched into physical insults rather than addressing the question logically.

The fallout was swift. The campaign moved to ban the journalist involved from future White House coverage, triggering widespread condemnation from press freedom organizations across the United States. Rather than putting health rumors to rest, the hostile reaction exposed an extreme sensitivity surrounding the candidate's age and physical stamina during a pivotal moment in the race.

This hostile posture toward the media was accompanied by increasingly bizarre public assertions. During one public appearance, Trump claimed that voting ballots in Alaska were so extraordinarily complex that an individual would need an "IQ of 190" to comprehend them. Furthermore, as unfavorable polls continued to circulate, he repeatedly dismissed all negative data as completely "fabricated and manipulated fake news."

More critically, this language lays the groundwork to contest the election results themselves. With fewer than six days remaining, Trump explicitly indicated in interviews and campaign speeches that he would not offer an unconditional acceptance of the outcome. By sowing seeds of doubt regarding the legitimacy of the voting process long before the ballots are fully counted, the campaign has raised fears of a renewed democratic crisis reminiscent of the January 6 Capitol riot. Former President Barack Obama joined the Democratic campaign trail to issue stark warnings, declaring that constitutional democracy itself is at stake, while Republican organizers declared a "Red Scream" emergency to drive conservative turnout to its absolute limit.

What Happens if Democrats Control Congress?

Should the Democratic Party manage to win control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate, the political landscape in Washington will undergo an immediate transformation. Major international outlets, including the BBC, highlight that a Democratic-controlled Congress would launch at least four major congressional investigations targeting Trump’s leadership and personal affairs:

  • Personal Financial Gain: Sweeping inquiries into personal enrichment, corporate holdings, and wealth accumulation during office.
  • Abuse of Power: Detailed hearings examining allegations of executive overreach and political interference with the justice system.
  • The January 6 Insurrection: Formal investigations into direct actions and decisions surrounding the attack on the U.S. Capitol.
  • Classified Documents: Congressional scrutiny regarding the mishandling, unauthorized retention, and leaking of sensitive national security materials.

If these investigations proceed, the executive branch will spend much of its remaining tenure bogged down in subpoenas, public hearings, and potential impeachment proceedings. For foreign allies, a distracted and embattled U.S. legislature creates profound uncertainty, making long-term diplomatic agreements and predictable economic policies far more difficult to secure.

The Stakes for Seoul: Tariffs, Supply Chains, and the Lee Jae-myung Administration

For South Korea, these midterms carry high-stakes implications for national trade strategy and major industrial sectors. Trump has consistently championed aggressive protectionist tariffs, asserting that only companies manufacturing entirely within the United States should receive tax relief and preferential tariff treatment. This stance creates severe challenges for South Korean conglomerates—particularly automakers like Hyundai and Kia, as well as leading electric vehicle battery producers—that have committed billions of dollars to manufacturing facilities in North America.

If protectionist trade measures intensify following the election, Korean firms could face sudden policy shifts, altered subsidy frameworks, or new trade barriers. This places a premium on pragmatic diplomacy from South Korea’s executive branch. President Lee Jae-myung and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea must prepare flexible economic strategies capable of handling sudden shifts in Washington's trade stance.

To many Korean citizens, watching a political leader react to criticism with personal attacks, attack the press, and question democratic processes evokes uncomfortable domestic memories. South Korea experienced its own intense political trauma when former President Yoon Suk-yeol engaged in aggressive rhetoric and uncommunicative governance, a path that ultimately culminated in his impeachment and imprisonment. The memory of that crisis reinforces why Korean voters and policymakers are deeply sensitive to signs of democratic instability abroad. When the leader of the world’s largest economy disregards institutional norms, the resulting volatility destabilizes global markets and international security alliances alike.

What to Watch as the Ballots Are Counted

As the final voting window closes, key signals will reveal how these midterm dynamics will affect international markets and South Korean interests:

  • Congressional Balance of Power: Whether Democrats secure enough seats to initiate high-level congressional investigations or if Republicans hold legislative control.
  • Protectionist Legislation Movement: Early indications of legislative bills targeting foreign manufacturing, electric vehicle supply chains, or clean energy subsidies.
  • Election Acceptance and Post-Election Litigation: The extent to which legal challenges or public protests emerge following the declaration of results.
  • Seoul's Diplomatic Engagement: How President Lee Jae-myung’s administration establishes channels with new congressional committee leaders to safeguard South Korean corporate investments in North America.

The choices made by American voters over the coming days will reshape Washington's internal power balance. For South Korea, keeping a vigilant eye on these changes is not a luxury, but a necessity for economic and geopolitical survival.
