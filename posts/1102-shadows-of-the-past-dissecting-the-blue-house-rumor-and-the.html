Shadows of the Past: Dissecting the "Blue House Rumor" and the Anti-Reform Backlash

October 14, 2026

Despite the recent transition of power to the Lee Jae-myung administration, the corridors of South Korean politics are far from quiet. While the surface appears to be moving toward progress, a familiar, corrosive undercurrent of anonymous "information sheets"—known locally as jjirasi—has begun to circulate. These pamphlets, often disguised as insider tips, are being weaponized to undermine the current government and stifle its reform agenda. At the heart of this latest campaign is a sensationalist rumor alleging that key political influencers are making high-level personnel demands of the Presidential Office.

The Anatomy of a Manufactured Scandal

The rumor, which began circulating around October 13, claims that an influential figure—thinly veiled by the initials "G.O.J."—demanded that the administration appoint specific people to top posts, including Minister of Justice, Prime Minister, Chief of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (SCIA), and Senior Secretary for Civil Affairs. The narrative suggests that when these demands were allegedly rejected, this individual turned against the administration.

To any seasoned observer of Korean politics, this is a textbook example of a "black propaganda" operation. By framing the narrative as a leak from within the Presidential Office, the architects of this rumor hope to manufacture an image of internal conflict and instability. It is a desperate, transparent attempt to weaken the reform momentum during the early stages of the Lee administration. The fact that such low-quality fabrications gain traction highlights a persistent rot in our information ecosystem, where malicious actors prioritize disruption over fact.

Targeting the Voice of Reform

The "G.O.J." mentioned in these documents is clearly intended to be Kim Ou-joon, a prominent political commentator and a major voice for the liberal base. The absurdity of the claim is staggering. Kim is well-known for his refusal to use his proximity to power for personal gain—a reputation that makes the allegation that he is "demanding" cabinet positions laughable to anyone familiar with his track record. Even during the Moon Jae-in administration, Kim famously declined even the most trivial of tokens, such as holiday gift sets from then-presidential secretary Tak Hyun-min.

Why, then, is he being targeted? The answer lies in his willingness to hold the establishment accountable. Kim recently issued sharp critiques of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, suggesting that the judiciary behaves as if "politics is under its control." By challenging the conservative legal establishment—the so-called "vested interest cartel"—Kim has made himself a primary target for those who wish to maintain the status quo. These malicious rumors are not just attacks on a person; they are retaliatory strikes against anyone who dares to challenge the authority of the judiciary and the prosecution.

The Real Stake: The Fate of the SCIA

Beyond the personal attacks, there is a larger, more urgent issue at play: the stability of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (SCIA). The SCIA is the cornerstone of the current administration’s efforts to curb the immense power previously wielded by the prosecution. If the agency is paralyzed by internal strife or public scandal in its infancy, the reform movement will lose its primary engine.

This is exactly what the architects of these rumors are hoping for. If the SCIA fails, it clears the path for the "resurrection of the prosecution." We have already witnessed, during the administration of the now-incarcerated former president, Yoon Seok-youl, how the abuse of investigative authority can dismantle the very systems of a democratic nation. The fear among the administration’s supporters is palpable: if the SCIA is weakened, the country risks sliding back into a period of "prosecutorial dictatorship," where legal power is weaponized to protect political interests.

A Coordinated Campaign Against Independent Voices

This is not a solitary incident, but part of a broader, synchronized effort to silence the voices that support reform. Alongside Kim Ou-joon, public intellectuals like author Rhee Si-min are also being targeted by simultaneous smear campaigns. The objective is clear: to isolate the administration from the public by destroying the channels of communication that keep voters informed.

These maneuvers are a hallmark of the old-school "machination politics" that have long plagued South Korea. By relying on back-alley rumors rather than logical debate, the conservative establishment reveals its own intellectual bankruptcy. They cannot win the argument, so they attempt to destroy the messengers.

However, we must remember that the democracy we have today was not given; it was fought for. The systems of checks and balances we are currently establishing are the result of immense sacrifice. The only way to break this cycle of deception is for the public to remain vigilant, to look past the superficial headlines of these jjirasi, and to continue supporting the institutions that are working to clean up the corruption of the past.

What to Watch Next

  • Institutional Stability: Keep a close eye on the staffing and mandate of the SCIA. Any move to delay its operations or dilute its power should be viewed as a signal that the "vested interest cartel" is gaining ground.
  • The Judiciary's Response: Observe how the Supreme Court and the prosecution react to ongoing reform efforts. Their level of cooperation—or obstruction—will indicate how deep the resistance to the Lee Jae-myung administration runs.
  • Information Integrity: As these smear campaigns continue, verify the sources of "inside info." These rumors are designed to trigger emotional reactions; maintaining a cool head is the most effective way to neutralize them.

Truth has a way of catching up to deception. While the purveyors of these rumors may hope to cause chaos today, their tactics will eventually collapse under the weight of reality. The path forward is difficult, but staying informed and refusing to be swayed by manufactured crises is the only way to ensure the progress we have fought so hard to achieve remains permanent.
