Unpacking South Korea's Power: Media Self-Indulgence, Prosecution Scandals, and the Illusion of Reform

October 03, 2026

If you spend enough time watching Korean current affairs YouTube channels lately, you will frequently run into journalists and media workers patting themselves on the back. 👀 They beam with pride, declaring, "Look at how hard I investigated this, look at this meaningful report I produced." Meanwhile, viewers grow exhausted and change the channel in frustration. The creators are acting like tormented, high-minded artists, completely lost in self-admiration while reality crumbles around them. 📉

This dynamic forces us to ask a hard question: Where are the media and journalism actually heading, and what is the naked, unfiltered face of South Korean politics and press that we are currently witnessing?

Veteran investigative journalist Chang In-soo’s incisive commentary on his YouTube broadcast goes far beyond simple media critique—it pierces straight through the structural contradictions of South Korean society. 💡 From the freezing waters of the Danube River in Budapest to the bizarre, prosecutor-dominated staffing of the Ministry of Justice, and the ruling power's bitter, insular methods of communication, his work exposes a dangerous reality. 😅 Let us step inside this sharp and deep autopsy of power to understand what is really at stake.

Reporting from Budapest and the Danger of Self-Indulgent Journalism

The Danube River and a Secret 46-Year-Old Diplomatic Cable

In July of this year, a reporting trip to Budapest, Hungary, carried far more weight than a standard overseas assignment. 🏛️ While foreigners usually know the waterway as the Danube, its accurate local pronunciation is the "Duna River." Capturing the cityscape and sunset time-lapses along its banks was an exercise in rigorous historical documentation. 🌉 The nighttime view of the majestic Hungarian Parliament building across the river was breathtaking, but behind those postcard aesthetics lay the grueling footwork of reporters trying to unearth heavy historical truths.

This local reporting trip was not meant to capture pretty scenery. 🕵️‍♂️ Its core mission was to locate and film a secret diplomatic cable sent to Pyongyang by the Hungarian Embassy in May 1980, currently preserved in the National Archives of Hungary. 📊 Remarkably, this document—written by a diplomat from North Korea's historical ally—contained no evidence whatsoever that North Korean troops were deployed to the Gwangju Uprising. Instead, it showed that Pyongyang was nervously monitoring the situation while worrying about a potential U.S. military response. This served as direct, undeniable proof dismantling the 46-year-old right-wing conspiracy theory that North Korean special forces instigated the Gwangju democratization movement.

Professor Kim Hee-song of the May 18 Research Institute at Chonnam National University joined the field reporting, while Kim Bo-guk, director of the East Asian Research Institute at the Hungarian National Archives, provided crucial translation support. 🌐 South Korea’s state-backed truth commission has also stated that the North Korean infiltration claims are false, yet deliberate cover-ups by the military and intelligence agencies remain an unresolved burden. Recovering historical truth by digging through faded diplomatic documents in a foreign land proves why independent journalism must exist in an era flooded with shallow gossip.

Artists Playing Pretend: The Repulsion of Self-Gratifying Reporting

Yet, looking inward at the media industry, one easily spots individuals racing in the exact opposite direction of this public-interest mission. 😤 These are self-satisfied reporters and producers who congratulate themselves: "Look what I pulled off," "Look at this scoop I landed." 📉 Their ratings hit rock bottom, the public ignores them, and their work leaves zero social impact, yet they posture as grand auteurs. It is a completely dysfunctional distortion of journalism.

Critics within the broadcasting world call this out with stinging accuracy. 💡 "If you want to give yourself deep meaning and satisfy only yourself, you should start a personal YouTube channel, make art, or host an exhibition," goes the sharp rebuke. 🚫 A journalist or producer only fulfills their purpose when they communicate with the public and earn genuine resonance. 🗣️ Flaunting ignored broadcasts like medals is nothing more than arrogance disguised as journalism—pure self-soothing.

Ultimately, high-cost investigative projects like the Budapest archival hunt are only made possible by the passionate subscriptions and financial support of everyday viewers. 💰 When producer Lim Sung-jun worried beforehand, "That trip is going to cost around 10 million won (about $7,000), is the story really worth it?", he was wrestling with economic reality. 🤝 The moment public backing vanishes, independent media suffocates. Journalists cannot afford to lock themselves in ivory towers of self-satisfaction.

Public Empathy: The Only Oxygen for Independent Media

In an era where the boundary between news and general content has collapsed, audience engagement dictates media survival. 📈 Reporters are supposed to have their fingers on the pulse of public reaction. 📺 Deep-dive investigative formats succeed precisely because they accurately tap into what viewers find genuinely valuable.

Journalism that fails to win public resonance will inevitably end up isolated in the cold. 📉 True public interest does not materialize simply because ten reporters sit in a room patting each other on the back. 👥 Life is breathed into a report only when countless citizens look at it together, nod, and feel a shared understanding. 💡 The simple truth that "public value comes from the audience engaging with and watching a story" is a rule every media worker should recite daily.

Ultimately, donations and support from subscribers mean far more than mere financial assistance. 🙏 They are a strict command and encouragement from the public: look only at the truth, ignore the pressures of power, and run forward. 🏃‍♂️ The power to dig up 46-year-old truths from freezing foreign archives comes entirely from that trust. 🌟 The media must never take that weight lightly.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • On-the-ground investigation of secret Hungarian diplomatic archives empirically disproved the myth of North Korean military involvement in the 1980 Gwangju Uprising.
  • "Self-satisfying journalism" that ignores public ratings and empathy is nothing more than isolated arrogance.
  • The only fuel allowing independent media to pursue deep investigations without compromising to power is passionate viewer support.

Prosecutorial Power, Public Defense, and the Pro-Yoon Paradox

From Chミae-ae's Favor to Disciplinary Backlash

Lee Jae-myung posted a strong defense on social media regarding public backlash against Kim Ji-yong, a controversial nominee for high office. 🗣️ He argued that if critics launch baseless attacks knowing accusations are false, it is not critique but malicious slander, and a disruption of state administration rather than constructive advice. 📉 The stance is that labeling nominees simply as "pro-Yoon" (loyalists to former President Yoon Suk Yeol) or die-hard prosecutors is obstructionist rather than fair criticism. But we must carefully examine whether the government’s defense holds up to common sense.

The administration’s primary defense is that Kim Ji-yong was promoted and selected as a chief prosecutor during the liberal Moon Jae-in administration under Justice Minister Choo Mi-ae. ⚖️ By that logic, the most prominent prosecutor elevated under Moon Jae-in was Yoon Suk Yeol himself—meaning the government should arguably draft Yoon back into service immediately. 🤷‍♂️ If a past promotion under a liberal government grants total immunity, then figures like Han Dong-hoon (former Justice Minister and conservative leader) are immune from criticism under the same rule.

Furthermore, when prosecutors attempted to discipline then-Prosecutor General Yoon Suk Yeol, Kim Ji-yong voluntarily signed a petition opposing that disciplinary action. 📝 Having passed through major Seoul posts at the core of the prosecutorial establishment, his trajectory clearly shows how he shifted stances depending on which way political winds blew. 🌊 While apologists frame his post-Yoon-election demotion to a regional deputy chief post as being "driven out as a persecuted victim," reaching the position of Gwangju High Prosecutors' Office deputy chief is a towering altitude that ordinary line prosecutors can only dream of. 📉 Packaging a voluntary resignation as political martyrdom is blatant historical distortion.

The Disappearance of Choi Eun-soon's Perjury Case and Silence on Channel A

The true nature of nominee Kim Ji-yong is further laid bare by the cases he handled as chief of the Supreme Prosecutors' Office Criminal Department under Yoon Suk Yeol. 🔍 Regarding the perjury incitement case involving First Lady Kim Keon-hee’s mother, Choi Eun-soon, the Supreme Prosecutors' Office ordered a reinvestigation, which should have led to formal indictment and accountability. ⚖️ Instead, just four months after ordering the reinvestigation, the department quietly closed it without charges, building an impenetrable wall against future probes.

Despite the reinvestigation order, practically nothing changed on the ground. 🚫 There was no high-intensity interrogation or search and seizure directed at Kim Keon-hee or Choi Eun-soon. 📉 It was a tailored, face-saving disposition designed not to ruffle the feathers of power. 💡 The critique that "cherry-picking only the facts you like does not constitute objective evidence" directly targets this selective memory and self-exculpating behavior.

During the "Channel A scandal" (concerning allegations that a conservative journalist and prosecutors conspired to pressure a jailed executive to frame a liberal politician), Kim was in the command chain yet remained completely silent, claiming he "did nothing." 🏃‍♂️ Amid the chaotic battles over search warrants and Han Dong-hoon's phone, hiding behind claims of non-involvement is hardly a responsible posture for a high-ranking official. 🗣️ The sharp critique—"Isn't that just cowardice? Pure cowardice, following the current and reading the room"—projects the pathetic shadows of political prosecutors who shrink away whenever principle is required.

The Hollow Fiction of the "Non-Pro-Yoon" Prosecutor Label

Just because Yoon Suk Yeol and Han Dong-hoon later fell out and became bitter enemies does not magically transform Han into an "anti-Yoon prosecutor" or a reformer. 🤔 Absolutely not. ⚖️ Presenting internal turf wars over spoils as a grand anti-dictatorship struggle is deceitful fraud. 📉 The controversies surrounding Kim Ji-yong follow the exact same logic.

Fragmentary facts presented as proof that someone has shed their pro-Yoon identity are merely blindfolds hiding the reality of the prosecutorial kingdom. 🎭 Those who served as bodyguards on the front lines of prosecutorial overreach cannot magically be laundered into reform workers just because the political map shifted. 💡 The analogy that "if falling out with Yoon proves you aren't pro-Yoon, then Han Dong-hoon is the ultimate example" cuts right through the logical fallacy.

Defensive barriers erected under the banner of "objective evidence" are ultimately partisan selections meant to cover up the past sins of chosen appointees. 🧱 Labeling criticism as "state obstruction" and hard-hitting questions as "slander" represents a politics of non-communication that democratic societies must guard against. 📉 If genuine reform is the goal, the administration must stop chanting hollow slogans about objectivity and coldly assess who actually benefits from handing absolute power to the prosecution.

🔥 The Fatal Flaw in Defending Pro-Yoon Prosecutors

Attempting to launder historical misdeeds—such as signing anti-disciplinary petitions and shielding Choi Eun-soon from prosecution—using the flimsy badge of having been promoted under the Moon administration is explicit deception.

Classified Documents, Presidential Privilege, and Silent Reform

The Blue House's Arrogant Excuse and Assault on the Right to Know

A photo of a daily general situation report posted on President Lee Jae-myung's social media exposed both institutional laxity and Blue House privilege. 📸 Stamped right in the middle of the document was the word "Classified" (대외비—*daewoe-bi*), symbolizing strictly protected security materials that must never be released externally. 📉 If an ordinary investigative reporter or independent outlet had obtained and published such a document, they would instantly be branded as state-obstructing subversives and hit with police investigations and defamation lawsuits. 🚨 The self-deprecating groan, "I would have had to leave South Korea," is no exaggeration.

Even more absurd was the explanation offered by the Blue House spokesperson's office. 🤬 To claim that "this classification applies to ordinary public servants, but the President does not fall under it" belongs in no legal code on earth. 👑 Does South Korea possess an absolute monarch? 🏛️ Ordinary bureaucrats are bound hand and foot by constitutional rules and surveillance, yet the supreme leader claims the privilege of ignoring even self-imposed security regulations.

The "classified" stamps carelessly distributed by the government are ultimately shields allowing bureaucrats to evade responsibility and dodge public scrutiny. 🛡️ Officials prefer a world where citizens remain entirely in the dark about their activities. 📉 Transparent information brings accountability and potential disciplinary action, so burying documents under security labels is the easy way out. 💡 Breaking down this chronic practice of violating the public's right to know should be the starting point of true media reform; instead, watching Blue House public relations staff lose their minds to excuse the President inspires profound disappointment and anger.

The President’s Illusion: Bypassing the Press for Social Media

When President Lee encourages his base via social media with messages like, "Let us not feel bitter if the media fails to report or trusted YouTube channels do not inform us," it leaves a bitter aftertaste. 📱 The rhetoric that "we can proudly handle building a better world ourselves" sounds appealing, but it rests on deep distrust and resignation toward the press. 📉 If the mainstream media fails to properly report or ignores institutional abuses, didn't the public delegate power precisely to correct those failures?

A president is not an entertainer swapping hearts with fans on social media. 🌟 If a critical press is the problem, the administration should reform the structural contradictions of privileged media and create legal incentives for proper reporting. ⚖️ Running away with the excuse, "The media won't print our thoughts anyway, so I'll just use social media," is deeply irresponsible for a chief executive. 🗣️ The cry to "just carry out media reform" serves as a piercing warning to a regime hiding behind social media and basking in partisan applause while keeping its reformist blade sheathed.

President Lee's messaging on responding to "lies and slander with dignified, rational criticism" sounds refined, but it grows bitter when considering who is targeted. 📉 Those who have shouted for prosecutorial and media reform while sharply criticizing irrationality in power are routinely smeared as forces of "falsehood and abuse." 🗣️ Phrases warning against those who "lose privileges through reform, or potential beneficiaries seduced by privileged forces" lay bare an institutional aversion to critical voices.

Ultimately, President Lee has made clear his alignment with his core support base—the "New Lee Jae-myung" faction that offers unconditional support. 🤝 Past analyses doubting whether a president would interfere in party affairs or hold back reform turned out to be dead wrong. 📉 Bypassing internal reform allies and hesitating to swing the sword of justice were all part of this overarching political choice.

Trusting only one's inner circle feels comforting, but when a government shatters the blades of those crying out for reform outside its fortress walls, its future dims. 🌑 When an administration elected to complete historical tasks like prosecuting corruption and reforming the media writes off dissenting voices as "state obstruction" and retreats behind a partisan wall, the end result is inevitable. 💡 The sigh that "I try to look at this with blurred vision, but I cannot because it is too obvious" captures the cruel reality facing disappointed reformers.

📊 The Double Standard of Classification and Communication

Imposing harsh security rules and investigative blades on ordinary citizens and independent media while granting a supreme executive the privilege of exemption from classification rules destroys basic common sense.

The Prosecutorification of the Ministry of Justice

From Justice Ministry to Prosecutor Ministry: A Staffing Takeover

Inspection of the Ministry of Justice personnel status chart reveals a staggering reality. 📋 Completed through the tireless footwork of civic researchers, this chart visually proves that South Korea’s Ministry of Justice has completely lost democratic control and degenerated into a "Ministry of Prosecutors." 📉 From the Minister's office, Vice Minister's office, spokesperson's office, policy planning unit, planning and coordination office, down to the core Prosecutors' Bureau and Inspection Office, key positions are filled exclusively by prosecutors and prosecution career bureaucrats.

The severity becomes sharper examining exact numbers. 📊 In the Minister's office, out of 9 total staff, 1 is a prosecutor and 2 are prosecution officials; the Vice Minister's office includes 2 prosecutors among 6 staff. 🏛️ In the spokesperson's office, both the spokesperson and deputy are prosecutors out of 26 staff, while the policy planning unit fields 4 prosecutors including its head. ⚖️ Most notably, the Prosecutors' Bureau packs 27 prosecutors and 71 prosecution bureaucrats among its 127 personnel. Key seats—including the head of criminal planning, public criminal affairs, international criminal affairs, criminal legislation, and the prosecution reform task force—are monopolized without exception by active prosecutors.

Before this massive personnel cartel, civilian control and the "de-prosecutorization" of the ministry are exposed as empty lies. 📉 The Ministry of Justice operates like a subordinate agency commanded by the prosecution, with even the internal inspection office headed by a prosecutor. 💡 The outcry that "within months, the Ministry of Justice became a playground for prosecutors; it is a Prosecutor Ministry, not a Justice Ministry" accurately diagnoses that the prosecutorial castle hasn't lost a single millimeter of ground.

Betrayed Reform and Deep Self-Reflection

Naive assumptions from early on in the term—that "surely a president wouldn't interfere in party affairs or obstruct prosecution reform"—proved to be painful miscalculations. 📉 Sidelining reform figures and choking the momentum of structural change demonstrate how reform forces themselves grew complacent and left the prosecutorial sword untouched. ⚖️ The frank confession, "I was wrong; consequently, we rejected reformers and refused to carry out prosecution reform," carries heavy resonance.

While pointing out the tyranny of major media owners, rank-and-file reporters cannot all be dismissed as privileged elites, yet media executives and pro-regime commentators have abetted the death of journalism by scraping together scraps from power. 📉 Within the symbiotic relationship between the prosecutor-dominated Ministry of Justice and a horse-race press churning out dictation journalism, democratic procedures have been trampled. 💡 When a government elected to enact reform compromises with the core privileged elite and hands over institutional control, the citizens' betrayal needs no explanation.

The Uncertain Horizon of a Base-Locked Regime

Once a president declares loyalty exclusively to a core partisan base, the clock of reform stops completely. 🛑 A regime that dismisses rational critique as "lies and malicious plots" while branding internal dissent as "state obstruction" will face historical isolation. 📉 The democratic procedures won with blood and sweat risk collapsing inside a populist fortress reliant on a loyal few.
