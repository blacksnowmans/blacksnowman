Conspiracy Theories and Internal Strife: How an Unproven Cult Allegation is Shaking South Korea’s Ruling Party

November 04, 2026

Inside the halls of the National Assembly in Seoul, South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) was supposed to be entering a period of disciplined political consolidation. Following the tumultuous impeachment and subsequent imprisonment of Yoon Suk-yeol, the nation inaugurated President Lee Jae-myung, promising transparent state management, robust internal democracy, and a clean break from past political scandals. Instead, the ruling party finds itself entangled in a bitter, self-inflicted crisis—one fueled not by policy disagreements, but by wild, unverified conspiracy theories involving a controversial religious sect.

At the center of this storm is Kim Min-seok, a prominent leader within the DPK, who publicly floated accusations of a "secret three-party alliance" allegedly conspiring with Shincheonji, a fringe religious group notorious in South Korea for its secretive operations and past political friction. What began as a tactical weapon in an internal power struggle has rapidly spiraled out of control. With zero hard evidence presented to support the claims, the party now faces an explosive backlash from its own rank-and-file members, who accuse leadership of betraying core democratic principles to protect factional interests.

Weaponizing Religion: The Origins of the 'Shincheonji' Phantom

To understand why an accusation involving Shincheonji strikes such a raw nerve in Korean politics, one must understand the group's fraught status in the public imagination. Known officially as the Shincheonji Church of Jesus, the organization is widely viewed by mainstream Christian denominations and the broader public as a dangerous cult. In South Korean political discourse, branding an opponent with "Shincheonji ties" is a nuclear option—a stain designed to trigger immediate moral outrage and public distrust.

When Kim Min-seok invoked the bizarre frame of an "Anti-Bun-Shin 3-Party Secret Alliance" during official party proceedings, he elevated what sounded like an internet conspiracy theory into formal political debate. Rather than presenting sworn testimonies, bank records, or concrete digital evidence, the leadership relied on vague assertions of "circumstantial evidence" and unnamed whistleblowers to accuse internal rivals of colluding with the religious group.

For a ruling party obligated to uphold the constitutional principle of the separation of church and state, launching an unprovoked witch hunt without solid facts represents a dangerous precedent. Instead of fostering rational deliberation—a core promise of President Lee Jae-myung’s administration—the party leadership turned to old-school smear tactics. The immediate fallout has been a complete breakdown of trust within party ranks, distracting the government from pressing legislative matters and public welfare issues.

A Crisis of Accountability: Tears Without An Apology

In politics, launching an aggressive political offensive is risky, but refusing to take responsibility when it falters is fatal. Despite formal discussions at the highest levels of the party's Supreme Council, Kim Min-seok has neither produced tangible proof nor offered an explicit apology to those falsely accused. Instead, the leadership has attempted to quietly move past the issue, hoping public memory will fade.

This strategy of stonewalling reached a dramatic point during a press event marking Kim’s first 100 days in leadership. Confronted by reporters, Kim shed tears in public—a moment quickly framed by sympathetic media outlets as a display of heartfelt passion and political resolve. However, many political observers and party members viewed the emotional outburst with deep cynicism, seeing it as an attempt to substitute emotional theater for factual accountability.

"Admitting a mistake is not a sign of weakness; the true disgrace lies in inventing ever more convoluted excuses to avoid admitting error. What the party needs right now is not theatrical rhetoric, but the courage to tell the truth."

This dynamic has created a jarring paradox. During the presidency of Yoon Suk-yeol, the Democratic Party relentlessly criticized the conservative administration for abusing state power, framing political opponents, and ignoring procedural fairness. Yet, by resorting to baseless accusations against its own members, the ruling party risks adopting the very behavior it once condemned. If power is acquired or maintained through false narratives, its underlying legitimacy collapses.

Veteran Both-Sides-ism and the Capture of Youth Politics

The party’s failure to deal decisively with the controversy has been further exacerbated by how different generations of politicians have responded to the mess.

Park Jie-won, a veteran lawmaker known for decades of political maneuvering dating back to the administration of President Kim Dae-jung, attempted to play the role of elder mediator. However, his approach relied heavily on classic "both-sides-ism" (양비론). Park privately counseled Kim Min-seok to seek prior understanding from rival leaders like Jung Chung-rae, while simultaneously advising Jung not to escalate the conflict. By retreating into nostalgic anecdotes from the Kim Dae-jung era, Park offered a superficial compromise that effectively shielded the accusers from taking genuine responsibility.

Equally troubling to party reformers is the behavior of the party's younger generation of lawmakers. Figures such as Jeon Yong-gi and Kim Dong-ah, who entered politics promising fresh perspectives and institutional reform, have instead rushed to serve as human shields for the party leadership.

  • Factional Loyalty over Principle: Public statements issued by younger lawmakers have focused almost exclusively on defending party bosses rather than demanding factual transparency.
  • Electoral Calculation: Critics note that junior politicians appear increasingly anxious about securing candidate nominations for upcoming elections, compelling them to align unconditionally with current faction leaders.
  • The Erosion of Youth Politics: Rather than representing the public or rank-and-file members, young lawmakers risk repeating the worst habits of machine politics.

This dynamic threatens to destroy the very purpose of youth representation within the party. When emerging leaders prioritize factional self-preservation over political integrity, it signals to voters that the party’s reformist impulses have stalled.

The Grassroots Revolt: What to Watch Next

The rank-and-file membership is no longer staying silent. In a rare and significant escalation, nine distinct DPK member organizations issued a joint declaration demanding Kim Min-seok’s immediate resignation from leadership. Their demands are straightforward: produce concrete, verifiable evidence regarding the alleged Shincheonji secret alliance, or issue a public apology and step down to take full political responsibility.

Meanwhile, the conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), has seized on the inner turmoil, labeling the entire episode a "fraudulent political hit job" and calling for formal investigations. For a ruling party tasked with driving national policy alongside President Lee Jae-myung, being dragged down by unforced errors provides easy political ammunition to its opponents.

As this internal battle reaches a boiling point, here are the key developments to watch in the coming weeks:

  • Formal Disclosure of Evidence: Watch whether Kim Min-seok and the leadership release any verified materials or whistleblower logs regarding Shincheonji, or if they continue to rely on vague assertions of "circumstantial evidence."
  • Escalation of Grassroots Petitions: Pay attention to whether the nine member groups can coordinate wider rally efforts or formal motions within the party's central committee to force a leadership vote.
  • Factional Realignment: Monitor how senior figures, including Jung Chung-rae, respond if leadership attempts to sweep the matter under the rug without a formal apology.
  • Legislative Friction: Observe whether the opposition People Power Party successfully uses this scandal to stall key legislative initiatives in the National Assembly.

Political trust is notoriously difficult to build and remarkably easy to squander. If South Korea’s ruling party hopes to maintain its credibility and deliver on its promises of democratic governance, it must demonstrate that it holds its own leaders to the same standards of truth and accountability that it demands of its opponents.
