A House Divided: Inside the Sudden Ethics Referral Shaking South Korea’s Ruling Party

November 16, 2026

When the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) convened behind closed doors on the afternoon of November 15, the atmosphere inside the National Assembly halls in western Seoul was already electric. What usually takes weeks of backchannel political horse-trading, cautious mediation, and discreet quiet counseling collapsed into an extraordinary display of swift bureaucratic discipline. Kim Min-seok, a senior member of the DPK’s Supreme Council, formally received the findings of an internal ethics inspection into representative Choi Min-hee. The investigation had been launched just three days earlier. Without hesitation or a moment spent trying to defuse the matter out of the public eye, Kim issued an immediate directive: send the case straight to the party’s Ethics Tribunal for formal disciplinary action.

In South Korean politics, internal party disputes are often allowed to simmer in committee rooms until backroom elders can broker an uneasy truce. But the decision to refer an outspoken lawmaker to the party’s highest quasi-judicial disciplinary body in record time signals that the simmering feud within the ruling camp has broken through the surface. What began as a mounting public backlash over Choi’s aggressive public statements has now triggered an open factional confrontation, drawn junior lawmakers into emergency late-night huddles, and opened the door to outside police scrutiny.

A Lightning-Fast Referral: The Collapse of Informal Settlement

The speed with which the party leadership moved against representative Choi Min-hee surprised both allies and rivals across Yeouido, the island district that serves as the center of South Korean politics. Typically, when a sitting lawmaker generates controversy, the party’s Ethics Inspection Corps conducts a protracted fact-finding probe while leadership figures test the political temperature to see if a public apology can put the fire out. Instead, Kim Min-seok and the leadership moved in just seventy-two hours, treating the issue not as an unfortunate verbal stumble, but as an acute threat to the party’s governing momentum.

At the center of the storm is Choi’s track record of contentious rhetoric and hardline public remarks on sensitive political battlegrounds. Her uncompromising, confrontational statements had dominated domestic headlines for days, sparking accusations that she was alienating the broader public and handing easy ammunition to political opponents. By handing the matter directly over to the Ethics Tribunal, the leadership signaled that standard political mediation had failed—or was deemed too politically costly to attempt.

The Ethics Tribunal will now evaluate whether Choi breached the party constitution’s fundamental mandate requiring members to maintain personal dignity and uphold the party’s public standing. Under DPK internal statutes, the panel possesses broad punitive authority ranging from formal warnings and temporary suspensions of party membership to the ultimate sanction: permanent expulsion from the party. Observers widely interpret the leadership's rapid referral as a clear precursor to substantive punishment, raising the stakes for the entire legislative majority.

Factional Fault Lines: Pro-Lee vs. Pro-Jung Clash

While the leadership couches the move in terms of party discipline and decorum, the dispute has instantly ignited the ideological and factional fault lines within the ruling Democratic Party. On one side stands the party’s dominant wing—the "pro-Lee" (chin-myeong) faction, loyal to President Lee Jae-myung and his policy agenda. On the other side are members aligned with veteran lawmaker Jung Cheong-rae, commonly referred to as the "pro-Jung" (chin-cheong) camp.

Immediately following the news of the Ethics Tribunal referral, pro-Lee figures went on the offensive, openly demanding maximum penalties. Hardliners argued that Choi’s behavior had become an unacceptable drag on the government’s reform agenda, with some going so far as to demand her outright expulsion or forced withdrawal from the party. To this camp, maintaining iron discipline is paramount: any lawmaker who creates needless controversy while the administration is pushing critical initiatives is viewed not merely as a liability, but as someone firing arrows from behind their own ranks.

Conversely, members of the pro-Jung faction have reacted with deep apprehension, questioning whether the swift referral amounts to a targeted, factional purge. Instead of isolating an individual lawmaker through severe administrative punishment, voices within this circle argue that the party should look in the mirror, using the moment to examine its internal communication structures and broader culture of debate. Meanwhile, Jung Cheong-rae himself maintained a conspicuous silence in the immediate wake of the decision, choosing not to take a public stand while monitoring whether the disciplinary push will permanently alter the internal balance of power.

Junior Lawmakers Step In: The Emergency Caucus and the Limits of Consensus

The widening rift quickly rattled the broader rank and file. On the evening of November 15, "Deo Mincho," the caucus representing the Democratic Party’s freshman lawmakers, held an emergency meeting to determine their collective response. While first-term lawmakers in South Korea often fall in line behind senior leadership directives, the sheer velocity of the internal blowback forced them to confront the crisis directly.

During a grueling, late-night discussion, the junior lawmakers reached an overwhelming consensus on one point: the crisis was extraordinarily grave, and Choi’s actions had caused undeniable damage to the party’s public standing. Anger ran high, with several participants vocally backing calls for severe sanctions or demanding that Choi leave the party entirely. The fact that junior legislators felt compelled to organize an independent, collective forum demonstrated how deeply the controversy had shaken the caucus’s confidence in day-to-day party stability.

Yet, despite the heated debate, the caucus was unable to release a unified declaration before the night ended. While hardliners pushed for an immediate, high-profile statement endorsing Choi’s expulsion, more cautious members argued against preempting the formal procedures of the Ethics Tribunal, warning that piling on would only inflame factional bitterness. Unable to bridge the gap on the exact wording and demands, the freshman caucus adjourned, agreeing to reconvene and release their definitive position the following morning.

Police Complaints and Cross-Party Turmoil in the Capitol

The controversy spilled entirely beyond internal party boundaries when outside legal machinery was set into motion. On the same day that the party leadership referred Choi to the Ethics Tribunal, a domestic civic organization filed a formal criminal complaint against her with the police, citing the illegality of her controversial remarks. The complaint introduces an unpredictable wild card: representative Choi must now navigate both an internal disciplinary hearing and potential criminal investigation.

For the ruling party leadership, this dual pressure creates an urgent dilemma. If state investigators begin probing a prominent ruling-party lawmaker before the party can clean its own house, the DPK risks losing control of the public narrative. The threat of a protracted legal drama has effectively locked the leadership into an accelerated timeline, giving them little choice but to demonstrate swift internal accountability to blunt external scrutiny.

The timing could hardly be worse for the National Assembly as a whole. The main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), has been consumed by its own bitter internal power struggle over the potential reinstatement and disputed disciplinary treatment of former interim leader Han Dong-hoon. With both the ruling party and the chief opposition simultaneously locked in vicious intra-party civil wars, parliamentary operations are edging dangerously close to paralysis just as lawmakers face the critical November legislative window, where key policy reforms and the national budget must be negotiated and passed.

What to Watch Next

The coming days will test the ruling party's ability to contain an explosive internal crisis before it completely hijacks parliamentary governance. As the dispute moves forward, several concrete developments will determine the outcome:

  • The Freshman Caucus Declaration: The exact wording of the statement released by "Deo Mincho" on November 16 will signal whether the party’s rank-and-file lawmakers are willing to publicly endorse a complete break with Choi, which would exert tremendous pressure on the tribunal.
  • The Ethics Tribunal's Ruling: Whether the disciplinary panel settles on a mild reprimand or imposes severe punishment—such as a suspension of party rights or outright expulsion—will indicate whether the pro-Lee leadership intends to enforce strict ideological conformity.
  • The Opposition’s Counterattack: Observers should monitor whether the People Power Party sets aside its own internal warfare over Han Dong-hoon long enough to leverage Choi's police complaint into a coordinated blockade of legislative proceedings.
