The $1.5 Trillion Litmus Test: Why Anthropic’s Nasdaq Debut Is the Event of the Century for Korean Tech

November 12, 2026

Imagine the tension on the floor of the Nasdaq on the morning of November 19. Traders are not just looking at a ticker symbol; they are looking at the culmination of the greatest capital-raising exercise in the history of the technology industry. For months, the rumors of Anthropic—the San Francisco-based artificial intelligence startup known for its "Claude" models—going public have fueled a speculative fire that has reached across the Pacific to the boardrooms of Seoul. With a valuation target now soaring toward $1.5 trillion and a fundraising goal of roughly $90.4 billion, this is no longer just a "tech IPO." It is a referendum on the future of the global economy.

For readers outside of South Korea, it might seem strange that a San Francisco startup’s listing would be the lead story in Seoul. However, in a nation where the economy is built on the pillars of semiconductors and high-tech manufacturing, Anthropic’s success or failure is inextricably linked to the prosperity of the Korean peninsula. When Anthropic wins, the giants of the Korean stock exchange—companies like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix—win with it. As the countdown to November 19 begins, the stakes could not be higher for the global AI ecosystem and the Korean supply chain that powers it.

A Valuation Beyond Imagination and the Death of the "AI Bubble" Narrative

The scale of the Anthropic IPO is, quite literally, unprecedented. To put the $90.4 billion fundraising target into perspective, it dwarfs almost every major public offering in history, including landmark debuts like that of SpaceX. Investors are being offered shares in a range of $70 to $78, and the demand is already so overwhelming that lead underwriters are considering hiking the price before the first bell even rings. This isn't just "strong demand"; it is a stampede of institutional capital trying to find a seat at the table before the doors close.

The core of the excitement lies in the revised valuation. Initially, analysts whispered about a $1.4 trillion market cap, but following the "sell-out" of the initial subscription tranches, that figure has been adjusted upward to over $1.5 trillion (approximately 1,894 trillion KRW). For a company that specializes primarily in generative AI, this is a staggering vote of confidence. It suggests that the "AI bubble" skepticism that haunted the markets throughout the early 2020s is being replaced by a hard-nosed belief in the long-term cash flows of the industry.

This valuation matters because it serves as a "wealth effect" for the early believers. In Korea, all eyes are on SK Telecom, the nation’s largest telecommunications carrier. Having had the foresight to invest in Anthropic during its earlier stages, SK Telecom now finds its stake valued at approximately $3.1 billion (about 4.3 trillion KRW). This windfall is a validation of the current administration's push to transform South Korea from a hardware-centric nation into a global AI powerhouse. Under the government of President Lee Jae-myung, there has been a concerted effort by the ruling Democratic Party to foster "AI sovereignty," and the massive paper gains for a domestic champion like SK Telecom provide significant political and economic capital for that mission.

The Triangular Alliance: TSMC, Nvidia, and the Silicon Backbone

To understand why Anthropic is worth $1.5 trillion, one must look at the "Triple Alliance" it has formed with the kings of the semiconductor world. In a move that signaled the end of the era of startups working in isolation, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC)—the world's largest contract chipmaker—recently approved a direct investment of $500 million (about 670 billion KRW) into Anthropic. This was part of a broader $1 billion package that also included a stake in OpenAI.

Why would a foundry like TSMC, which usually stays neutral, take such a massive equity stake? The answer lies in the roadmap. Both Anthropic and OpenAI are moving toward designing their own custom AI chips to reduce their dependence on off-the-shelf hardware. By becoming a shareholder, TSMC ensures that it will be the one manufacturing those chips. It is a strategic move to lock in the "AI Foundry" demand for the next decade.

Furthermore, the relationship with Nvidia has evolved from a simple customer-vendor dynamic into a deep strategic partnership. Anthropic has reportedly secured over $10 billion in new large-scale contracts and infrastructure deals, much of it tied to Nvidia’s computational power. This synergy between Nvidia’s hardware and Anthropic’s "Claude" software has created a feedback loop of revenue growth. Some estimates suggest Anthropic’s recent revenue growth could be as much as 150 times higher than its previous benchmarks. This isn't just hype; it is a massive influx of B2B (business-to-business) cash that proves AI models can generate real-world profit, not just interesting chat transcripts.

Why the Korean Market Is Riding the Anthropic Wave

For the average investor in Seoul, the Anthropic IPO is the best news they have heard all year. On the news of the finalized IPO schedule, shares of Samsung Electronics (South Korea’s largest company) and SK Hynix (the world's second-largest memory chipmaker) jumped by 3% to 5%. The KOSDAQ, Korea’s tech-heavy secondary board, surged 5% in a single day. To the uninitiated, this might seem like a strange correlation, but it is actually quite simple: Anthropic’s $90.4 billion in new capital will be spent almost immediately on building data centers.

A modern AI data center is, essentially, a giant warehouse full of Korean memory chips. Specifically, Anthropic requires High Bandwidth Memory (HBM), a specialized type of DRAM that allows data to move at the speeds required by generative AI. SK Hynix currently leads the world in HBM production, with Samsung Electronics racing to catch up. When Anthropic raises $90 billion, a significant portion of that money will eventually flow into the bank accounts of SK Hynix and Samsung as they fulfill orders for the next generation of AI servers.

"The Anthropic IPO is acting as a 'savior' for the Korean semiconductor supply chain," noted one local market analyst. "It provides a clear signal that the demand for high-performance memory isn't just a temporary spike, but a structural shift in the global economy."

This is particularly important given the broader economic climate. The Korean market has often struggled with "Korea Discount" (the tendency for Korean stocks to be undervalued due to geopolitical tensions or corporate governance issues). However, the "AI momentum" provided by the Anthropic listing is effectively masking external risks and driving a domestic rally in large-cap IT stocks. Investors are now positioning themselves a week ahead of the listing, trying to pick the sub-contractors and component makers that will benefit from the massive infrastructure expansion Anthropic has planned.

From Chatbots to National Security: The Real-World Stakes

If the first half of the Anthropic story was about valuations and chips, the second half is about utility. One of the most compelling reasons for the "Claude" developer's high valuation is its expansion into national security. In the United States, major power utility companies have begun deploying Anthropic’s AI solutions to defend against "AI hackers"—sophisticated cyber-attacks that use automated systems to probe for weaknesses in national infrastructure.

We are entering an era of "AI vs. AI" in the realm of cybersecurity. Anthropic's models are being used as a shield to protect the electrical grid and water systems. While this proves the technical reliability of their software, it also highlights a significant challenge: the cost. Maintaining a high-performance security model 24/7 requires an astronomical amount of electricity and computing power. This "cost of operation" is the primary hurdle Anthropic must clear after it goes public.

Critics argue that while the revenue growth is impressive, the sustainability of these $1.5 trillion valuations depends on the company’s ability to prove that AI isn't just a "cost center" but a "profit engine" for its clients. If a utility company spends more on the AI shield than it saves in prevented attacks, the math eventually fails. This is the "bubble" that skeptics are waiting to see pop. Anthropic’s task is to use its new $90 billion war chest to innovate its way out of this efficiency trap, making its models cheaper and faster to run.

What to Watch Next

As we approach the November 19 debut, the global financial community—and the Korean tech sector in particular—will be watching several key indicators to judge the health of the AI industry:

  • The Final Offering Price: If Anthropic raises the price range above $78, it will signal that institutional "FOMO" (fear of missing out) is at an all-time high, likely leading to a massive first-day "pop" in the stock price.
  • Post-Listing Institutional Inflow: Watch whether major global pension funds and sovereign wealth funds maintain their positions or sell for a quick profit. For Korean investors, the movement of institutional "big money" will dictate the longevity of the current semiconductor rally.
  • Revenue Conversion: In the first quarterly report following the IPO, the market will look for proof that those $10 billion in infrastructure contracts are converting into sustained, high-margin revenue.
  • The Korean Supply Chain Impact: Keep a close eye on the shipment volumes of HBM chips from SK Hynix. If Anthropic’s capital flows directly into new server orders, the earnings reports for Korea’s chip giants in early 2027 could be record-breaking.

The Anthropic IPO is more than just a company going public; it is the moment the AI industry grows up and accepts the burden of proof. For the workers in the semiconductor fabs of Gyeonggi Province and the traders in Seoul’s Yeouido financial district, November 19 isn't just a date on the calendar—it's the beginning of a new era.
