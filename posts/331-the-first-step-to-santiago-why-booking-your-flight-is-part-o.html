The First Step to Santiago: Why Booking Your Flight is Part of the Pilgrimage

September 07, 2026

The journey to the Camino de Santiago—the historic network of pilgrimage routes leading to the shrine of Saint James the Great in northwestern Spain—rarely begins on a cobblestone path. For most modern travelers, it starts in front of a glowing computer monitor, staring at flight aggregators and debating multi-city itineraries. Deciding to walk the Portuguese Way (one of the most popular routes, typically starting in Lisbon or Porto) is not just about lacing up hiking boots. It requires a logistical leap: deciding how to weave two countries, Portugal and Spain, into a single, cohesive narrative.

When planning a month-long expedition—flying into Lisbon and out of Barcelona, for instance—booking airfare ceases to be a mindless administrative chore. It becomes an exercise in setting your travel philosophy. Every click on a seating chart or a baggage tier is an early negotiation with the uncertainties of the road ahead, a reality that hits home particularly hard for travelers navigating post-pandemic airline policies and complex international layovers.

Mastering the Multi-City Route

If your goal is to land in Lisbon, walk your way north toward Santiago de Compostela, and eventually make your way across Spain to catch a flight home from Barcelona, a standard round-trip ticket simply will not work. Doubling back to Lisbon just to catch a return flight wastes precious time and energy—resources that are infinitely more valuable on the trail than in an airport terminal.

This is where the "multi-city" option on flight booking engines becomes essential. By designating different entry (In) and exit (Out) cities, you maintain a continuous, forward-moving trajectory. For a typical month-long itinerary—such as arriving in Lisbon at the end of March and departing from Barcelona in early May—this optimization eliminates backtracking. In the context of a long-distance trek, time saved is literal peace of mind.

The way you choose to enter and exit a country shapes the entire narrative of your trip. Efficiency here isn't just about saving hours; it is about protecting your stamina for the road.

Yet, the temptation to chase the absolute lowest price through third-party travel agencies remains strong. While saving a few tens of dollars via an aggregator-linked reseller might look attractive on a spreadsheet, the hidden costs emerge the moment plans change. Post-pandemic aviation has made flight cancellations, schedule shifts, and missed connections frustratingly common. When an itinerary unravels, travelers who booked through third-party brokers often find themselves trapped between a customer service hotline and an unyielding airline desk, discovering that their minor initial savings have translated into hundreds of dollars in opportunity costs.

The Hidden Value of Booking Direct

Aggregators like Skyscanner are invaluable for initial price discovery, but seasoned international travelers increasingly recommend taking the final step on the airline’s official website. Whether you are flying with Lufthansa or another major carrier, booking directly grants you direct ownership of your reservation.

When you use a middleman, asking an airline to modify a ticket often results in a frustrating dead end: *"You bought through a third party; you must contact your point of purchase."* Booking direct acts as an insurance policy. The minor price differences sometimes found on official airline sites cover more than just a seat—they buy direct communication lines, clearer change policies, and immediate administrative control when travel disruptions occur.

The Economics of Refundability

Another major dilemma facing long-haul travelers is the gap between non-refundable basic economy fares and flexible tickets. A price difference of roughly $150 to $200 can easily split a travel budget down the middle—an amount that could cover a dozen modest meals along the pilgrimage route.

Choosing a non-refundable ticket is a deliberate gamble, acting as a psychological commitment device. It forces you to commit to the journey. At the same time, European Union passenger rights regulations offer baseline legal protections for severe delays or cancellations regardless of fare class, softening some of the risk. Weighing this financial gap requires an honest assessment of your own schedule and risk tolerance rather than yielding to panic.

Navigating Layovers and Logistics

For travelers unaccustomed to international transit, choosing a connecting flight introduces its own brand of anxiety. It is tempting to pick the tightest connection to minimize total travel time, but a delay of twenty minutes on your first leg can turn a simple airport transfer into a frantic sprint.

Choosing a more generous layover—say, 15 to 17 hours—transforms a stressful bottleneck into a buffer zone. While it lengthens the overall journey, it provides a quiet space to stretch cramped muscles, grab a proper meal, and mentally transition into foreign territory. On a long-distance expedition, the fastest route is rarely the most restorative one.

Essential Booking Checklist for Long-Haul Treks

  • Embrace Multi-City Logic: Structure your flights to form a single, continuous forward path rather than an out-and-back loop.
  • Research via Aggregators, Buy Direct: Use platforms to compare prices, but lock in your confirmation on the airline’s official site.
  • Double-Check Name Fields: International carriers like Lufthansa often do not ask for passport numbers during initial booking, but exact spelling matching your passport is non-negotiable.
  • Factor Risk into Your Fare: Treat the price gap between basic and flexible tickets as an insurance premium against life's unpredictability.

Reclaiming Agency in an Uncertain World

In eras marked by economic pressure and professional uncertainty, travel is rarely viewed as mere leisure; it functions as a vital pressure valve, an intentional pause to reconnect with oneself. That is why the seemingly mundane task of securing an airline ticket carries psychological weight.

When you print that E-ticket, holding a reservation code made of six alphanumeric characters, you are holding more than proof of transport. You are holding a concrete exit strategy from the repetitive loops of daily routine. Managing the fine print, verifying passport name spellings character by character, and plotting out layovers are the first quiet acts of agency on a journey where much will ultimately be left to chance.

What to Watch Next

As you move from booking flights to final preparations, keep an eye on a few critical operational details:

  • Airline Policy Shifts: Monitor baggage weight allowances and transit visa requirements for your specific layover hub, as international rules remain fluid.
  • Digital Document Backup: Keep offline PDF copies of your flight confirmations, accommodation bookings, and insurance policies stored securely on your phone.
  • Physical Conditioning: Use the weeks leading up to departure to break in your footwear and build baseline stamina, ensuring your body matches the commitment of your itinerary.

Every decision made in front of a screen today—every calculated layover and carefully verified name field—becomes part of the foundation you will walk on tomorrow. May your preparations be steady, and your journey entirely your own.
