The 147 Trillion Won Evaporation: Inside Anthropic’s Nasdaq Rollercoaster and Its Shockwaves from Wall Street to Seoul

November 23, 2026

On the morning of November 22, screens across Wall Street and Seoul flared bright green. Anthropic, the artificial intelligence powerhouse behind the Claude model, made its long-awaited debut on the Nasdaq exchange in an monumental public offering valued at $81.3 billion. Within hours of the opening bell, its shares surged 17%, appearing to validate the immense capital and hype that has defined the generative AI boom. But the celebration was cut brutally short. By the second day of trading, the stock plunged 8.5%, bringing its two-day decline to 15% and instantly erasing 147 trillion won (approximately $105 billion) in market capitalization.

Thousands of miles away, South Korea’s retail investors—famously known in local market parlance as Seohak Ants (Seohak Gaemi, a nickname for domestic individual investors who aggressively trade overseas equities, particularly U.S. tech stocks)—were caught right in the blast radius. Having poured roughly 220 billion won (about $160 million) into Anthropic on its opening day, these retail traders watched their fresh gains transform into steep paper losses overnight. With the Goldman Sachs Panic Index spiking to a near-maximum 9 out of 10, the debut has sparked a fresh wave of AI skepticism that is now echoing directly into South Korea’s financial markets.

Inside the Debut: $81.3 Billion, Internal Battles, and Market Realities

Anthropic’s Nasdaq listing was designed to be a crowning moment for the AI industry, leveraging an extraordinary $81.3 billion public offering. Yet the dramatic 15% decline over forty-eight hours exposed deep structural vulnerabilities in how major tech debuts are being engineered. In digital prediction markets monitoring AI equities, the sentiment flipped violently in real-time: the estimated probability of Anthropic closing below its initial offering price swung back and forth between 37% and 57%, moving by 20 to 25 percentage points in a single day. The pre-market euphoria vanished, replaced by acute selling pressure.

As the market cap bled 147 trillion won in twenty-four hours, a bitter blame game erupted between Silicon Valley insiders and Wall Street underwriters over the sudden crash. Critics pointed to two main operational choices during the listing process:

  • Concentrated Allocation: Market observers argued that the allocation process heavily favored existing core institutional shareholders, leaving trading dynamics prone to sharp rebalancing.
  • Immediate Employee Liquidity: Anthropic permitted insiders and employees to sell shares immediately upon listing without standard lockup restrictions, triggering a massive wave of insider supply directly into public order books.

Beyond these mechanics lies a fundamental culture clash between Silicon Valley and Wall Street. While tech executives in California remain fiercely focused on long-term capability gains and technical scaling, institutional investors on Wall Street are demanding immediate, tangible corporate earnings. For retail and institutional buyers alike who rushed into the stock on day one, Anthropic’s rapid decline underscores a cold lesson: massive valuation tags do not guarantee downside protection when structural supply issues meet growing doubts over corporate profitability.

Retail Hesitation, ETF Shocks, and Wall Street’s $110 Million Payday

Interestingly, South Korean retail investors demonstrated a rare level of initial restraint before being caught in the downdraft. The 220 billion won ($160 million) poured into Anthropic on day one by Seohak Ants represented just 20%—one-fifth—of the capital Korean retail traders deployed during SpaceX’s legendary public debut. Yet despite this relative caution, those who chased the initial 17% spike found themselves facing severe short-term pressure as the stock plummeted on day two.

For index and passive fund investors, escaping the fallout was virtually impossible. Anthropic was rapidly fast-tracked into major exchange-traded funds (ETFs) with weightings reaching up to 25%. This rapid inclusion meant that even diversified retail investors who avoided buying individual shares were automatically exposed to Anthropic's violent price swings.

The Capital vs. Consumer Dilemma: Through its public listing and corporate structure, Anthropic has amassed an astonishing $93.5 billion in total capital. Yet as megacaps raise vast treasure chests, corporate and individual consumers are increasingly scrutinizing their monthly AI subscription budgets, weighing recurring software costs against actual utility.

While public investors absorbed immediate hits, Wall Street’s lead underwriters walked away with guaranteed fortunes. Deal leads Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley each pocketed a staggering 150 billion won (approx. $110 million) in underwriting fees. However, behind closed doors, relations between Anthropic management and its bankers were intensely strained. Anthropic strictly capped the size of bank deal teams and imposed a controversial clause barring its lead underwriters from working on transactions for its main rival, OpenAI, for six months. Compounding these corporate friction points are escalating legal and tax debates in the United States over state-level wealth taxes, which threaten to impose ongoing fiscal burdens on Anthropic’s ballooning capital base.

Mathematical Milestones Ignored as AI Skepticism Takes Hold

In normal market conditions, groundbreaking academic achievements provide immediate tailwinds for tech stocks. On the very day of the market drop, an Anthropic researcher publicly claimed that its advanced AI model, Claude, had successfully proven the Riemann Hypothesis—one of mathematics' most famous and elusive unsolved problems—while the firm’s engineering team unveiled its post-quantum cybersecurity roadmap. Under typical bull-market conditions, such headlines would have triggered a massive wave of buying.

Instead, the market ignored the news entirely. The stock continued its downward slide, proving that investor psychology around generative AI has fundamentally shifted. Wall Street is no longer buying academic milestones or lab demonstrations; fund managers are demanding clear evidence of how these breakthroughs translate into corporate revenue and sustainable margins.

This reality check was reflected in broader market metrics. The Goldman Sachs Panic Index surged to 9 out of 10, signaling extreme risk aversion. The panic spread rapidly beyond Anthropic, causing a synchronized sell-off across specialized AI investment funds. Rather than acting as a catalyst for a broader market rally, Anthropic’s rocky debut activated a sector-wide wave of AI skepticism.

The Seoul Connection: KOSPI’s 4,900 Crack and the Local IT Play

The contagion quickly crossed the Pacific, hitting hardware and semiconductor heavyweights. As chip giant Nvidia dropped alongside Anthropic, the narrative that hardware makers would remain immune to software valuation doubts collapsed. This double blow hit the Korean equity market directly at a critical moment.

South Korea's benchmark market index, the KOSPI (the main index of the Korea Exchange, dominated by tech exporters like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix), broke below the critical 4,900-point threshold ahead of the November 23 trading session. The synchronized drop in U.S. tech leaders raised fears of a sustained retreat by foreign institutional investors, whose capital flows largely dictate the direction of Seoul’s main board.

Yet amidst the broader market anxiety, local traders began hunting for domestic plays linked to global enterprise AI adoption. Two major Korean IT service giants emerged at the center of market attention:

  • LG CNS: The IT service arm of South Korea’s LG Group, recognized for its aggressive integration of enterprise cloud and AI transformation services.
  • Samsung SDS: The digital solution and logistics IT branch of the Samsung conglomerate, increasingly seen as a primary vehicle for enterprise-level AI implementation across Asian markets.

Market analysts note that while LG CNS and Samsung SDS are frequently cited as local enterprise beneficiaries of the global AI expansion, domestic equity performance will ultimately hinge on broader liquidity. If foreign investors continue dumping KOSPI holdings due to U.S. tech uncertainty, individual domestic beneficiaries will struggle to rally independently.

What to Watch Next

As markets digest the fallout from Anthropic’s turbulent entry into the public markets, several key indicators will determine whether this price action is a temporary bump or the beginning of a broader AI market correction:

  • KOSPI Foreign Capital Flows: Watch whether foreign institutional selling on the KOSPI stabilizes following the crack below the 4,900 level, particularly around domestic semiconductor and IT service leaders like Samsung SDS and LG CNS.
  • Employee Share Absorption: Track whether Anthropic’s ongoing insider and employee share sales continue to cap upside bounces over the coming trading sessions.
  • ETF Rebalancing Impacts: Monitor passive capital flows as major funds rebalance their holdings to account for Anthropic’s 25% max-weight inclusion amidst heightened volatility.
  • Wall Street Underwriting Fallout: Observe how the 6-month ban on OpenAI transactions impacts deal-making and advisory relationships across major investment banks in Silicon Valley.
