The Rise of AfD: Why Germany’s Political Earthquake Matters to the World

September 10, 2026

There are moments when watching global political news sends a genuine chill down your spine. The recent results of the state parliament election in Saxony-Anhalt, a state in eastern Germany, were precisely one of those moments. Seeing a 43.8% vote share for the winner—a figure I found myself double-checking repeatedly—felt surreal. For decades, the world has known a Germany defined by its profound atonement for the tragedies of World War II, a nation where even a whisper of Nazism was enough to trigger social exile. That defensive line, once thought impenetrable, has finally crumbled. The victory of the Alternative for Germany (AfD), the country’s main far-right nationalist party, is more than just a regional election result; it is a signal that the global surge of right-wing extremism has finally breached one of history’s most fortified bastions.

What is happening at the heart of Europe? To understand why a society that has spent over 80 years meticulously purging itself of Nazi ideology has made this choice, and what this massive wave implies for the rest of us, we must look beyond the borders of a single German state. It is all too easy to dismiss this as a distant, localized event, but the air surrounding us right now feels hauntingly similar.

The Shocking Reality of the Saxony-Anhalt Election

The Saxony-Anhalt election sent a massive tremor through the European political landscape. The AfD secured a staggering 43.8% of the vote, leaving the traditional establishment parties in the dust. The numbers paint a grim picture for the center: the Christian Democratic Union (CDU), Germany’s primary conservative party, trailed at 17.2%, while the Social Democratic Party (SPD), the main force in the current national coalition government, managed a mere 9.3%. Other parties, such as the Greens (8.9%), The Left (8.6%), and the new Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance (BSW, a populist left-wing movement, 5.2%), were left fighting for scraps.

A gap of more than 26 percentage points between the first and second place is not a competition; it is a total overwhelming of the status quo. As one commentator aptly put it, "If even Germany, which has so thoroughly rejected the Nazis for 80 years, is turning to this... then we are all at a critical juncture." This result is the clearest indicator that the foundations of the German democratic system are shifting. Language once strictly taboo in public discourse has now carved out a massive territory in the hearts and minds of the electorate.

Why did this happen? To understand the phenomenon, we must look at the "debt" accumulated by the political establishment. For years, the CDU and SPD—the parties that have long dominated the national scene—have failed to protect the daily lives of ordinary citizens. While inflation soared and the economy grew unstable, politicians in Berlin seemed obsessed with outdated ideological debates and endless, unproductive infighting. Voters are no longer buying the promises of established parties. When the public feels they have nothing left to lose, they are willing to throw a "political bomb" at the system. The AfD, by skillfully combining anti-immigration rhetoric with the genuine economic alienation of the working class, provided the fuse.

The G7 Domino Effect: A Continent Moving Right

The shock of Germany’s result is compounded by the fact that this is not an isolated incident. Across Europe, and indeed among the G7—the group of the world's most advanced economies—the rise of far-right movements has become a systemic trend. In the UK, the Reform UK party is gaining significant momentum; in France, the National Rally has edged closer to the presidency; and in Italy, the Brothers of Italy party has firmly established itself as a mainstream political force.

Why are nations once considered the "cathedrals of democracy" tilting toward the far right? It is deeply tied to the despair of the middle and working classes who have lost their way amidst the highs and lows of globalization. While globalized capital brought prosperity, the fruits were concentrated among the elite, leaving the rest with nothing but job insecurity and a loss of identity. Far-right parties are stepping into this vacuum, offering a sweet, intoxicating anesthetic: "We will protect you."

This is not a coincidence. The incompetence of the establishment, which has allowed inequality to fester and polarization to widen, has provided the fertilizer for these movements. When social dissatisfaction boils over, the reflexive political instinct—seen across borders—is to blame the "other" and incite hatred against minorities to consolidate a base. If we believe we are safe from this trend, we are mistaken. The shock in Saxony-Anhalt is a warning test that every nation built on democratic foundations will soon have to face.

The Evolution of Modern Fascism

What we are witnessing is the evolution of fascism. Unlike the fascism of the past, which arrived with jackboots and flags, today’s far-right appears wrapped in the sophisticated cloak of democracy. They use the legal, democratic process to seize power, exploiting the flaws in the system to erode democracy from within. This is why the AfD’s victory is so chilling: they are weaponizing democratic institutions to stifle democracy itself.

Voters might claim they aren't looking to overthrow the state but are simply voting for survival, but the ultimate product of this logic is the resurgence of totalitarianism. History has a cruel way of repeating itself, and we are now witnessing the front lines of a new, unrecognizable face of fascism. That this is happening in Germany—the very country that has exerted the most effort to exorcise the ghosts of the Nazi era—is not just ironic; it feels like a cruel, dark farce.

Watching What Comes Next

Ultimately, the root of all these problems is inequality and existential anxiety. When politics fails to play its role—when it stops caring for the lives of its citizens—democracy loses its immune system. The victory of the AfD is the loudest alarm bell yet for the entire system.

What to watch next:

  • Policy Shifts: Observe how the mainstream parties react. Will they continue to ignore the underlying grievances, or will they attempt to co-opt parts of the far-right’s rhetoric? Either path carries significant risks for the stability of European governance.
  • National Elections: Saxony-Anhalt is a bellwether. Watch for similar trends in upcoming state and federal elections. If the "cordon sanitaire"—the informal agreement to isolate the far-right—continues to break down, we may see a fundamental shift in how governing coalitions are formed in Germany.
  • The "Establishment" Response: Look for whether the traditional parties can articulate a vision that addresses the economic insecurity of the working class without resorting to the exclusionary tactics used by the AfD. The ability to reclaim the narrative on "protection" and "identity" will be the deciding factor.

In the face of these global shifts, it is easy to feel a sense of powerlessness. But we must remember that the moment we succumb to that helplessness is the moment the values we seek to protect begin to collapse. The heavy record of these events should serve as a wake-up call. Unless politics returns to serving the people and restores the foundations of fairness and common sense, the storm currently brewing in eastern Germany will only continue to grow.
