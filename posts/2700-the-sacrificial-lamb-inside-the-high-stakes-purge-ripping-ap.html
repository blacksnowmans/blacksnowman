The Sacrificial Lamb: Inside the High-Stakes Purge Ripping Apart South Korea’s Main Opposition

November 24, 2026

Inside the halls of South Korea’s political quarters, internal party discipline is rarely just about enforcing rules. More often, it serves as a scoreboard for high-stakes factional battles. On November 23, that unspoken reality burst into public view when a member of the ethics committee inside the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition party, brought forward a startling revelation: a planned campaign to expel former party leader Han Dong-hoon was deliberately timed to coincide with a major milestone in court—the prosecution demanding the death penalty for Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the impeached former president who currently sits in prison.

The whistleblower’s account suggested that the party’s leadership did not merely seek to discipline Han for political missteps. Instead, they sought to use his high-profile expulsion as a political distraction, offering up one of the party’s most recognizable figures as a sacrificial lamb to absorb the shockwaves of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's ongoing criminal trial. The fallout from this revelation has exposed an intense, zero-sum war for control of the conservative establishment, raising troubling questions about how party mechanisms are wielded to serve factional ends.

To understand why this battle matters, one must look at the fragile state of South Korean conservatism. Following the impeachment and removal of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the People Power Party found itself pushed into the opposition bench, while President Lee Jae-myung and his ruling Democratic Party of Korea assumed power. Rather than regrouping to present a unified alternative to the Lee Jae-myung administration, the conservative party has collapsed into internal score-settling, pitting the current party leadership under Chairman Jang Dong-hyeok against reformist factions aligned with Han Dong-hoon.

A Timed Purge: Whistleblower Exposes Plan to Sacrifice Han Dong-hoon

The ethics committee whistleblower’s allegations point to a calculated effort to manipulate political timing. According to the disclosure, key figures within the party leadership had already decided on expelling Han Dong-hoon, but held back the announcement to coordinate with external events. Specifically, the expulsion was set to be released on the exact day prosecutors were scheduled to demand the death penalty for Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol in his criminal trial.

The logic behind the alleged maneuver was simple: dominate the news cycle with the explosive internal expulsion of Han, thereby muting public focus on the legal demise of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. For a party still struggling to detach its image from the disgraced former leader, sacrificing a prominent insider was viewed by some as an acceptable trade-off to manage media optics. However, relying on internal purges to smother bad news reflects a governance model driven by short-term survival rather than institutional integrity.

In response to the allegations, PPP Chairman Jang Dong-hyeok launched a swift counteroffensive. Pushing back against accusations of orchestrating a planned purge, Jang maintained that the fundamental justification for disciplining Han remained valid. He dismissed criticisms of the process as attempts to undermine legitimate party governance, arguing that the disciplinary measure was grounded in party regulations rather than personal or factional motives.

Despite Jang's defense, skepticism within conservative ranks runs deep. When disciplinary procedures within a major party become intertwined with external legal proceedings, public trust in party leadership inevitably erodes. Instead of reassuring party members, the leadership’s explanations have fueled concerns that internal mechanisms are being weaponized to eliminate rivals.

Factional Warfare: Han Dong-hoon Counterattacks the Leadership

Han Dong-hoon did not take the threat of expulsion quietly. Breaking his silence shortly after the whistleblower's revelation, Han launched a direct assault on the party leadership, demanding that Chairman Jang Dong-hyeok take responsibility for what he termed an intentional political purge. By framing the disciplinary proceedings as a targeted fabrication, Han sought to delegitimize the ethics committee’s authority and position himself as the victim of a leadership hit job.

Han’s strategy extends far beyond mere self-defense. Even while attacking Jang, Han reiterated his commitment to remaining within conservative politics, declaring his intention to return to the party fold and focus his energy on challenging President Lee Jae-myung’s governing Democratic Party. This distinction is crucial: by framing the current PPP leadership as self-absorbed factionalists engaged in internal sabotage, Han presents himself as the true opposition fighter focused on the primary political adversary.

"The core issue has not changed," PPP Chairman Jang Dong-hyeok asserted, defending the disciplinary process. Yet Han Dong-hoon countered by demanding Jang take full responsibility for what he called a "fabricated political purge."

This dual messaging allows Han to rally loyalists within the party who are increasingly dissatisfied with the leadership's handling of the opposition role. For Han, enduring an orchestrated purge offers a potent political narrative—that of a persecuted leader fighting against an entrenched establishment. If he can maintain the support of reform-minded party members, this conflict could lay the groundwork for a future comeback bid for control of the conservative movement.

Tightening the Grip: Reshaping Local Nominations

While the feud over Han Dong-hoon commands headlines, another structural battle is taking place over party rules. On November 23, the PPP leadership unveiled a major overhaul of the evaluation system used to assess incumbent provincial governors, mayors, and local district heads ahead of upcoming elections.

Under the newly announced system, opinion polls conducted among "dues-paying party members" (책임당원) will now account for 50 percent of a local official’s overall evaluation score. Officially branded by Chairman Jang Dong-hyeok as an effort to restore "party member sovereignty," the policy dramatically shifts the baseline of local governance. Previously, local chief executives were judged primarily on administrative track records, policy execution, and regional development metrics.

  • 50% Weight on Party Dues-Payers: Evaluation scores for local leaders will rely heavily on polls of committed party members rather than broader public approval.
  • Centralization of Power: Because party member lists and local chapter heads are heavily influenced by the central leadership, the new metric gives party bosses leverage over local officials.
  • Administrative Distortion: Local mayors and governors may feel compelled to prioritize internal party networking and partisan events over civic management to secure reelection nominations.

Critics within the party view this reform as a preemptive move by leadership to tame incumbent local government heads. In South Korea’s political structure, local leaders who build strong independent popularity often pose a threat to central party leadership. By making party member polls the decisive factor in performance reviews, Chairman Jang’s leadership team effectively obliges local politicians to line up behind the central office if they hope to secure party nomination for the next election cycle.

Distraction Tactics: Weaponizing Religious Conspiracy Theories

Faced with mounting pressure over internal purges and structural controversies, the PPP leadership has sought to pivot the political narrative outward. In recent public statements, Chairman Jang raised provocative allegations concerning the ruling party, claiming that as many as 43,000 members of Shincheonji—a controversial religious group frequently embroiled in South Korean political disputes—may have infiltrated the Democratic Party of Korea.

Referencing past instances where members of the religious organization allegedly caused disruptions at conservative party offices, Jang suggested that the ruling Democratic Party was now becoming a primary target for secret organizational entry. PPP spokespersons quickly echoed the claim, calling on law enforcement and investigative agencies to probe the ruling party's membership rolls for signs of religious collusion.

This aggressive outward attack is widely interpreted by political analysts as a classic deflection strategy. By introducing a highly volatile, sensational narrative involving a fringe religious sect, the PPP leadership hopes to unite conservative voters against an external threat while diverting media attention away from the whistleblower's disclosures. However, relying on unsubstantiated conspiracy theories to deflect from internal dysfunction highlights the deepening polarization and tactical exhaustion currently afflicting South Korea's main opposition.

What to Watch Next

The turmoil within the People Power Party points to a deeper crisis of governance within South Korea’s conservative ecosystem. When party ethics committees are accused of operating on political timers and administrative evaluations are restructured to reward factional loyalty, the internal mechanisms designed to maintain public trust begin to break down.

As this multi-front conflict unfolds, political observers should keep a close eye on several key indicators over the coming weeks:

  • The Final Ethics Committee Ruling: Watch whether the PPP officially executes the expulsion of Han Dong-hoon, and how his faction responds—whether through legal injunctions, internal rebellions, or forming an independent political bloc.
  • Revolt Among Local Leaders: Observe whether incumbent conservative governors and major city mayors openly resist the new 50% party-member evaluation system, potentially creating regional power centers independent of central party headquarters.
  • Fallout from Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s Legal Proceedings: As prosecutors push forward with severe sentencing demands for the imprisoned former leader, monitor whether the PPP can establish a clear identity separate from Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's legacy or if his legal crisis continues to trigger collateral damage inside the party.
  • Opposition Effectiveness: Track whether the PPP’s internal strife impairs its ability to act as a credible check against President Lee Jae-myung’s legislative agenda, potentially leaving the ruling Democratic Party with an uncontested path in the National Assembly.
