Beyond the TV Screen: How Live Commerce Is Overhauling Korean Retail and Beyond

November 15, 2026

Imagine tuning into a live video feed on your smartphone where Lee Jung-jae, the internationally acclaimed star of Squid Game, chats casually with viewers, answers live comments, and sells out products in real time. Switch to another stream, and you are transported to a quiet field in rural South Korea, where a farmer holds up a handful of freshly harvested red peppers, explaining their crop quality directly to urban apartment dwellers before packing them for immediate delivery. This is not traditional home shopping, nor is it a simple internet infomercial. It is the new reality of South Korea's live commerce ecosystem, where retail, entertainment, and digital media have fused into a single high-stakes battleground.

What began as a convenient alternative during mobile shopping's early days has evolved into a radical restructuring of the retail supply chain. Across South Korea, the shift away from legacy media is accelerating at a breakneck pace. Reports on the state of the industry reveal that live commerce is no longer merely moving television broadcasts onto smaller screens; it is fundamentally rewriting how goods are designed, marketed, sold, and delivered, extending its reach from corporate boardrooms in Seoul to agricultural fields and even across borders into Southeast Asia.

From TV Hosts to Co-Creators: The New Celebrity and Influencer Playbook

For years, the formula for video-based selling was predictable: a charismatic host stood on a polished set, delivering a rehearsed pitch for a pre-manufactured product. That model is rapidly dissolving. Today, content creators and digital influencers are no longer brought in at the final stage just to hold a product in front of a camera. Instead, major retail companies are building deep, collaborative partnerships with creators from the very inception of a product.

In this evolving playbook, creators participate in product design, brand positioning, and even oversight of the delivery process. Retailers are tapping directly into the established trust and fandom that creators have cultivated with their audiences. Contracts have shifted away from one-off appearance fees or flat sponsorship deals toward profit-sharing arrangements and joint-brand launches. For consumers, buying a product co-created or vetted by a creator they follow daily feels less like a commercial transaction and more like supporting a shared story.

A-List Star Power and High-Volume Conversion

As the revenue potential of live streams has soared, A-list celebrities who once focused exclusively on television, film, and high-end print ads are flocking to mobile commerce platforms. The arrival of top stars like Lee Jung-jae on live streams has created broadcast events capable of generating hundreds of thousands of dollars in revenue in a matter of minutes. Industry insiders report that a single successful live stream can match or exceed an entire month's worth of traditional sales for a brand.

Retailers are willing to pay top dollar for celebrity appearances because the return on investment extends far beyond the live broadcast itself. A celebrity live stream serves a dual purpose:

  • Instant traffic spikes: Massive concurrent viewer counts boost real-time brand awareness and drive immediate checkout conversion.
  • Short-form amplification: The most engaging moments of a live stream are quickly edited into short-form video clips, which circulate endlessly across social media platforms long after the stream ends.

By blending celebrity interaction with instant messaging, live streams turn shopping into an interactive mix of fan meeting and entertainment show, allowing brands to maintain a premium image while capturing immediate sales volume.

The Survival Crisis of TV Home Shopping Networks

While mobile live commerce thrives, South Korea's legacy home shopping networks—long the giants of direct-to-consumer video retail—are facing a existential crisis. The phrase "Who buys things on TV anymore?" reflects a painful economic reality for the industry. Plunging television viewership combined with steadily rising transmission fees charged by cable and satellite operators has created a severe squeeze on profit margins for the country's top three home shopping operators.

In response, these major players are conducting major structural overhauls to migrate their core business models to mobile platforms. This transformation is not just about broadcasting the same show on an app; it requires discarding the traditional 60-minute TV format in favor of nimble, 24-hour mobile operations focused heavily on 1- to 2-minute short-form commerce clips and real-time interactive streams.

The shift in legacy retail: Traditional home shopping giants that once dominated living room screens are now forced to rebuild their entire media architecture around mobile-first formats to survive falling viewership and rising broadcast costs.

These legacy operators do bring distinct advantages to the mobile arena: decades of production expertise, robust logistics networks, and verified vendor pipelines. As they enter the mobile live commerce space, a fierce competitive battle with agile, native digital platforms is underway.

Seasonal Fashion Blitzes and Direct Branding

The operational shift is clearly visible in how fashion brands execute seasonal promotions. As cold weather approaches and the year-end shopping season kicks off, brands are using live commerce to bypass traditional distributor markups and sell directly to consumers. For example, National Geographic Kids—a major player in South Korea's premium children's wear and outdoor market—launched a major winter promotion running through November 18, offering discounts of up to 36% on new winter apparel lines exclusively through live commerce streams.

Live streams have proven exceptionally effective for fashion retail because they overcome the primary downside of online shopping: uncertainty over fit, fabric, and texture. During a live broadcast, hosts can respond directly to chat prompts, trying on different sizes or showing how materials move up close. For parents buying children's winter coats, this real-time feedback provides the confidence needed to complete a purchase immediately.

Harvesting Digital Dollars in the Korean Countryside

The live commerce transformation is not confined to urban fashion centers or corporate studios. In the Korean countryside, agricultural producers are using smartphone streams to fundamentally alter the rural supply chain. In farm fields across the country—such as recent streams set directly in red pepper harvests—farmers are picking up their smartphones and going live straight from the soil.

By broadcasting directly from the farm, producers bypass multi-tiered wholesale networks that historically took a large cut of agricultural profits. Consumers receive freshly harvested produce sent directly to their homes at fair prices, while gaining full transparency into how their food is grown. The unpolished, authentic style of farmers explaining their crop yields directly to the camera creates a level of trust that traditional grocery marketing rarely achieves.

Upward Mobility Through Regional Education

To help rural producers and local small businesses navigate this digital shift, local governments and educational institutions across South Korea are investing heavily in digital media training. Gyeongbuk Science University, located in North Gyeongsang Province, recently launched a specialized digital commerce training program tailored for local farmers, small business owners, and prospective entrepreneurs.

The program provides hands-on instruction covering practical skills needed for modern mobile retail:

  • Technical operation: Mastering mobile camera setups, lighting, and streaming application interfaces.
  • Broadcast communication: Structuring concise product pitches and engaging naturally with real-time viewer comments.
  • Digital marketing: Managing stream promotions and retaining audience attention through interactive events.

By empowering local producers to manage their own digital storefronts, regional universities and municipalities are building self-sustaining economic model that keeps a higher percentage of farm revenues within rural communities.

Exporting the Stream: The Southeast Asian Ripple Effect

The live commerce playbook refined in South Korea is rapidly becoming a blueprint for agricultural and retail modernization across Southeast Asia, with Vietnam emerging as a major hub for livestreaming innovation. In cities like Hanoi and throughout regional provinces, local governments are pairing agricultural supply chain digitization with live video sales.

A driving force behind this shift is Vietnam's national "One Commune One Product" (OCOP) initiative, a government policy aimed at developing and promoting local specialized goods. OCOP-certified agricultural items and regional crafts are now featured heavily on e-commerce live streams, enabling rural producers to sell directly to urban consumers in Hanoi and beyond. Traditional five-day rural markets and physical stalls are increasingly being supplemented—and in some cases replaced—by mobile screens that connect farm communities with buyers nationwide.

This rapid digital transition has created strong demand for media expertise and professional training in the region. Media consultants across Vietnam are stepping in to teach agricultural producers professional production techniques, framing strategies, and branding methods. In bustling urban hubs like Hanoi's Nguyen Hue Pedestrian Street, interactive physical pop-up events are now regularly paired with live mobile broadcasts, demonstrating how offline foot traffic and online streaming networks can reinforce each other.

What to Watch Next

As live commerce transitions from an emerging trend into the dominant framework for video retail, several key developments will indicate where the market is headed next:

  • Legacy home shopping survival metrics: Watch whether traditional home shopping networks can cut their reliance on television fast enough to offset rising transmission fees with mobile short-form revenues.
  • Creator equity structures: Monitor how contract models evolve as top creators push for equity stakes and co-ownership of products rather than simple commission splits.
  • Cross-border streaming infrastructure: Pay attention to how Southeast Asian markets adapt live commerce training programs to scale regional agricultural exports directly to international consumers.
