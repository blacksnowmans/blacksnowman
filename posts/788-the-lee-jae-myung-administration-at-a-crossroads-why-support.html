The Lee Jae-myung Administration at a Crossroads: Why Support is Collapsing

September 16, 2026

Walking through the streets of Seoul today, the mood is palpably heavy. There is a sense of disbelief—not just among casual observers, but within the very base that propelled Lee Jae-myung to the presidency. What began as an ambitious mandate for reform is now adrift, and the political implications extend far beyond mere partisan squabbling; they suggest a fundamental disconnect between the current administration and the future of South Korean society.

The latest data from the Korea Society Opinion Institute (KSOI) provides a sobering look at this reality. President Lee’s approval rating sits at a precarious 35.9%, while his disapproval rating has climbed to 59.4%. These are not just arbitrary statistics; they are a direct reflection of a growing, visceral disappointment among the public as promises to improve daily life remain unfulfilled.

A Harsh Report Card from the Electorate

The Cooling Sentiment in Seoul and Among the Youth

The most alarming trend in the KSOI survey is the sharp decline in support among the 18–29 age demographic, which has dropped to 28.3%. This is not a temporary dip; it is a clear indicator that the youth—a constituency crucial to any progressive platform—feel entirely alienated by the current policy direction.

Even more concerning is the geographic breakdown. Disapproval ratings in the metropolitan areas of Seoul (61.5%) and Gyeonggi-Incheon (61.3%) are staggeringly high. When a government loses its grip on the capital region—the economic and political heart of the nation—it loses its governing momentum. With local by-elections for the Seoul mayoralty on the horizon, members of the Democratic Party of Korea (the main liberal opposition and the party Lee represents) are openly questioning their chances of survival. The old tactics of defending the administration at all costs are no longer working; the voters have sent a clear warning.

Party Support in Tandem Decline

The crisis is not confined to the President; the Democratic Party’s own approval rating has slipped to 36.3%, putting it neck-and-neck with the People Power Party, South Korea's main conservative party (33.2%). This structural decline signals that the public views this as a failure of the entire political apparatus rather than just a personal shortcoming of the President. The lack of policy consistency and the perception that the government is always one step behind have transformed disappointment into resignation.

Core Findings at a Glance:

  • President Lee's approval rating has fallen to 35.9%, while disapproval has reached 59.4%.
  • Traditional support bases, particularly in the metropolitan areas and among the youth, are rapidly fracturing.
  • The Democratic Party's support is sliding alongside the administration, indicating a systemic loss of momentum.

The Controversy Over Justice Minister Nominee Kim Seung-won

A Referendum on Governance

The public opposition to the appointment of Kim Seung-won as the Minister of Justice stands at 52.1%. To dismiss this as a mere debate over one individual’s credentials would be a grave error. This figure represents a broader "slap on the wrist" from a public frustrated with the administration's personnel choices and policy priorities. When negative sentiment doubles that of the positive (25.1%), it is clear that even the administration's base is confused by the direction being taken.

The core of this frustration lies in the perception that the government is catering to the wrong interests. Many supporters are baffled by the appointment of figures who held significant power during the previous conservative administration of Yoon Suk-yeol, or by the lack of speed in implementing promised reforms to the prosecution service. When those who once bolstered the old guard are promoted, the public asks: Is this the change we voted for?

The Danger of Supporter Silence

Perhaps even more painful is the current state of the base. Many supporters have fallen into a state of passive apathy, choosing not to defend or explain the administration's actions as they did in the past. While blind loyalty is never the answer, total silence or negligence can encourage arrogance within the halls of power. By failing to hold the government accountable, supporters have allowed the administration to drift further away from the urgent tasks of judicial reform and fair personnel management.

The Cycle of Stifling Dissent

The recent turmoil surrounding the prosecution—including the blatant use of personnel assignments to silence dissenting prosecutors—is a classic example of "muzzling" critics. Moving critical voices to peripheral posts and filling key roles with loyalists will not bring about judicial independence. The public demands a fair system that serves the law, not a set of tools to protect those in power. If this trend continues, the government is effectively sabotaging its own legitimacy.

The Vacuum of Achievement

The "Nothing Done" Verdict

Most shocking of all is that 40.6% of respondents in the KSOI survey stated that the government has "no accomplishments." While foreign policy and economic recovery were mentioned by smaller segments of the population, the fact that nearly half of the nation cannot identify a single success is a staggering indictment. The administration has failed to prioritize the issues that matter most: the immediate struggle for economic relief and the historic mandate to reform the nation's justice system.

A Betrayal of Reform

The most criticized areas are national integration (21.4%) and economic recovery (16.9%). The promise to purge remnants of the previous administration's "insurrectionist" elements—a core tenet of the current government's founding narrative—is increasingly viewed as hollow. When voters see prosecutors who were once celebrated by the previous conservative regime rising through the ranks, the sense of betrayal is absolute. Supporters expected a clean sweep and decisive legislative action on the proposed Investigation and Prosecution agencies; instead, they see bureaucratic infighting.

What Lies Ahead

President Lee Jae-myung remains in a position where he can still alter the trajectory, but the time for half-measures has passed. To recover, the administration must stop the "deceptive" politics that have characterized the last year. If the government cannot move forward with its reform agenda, it must be honest with the public about why, rather than offering opaque justifications that only fuel the feeling of being misled.

The path forward for the citizenry is clear: the public must not tire of demanding accountability. Democracy is not a finished product; it is a process that requires constant, vocal participation. As the saying goes, it is the duty of the "awakened citizen" to use their influence to steer the government back toward its original promises. Whether through vocal criticism or persistent pressure to prioritize the right personnel, the public remains the ultimate owner of the nation’s political direction. The administration may be at a crossroads, but the power to determine which path it takes still lies in the hands of the people.
