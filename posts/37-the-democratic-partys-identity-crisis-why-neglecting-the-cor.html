The Democratic Party’s Identity Crisis: Why Neglecting the "Core" is Backfiring

September 24, 2026

Imagine a captain so focused on the horizon that they fail to notice the crew is jumping ship. This is the current reality for South Korea’s Democratic Party (DP), the country’s main liberal opposition party. While leadership speaks of "expanding the political tent" to capture moderate, pragmatic voters, the very supporters who form the party’s bedrock are feeling increasingly alienated, silenced, and disregarded.

The recent discourse surrounding the party has been deeply unsettling. In a recent analysis on the YouTube channel Political Arcade, editor Kim Seung-hyun highlighted a paradox: in the name of chasing "moderate, practical conservatism," the party is systematically excluding its core base. It is a high-stakes gamble—trading the support of the "house rabbits" (loyal, established supporters) in the hopes of catching the "mountain rabbits" (undecided, moderate voters)—that currently shows no signs of succeeding.

The Illusion of Expansion and the 30% Trap

The latest polling data from Gallup Korea serves as a sobering wake-up call. The Democratic Party’s approval rating has slipped into the 30% range for the first time since last October. Typically, a party experiences a "convention effect"—a boost in popularity following major party events or leadership shifts. Instead, the DP has seen its numbers trend downward.

Under the leadership of Representative Kim Min-seok, the party has prioritized a "moderate-conservative expansion" strategy. However, this shift has created an immediate rift. The logic seems to be that if the party moves toward the center, the center will follow. But political pragmatism requires a solid foundation; without a united base, the party appears to be wandering aimlessly, losing its traditional supporters without attracting the moderate voters it covets.

This internal friction erupted during a recent party workshop. When Kim labeled former representative Jung Chung-rae as a "denier of the middle ground," Jung publicly expressed his indignation, stating that his name was being used as a mere tool for a flawed political argument. What was intended to be a rallying event instead served as a public airing of deep-seated factional grievances.

The Silent Exodus of the Core Base

The most alarming statistic cited by Kim Seung-hyun is the collapse of "core" support. As recently as May, the response rate from the party's core base sat between 40% and 50%. Recent surveys show that number has plummeted to 17–18%.

Internal analysis within the party admits that this drop is not purely ideological—it is a reaction to tangible pain. Failed policies, particularly in the real estate sector and the unstable stock market, have hit ordinary citizens hard. Lawmakers have begun to voice their frustration, with Representative Kim Young-jin noting that "tax policies were prioritized over supply, and our financial preparations were insufficient." While the party promised to focus on people's livelihoods, many citizens feel that the actual policy outcomes—a "tax bomb" and unprepared financial regulations—have only squeezed them further.

Despite this, the leadership remains preoccupied with internal power struggles rather than addressing the economic anxieties of the public. As Supreme Council member Park Sun-won warned, "Even if exports and stock prices look good on paper, if the people are saying 'Why is my life so hard?', the government and the ruling party must take responsibility."

Trapped in a "Metaverse" of Echo Chambers

Perhaps the most chilling observation is the similarity between the current DP and the late-stage administration of the Yoon Suk-yeol government. Both appear to be suffering from a detachment from reality. Kim Seung-hyun described this as being "trapped in a metaverse"—an environment where the leadership only hears its own feedback loops and views any objective criticism as a hostile attack.

This insularity is exacerbated by modern media. According to the Korea Press Foundation, over 60% of South Koreans rely on YouTube for current affairs, with algorithms heavily dictating what information they consume. This fosters "filter bubbles" and "echo chambers" where partisan logic is amplified and rational dissent is labeled as "heresy."

Those who dare to offer critical advice from within—like the well-known writer Rhyu Si-min—are frequently targeted by these online mobs. The tragedy, as Kim Seung-hyun notes, is that the political language and strategies used to solidify power have now become a cage. Even those who once practiced flexible politics find themselves unable to pivot because their own supporters, radicalized by algorithm-driven media, treat any hint of compromise as a betrayal.

What to Watch Next

The "golden time" for the Democratic Party to pivot back to reality is rapidly closing. The party is now facing a structural crisis that could severely damage its prospects in the upcoming local elections and beyond.

  • The 30% Floor: Watch if the party’s approval rating continues to crater or if they can stabilize their base by addressing bread-and-butter economic issues.
  • Youth Engagement: Recent polls indicate that 70% of voters in their 20s and 30s believe the Democratic Party is out of touch. Whether the party makes a genuine effort to engage this demographic will be a key indicator of its long-term viability.
  • The Rise of the "Right-to-Vote" Members: As traditional loyalists feel sidelined, there is a growing movement for individual party members to take a more active role in party administration. If these "rights-holders" (party members who pay dues) begin to organize from the bottom up, they may force the leadership to abandon their exclusionary tactics.

History has repeatedly shown that political powers that look down on their people—or attempt to "teach" them rather than serve them—inevitably face a reckoning. The Democratic Party’s greatest threat today is not the opposing party, but the 17% of core supporters who have quietly turned away. If the leadership continues to mistake silence for loyalty, they may soon find that their "metaverse" of power has left them with no support at all in the real world.
