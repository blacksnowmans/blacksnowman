From National Hero to Public Scapegoat: The Fall and Return of South Korea’s Top Dog Trainer

October 10, 2026

When Kang Hyung-wook sat down in front of a YouTube camera to share his side of the story, the atmosphere was heavy with a quiet exhaustion. For months, South Korea’s most famous dog trainer had vanished from public view, weathering a storm of fierce public backlash and devastating allegations. To understand Kang’s situation is to understand a darker side of South Korea’s hyper-connected, high-speed digital culture—one where public figures are elevated to national heroes overnight, only to be dragged through the mud with equal speed when controversy strikes.

For years, Kang was universally recognized as South Korea's ultimate dog whisperer. Through hit television shows and his own media presence, he taught the nation how to understand their pets, shifting the country's pet-care culture fundamentally. But in a matter of days, that reputation evaporated. As the legal dust finally clears, his journey back to the public eye offers a sobering look at the human cost of South Korea's digital witch hunts.

An Unlikely Trio: Finding Solace Among Icons

In his recent television appearances alongside prominent Korean entertainers like Lee Young-ja and Pak Se-ri—the legendary South Korean LPGA golfer and national hero—Kang has slowly begun to drop his guard. Reflecting on their initial meetings, Kang admits he was completely starstruck and deeply intimidated. When they first began working together, he was stiff, overly polite, and barely able to make eye contact.

Yet, spending time with cultural heavyweights like Lee Young-ja and Pak Se-ri eventually thawed his icy exterior. Through banter and shared vulnerability, Kang began to relax, revealing a side of himself that viewers had rarely seen behind his strict professional persona. Joking about Pak Se-ri’s stern first impression—comparing her initial silence to a tough, unyielding guard dog—Kang noted that underneath her intimidating exterior lies the warm, deeply empathetic heart of a poodle. These interactions were more than just lighthearted variety show banter; they were the first signs of emotional recovery for a man who had spent months isolated from the world.

Shedding Dogmatic Training for True Empathy

Kang’s path to becoming South Korea's top canine expert was built on unyielding authority. Early in his career, he approached dog training with rigid rules and absolute dogmatism. If a dog misbehaved, he believed there was a strict, scientific protocol that must be followed without exception, and he frequently clashed with owners who failed to meet his exacting standards.

Over time, however, hands-on experience humbled him. He recalls a case where a dog repeatedly bit its owner whenever she tried to wake up. While a textbook approach would categorize the dog as aggressive and problematic, Kang later discovered that the dog was actually smelling the early stages of breast cancer in the sleeping owner, desperately trying to alert her to the danger. Encounters like this taught Kang that standardized rules and rigid training frameworks cannot account for the complex, emotional bonds between animals and humans. Stepping away from the belief that he held all the answers allowed him to become not just a better trainer, but a more compassionate observer of life.

The Breaking Point: Navigating Scandal and Public Scrutiny

Before the controversy engulfed him, Kang hosted a documentary-style program called The Solitary Trainer, a grueling production that required him to travel across South Korea. Though the hours were brutal—often starting at dawn and ending late at night—the experience fundamentally shifted his perspective on humanity. Originally drawn to dogs rather than people, Kang found himself deeply moved by the quiet struggles of everyday citizens, from overworked long-haul truck drivers to families pouring their unspoken anxieties into their pets.

That empathy was tested to its limits when allegations of workplace harassment surfaced regarding his company. Overnight, the public turned on him. Kang recalls a living hell where he could not step outside his home, and drones hovered above his roof for days. Media outlets and online commentators consumed unverified accusations with relish, eager to dismantle his moral standing. Seeking a brief respite for his deeply distressed wife, Kang ventured into a local cafe disguised in a hat, sunglasses, and a mask. When the cafe owner recognized them and handed over a post-it note with a warm message of support alongside a cookie, it nearly broke him.

Pak Se-ri, who watched the ordeal unfold from close quarters over a year, admitted she was frequently frustrated by Kang’s refusal to aggressively defend himself. While she urged him to issue rapid denials, Kang understood that the weight of the moment was far larger than his own ego. His primary objective wasn't winning a public relations war; it was shielding his family from harm on the streets.

The Weight of Fatherhood and Legal Vindication

When asked what the most difficult moment of the entire ordeal was, Kang answered without hesitation: the moment he realized he had no right to cry. Even as the world collapsed around him and hostile criticism poured in from every direction, he had to suppress his own grief. As a father and the head of his household, his singular duty was keeping his family safe from collateral damage.

That personal endurance was eventually matched by a legal reality check. In February 2025, the Northern Gyeonggi Provincial Police Agency closed the investigation into allegations that Kang and his wife had improperly accessed employee messenger logs, ruling the case "no charges." Investigators determined that their actions fell strictly within legitimate administrative rights. Although the legal victory came too late to save Bodeum Company, which had already shut its doors, and could not erase a year of forced absence from television, it officially cleared his name.

What to Watch Next

As Kang returns to television—notably through Channel A's variety programming—and slowly rebuilds his digital footprint, the broader cultural fallout of his ordeal remains an open question in South Korea. Observers of media and society should monitor several key developments:

  • The Evolution of Cancel Culture Accountability: Watch whether South Korean media platforms and online communities begin practicing greater restraint when unverified workplace allegations emerge against public figures, or if the appetite for rapid online trials remains unchecked.
  • The Shift in Pet Media and Education: Observe how Kang’s newly adopted philosophy of empathetic, relationship-based training influences the broader South Korean pet industry, which has historically leaned toward rigid behavioral correction techniques.
  • Kang’s Corporate and Public Comeback: Track the trajectory of his return to mainstream broadcasting and digital content creation to see if public trust can be fully restored in the wake of cleared legal charges.

There are truths that only become visible after the storm clears. Kang Hyung-wook’s ordeal serves as a stark reminder of how quickly society is willing to tear down a public figure, and how much endurance it takes to survive the wreckage. As he steps back onto the walking paths with dogs and their owners, his story leaves behind a lingering question for a digital age: before we pick up stones to cast at others, do we ever pause to measure the weight of the lives we are attempting to crush?
