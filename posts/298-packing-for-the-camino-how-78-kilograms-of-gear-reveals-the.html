Packing for the Camino: How 7.8 Kilograms of Gear Reveals the Weight We Carry

September 07, 2026

Ten hours before departure, surrounded by a chaotic sprawl of gear across the living room floor, a familiar wave of doubt sets in. Staring down the pile, the thought echoes: Is this really the weight I am about to carry across Spain? Travelers often set out on ancient pilgrimage routes like the Camino de Santiago seeking to empty their minds and shed the burdens of modern life. Yet the process of packing reveals that the endeavor is not about effortless liberation. It is a grueling, psychological battle of selection, exclusion, and compromise.

They say the weight of your pack is the weight of your life. Every single item debated—tossed aside, picked up, and reconsidered—is stained with personal anxieties and a persistent fear of being unprepared. The journey ahead spans roughly 800 kilometers (about 500 miles), starting from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port in the French Pyrenees in mid-September and winding all the way to Santiago de Compostela in northwestern Spain. Documenting the preparation exposes the thin line between practical survival and emotional baggage.

The Philosophy of Base Weight: Packs, Sleeping Bags, and Shoes

Choosing a backpack is the first major psychological hurdle. For this trek, an old 35-liter Montbell pack purchased during college theater auditions gets a second life. Despite the years, the waist belt and shoulder straps remain remarkably sturdy. Reusing gear already sitting around the house rather than buying shiny new equipment is a core strategy to keep volume in check. The sleeping bag is a 700-gram fleece model from Jack Wolfskin. It is older, and the drawstrings shed tiny black flakes of fabric, but mathematically, nothing else matches its warmth-to-weight ratio. The plan is simple: use it until it falls apart, then discard it.

One of the most common regrets among returning Korean pilgrims is bringing an excessively heavy backpack. Unnecessary weight is the primary culprit behind the punishing muscle strains and debilitating blisters that plague walkers during their first week. Shaving off a kilogram does not happen all at once; it is accumulated in agonizing 200-gram increments. Without ruthless microscopic management, a pack breaches the 10-kilogram mark before the walker even leaves the first mountain pass.

Footwear presents an even tougher dilemma. Crocs are universally recommended as essential camp shoes, yet they ultimately fail the cut because they are simply too bulky to strap efficiently to a tightly packed bag. Instead, a pair of Nike sandals with adjustable foam straps takes their place—a bare-minimum insurance policy for days when blisters swell so badly that hiking boots become unwearable. Choosing the right shoes can make or break the entire pilgrimage. Countless veteran journals tell the same cautionary tale of feet destroyed by brand-new boots that were never properly broken in. Toe socks (Injinji) are added to the arsenal to reduce friction between digits—a minor investment that borders on biological survival.

Layering for Climate Shifts and Staying Clothed

Weather in the mountains can turn viciously cold and wet, even in early autumn. The outer shell is a heavy North Face Gore-Tex windbreaker. While bulky, it eliminates the need to fuss with a clumsy poncho during light drizzles. Meanwhile, a light Uniqlo UV-protection windbreaker gets left behind. Saving 200 grams here and there is the only way to keep the overall load manageable.

Lower-body clothing requires strict discipline. A pair of Snow Peak jogger pants, Uniqlo parachute pants, and a four-year-old pair of Lululemon leggings make the cut. Because laundry is a daily ritual in shared pilgrim hostels, fast-drying fabrics are non-negotiable. The Uniqlo parachute pants double as lightweight sleepwear and trail pants, earning their keep through sheer versatility. For the upper body, Nike Dri-FIT shirts paired with long sleeves featuring thumbholes protect against the blistering Spanish sun. Conventional wisdom on the trail dictates that covering up completely keeps a walker cooler than exposing bare skin to direct UV rays.

Thermal regulation is handled by a long-sleeved Uniqlo Ultra Light Down jacket, packed in response to reports of unseasonably cold snaps in the mountains. While a vest version might weigh slightly less, abandoning warmth is not an option when temperatures plummet at high altitudes. A wide-brimmed hat with a wind strap and a multi-purpose tube scarf round out the defense against the elements, alongside robust knee and ankle braces designed to protect tendons on grueling descents.

The Paradox of the Pilgrim’s Pack
The most common error found in veteran journals is the compulsion to pack for every conceivable worst-case scenario. In reality, local shops and pilgrim hostels provide most everyday necessities. As the saying goes, the true weight in the pack is not physical gear—it is internal fear. Stripping away redundant items is the mandatory first trial every traveler must pass before taking their first step.

Hygiene, Shared Dormitories, and First Aid

Managing personal care on the trail demands ruthless efficiency. Toiletries are minimized through the use of shampoo bars rather than liquid bottles, cutting down weight while protecting hair from drying out in harsh local water. Skincare is streamlined into a precise count of moisturizing cream samples—just enough to get by without carrying heavy jars. A basic first-aid kit is packed with painkillers, anti-diarrheal medication, sports tape, and sewing needles to drain and thread blisters.

Albergues—the communal pilgrim hostels—frequently house anywhere from four to twenty strangers in a single dormitory. Because they rarely provide towels, slippers, or bedding, a lightweight microfiber towel and a trusty set of laundry pins are mandatory. Every evening brings a war against sweat and grime. Without a reliable way to wash and dry clothes overnight, morale crumbles fast.

Security is another puzzle solved through compartmentalization. Passports are stripped of leather covers and sealed in plastic zip bags to shave grams. Cash is kept to a bare minimum, relying instead on travel-friendly debit cards distributed across three separate, hidden locations on the body. A lightweight cable lock provides peace of mind when chaining a pack down inside a crowded sleeping hall.

The Final Count: 7.8 Kilograms and the Road Ahead

In the end, trekking poles—upgraded from a heavy generic pair to a collapsible titanium model—and a modest power bank complete the kit. Every single item has been scrutinized, weighed, and justified against the reality of hauling it across half a continent.

The scale settles on 7.8 kilograms (about 17 pounds). It is far heavier than the idealistic 5-kilogram goal set at the very beginning, yet it represents a hard boundary between crippling anxiety and rational preparation. The yellow arrows and stone markers of Spain await. What remains to be seen is whether the journey will test the endurance of the body, or simply reveal how much more of the load can be abandoned along the way.
