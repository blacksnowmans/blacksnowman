A Deepening Rift in South Korea’s Ruling Party: Inside the Factional Warfare Threatening the Lee Jae-myung Administration

November 17, 2026

On the morning of November 16, a heavy silence fell over the meeting room of the Democratic Party of Korea, South Korea’s ruling liberal party. Inside, members of the Supreme Council—the party's top decision-making body—sat stiffly beside one another. Among them was Choi Min-hee, a high-ranking Supreme Council member who had been conspicuously absent from party proceedings for three straight days amid mounting political controversy.

As press cameras flashed, Choi stood up, stepped in front of the room, and bent at the waist in a deep, 90-degree bow. In South Korean political culture, such a dramatic gesture of contrition is designed to signal absolute humility and a desire to reset narrative momentum. Addressing the room, Choi offered a formal public apology to President Lee Jae-myung and the party's rank-and-file members, attributing her controversial past statements to a simple "lapse in memory."

Yet, if Choi hoped a bow would put an end to the crisis, the atmosphere in the room suggested otherwise. Seated right next to her, Party Leader Kim Min-seok kept his expression stony and cold. Throughout the entire proceeding, the two figures avoided eye contact. What was meant to be a moment of reconciliation instead exposed a deepening, open schism at the heart of South Korea's governing party—a conflict that is rapidly spiraling into full-scale factional warfare.

A Bow of Apology That Failed to Thaw the Ice

The controversy surrounding Choi Min-hee stems from recent remarks that appeared to contradict key administration positions, sparking debate over whether her statements undermined the political identity and authority of President Lee Jae-myung. Attempting to repair the damage, Choi told the Supreme Council on November 16 that her previous remarks—which included stances touching on anti-impeachment rhetoric and other sensitive political friction points—were the result of a memory lapse rather than an intentional challenge to the leadership.

However, her explanation fell flat among party insiders and political observers alike. Rather than smoothing over the rift, Choi's public apology was met with immediate skepticism across the political spectrum. Veteran lawmaker Park Ji-won noted in a broadcast interview that disciplinary action against Choi was essentially "unavoidable," emphasizing that her comments had inflicted serious damage on the identity of the Lee Jae-myung administration.

The cold reception inside the meeting room underscored how deep the distrust has grown. Major Korean news outlets captured the awkward dynamics: a party leader and a Supreme Council member sitting side-by-side yet looking in completely different directions. The formal apology lasted only moments before the meeting devolved back into sharp verbal sparring, demonstrating that superficial displays of unity could no longer paper over the governing party's structural divisions.

Referral to the Ethics Tribunal and the Push for Strict Discipline

Rather than accepting Choi’s apology as a closing statement, Party Leader Kim Min-seok took decisive action to enforce formal discipline. During the meeting, Kim explicitly confirmed that he had instructed party leadership to refer Choi’s case directly to the Ethics Tribunal, the independent internal watchdog responsible for reviewing member misconduct and enforcing party rules.

Kim emphasized that the party must strictly follow its constitution and bylaws, insisting that political compromises should not overwrite established procedures. By refusing to let Choi settle the matter with a personal apology, Kim sent a clear signal that the party leadership intends to draw a hard line against internal insubordination that threatens the administration’s momentum.

To further reinforce internal discipline, Kim outlined plans to toughen penalties for party members who attempt to evade internal reviews or engage in misconduct. Under proposals currently being reviewed, individuals who cheat or refuse to cooperate with internal audits could face election disqualification periods extended from the current 5 years to 10 years. Kim called on all party members to respect the independence and upcoming rulings of the Ethics Tribunal, which could issue its findings as early as next week.

Pro-Lee vs. Pro-Chung: Factional Warfare Erupts in Public

The decision to pursue formal sanctions quickly ignited an explosive debate between competing internal groups, primarily pitting the pro-Lee Jae-myung faction (allied with the president and party leadership) against the pro-Chung faction. Far from backing down, Choi used her speaking time at the Supreme Council meeting to launch a counterattack, demanding that the party also launch a thorough investigation into separate allegations involving ties to the Shincheonji religious group.

This counter-demand brought factional hostility out into the open. Members of the pro-Lee faction argued that Choi’s original statements directly undermined the legitimacy of the Lee Jae-myung government and demanded strict, uncompromising punishment. In response, members of the pro-Chung faction pushed back, accusing Party Leader Kim Min-seok of lacking empathy and arguing that it was unfair to relentlessly pursue a member who had already offered a public apology for a memory lapse. The exchanges became so heated that members openly lamented how embarrassing the spectacle had become for a ruling party.

The conflict has also spread beyond the Supreme Council and into the broader legislative body. "The Mincho," an association of first-term Democratic Party lawmakers, began organizing a joint statement calling for severe disciplinary measures and demanding that Choi take full responsibility. This move prompted pushback from other lawmakers. Representative Han Min-soo publicly urged The Mincho to halt its signature drive immediately, warning that collective pressure from lawmakers threatens to compromise the independent judgment of the Ethics Tribunal.

To manage the growing unrest among legislative ranks, Party Leader Kim Min-seok has scheduled a luncheon meeting with first-term lawmakers for November 18, where the disciplinary issue and the petition drive are expected to dominate discussions.

Chat Room Leaks, Legal Troubles, and Sinking Approval Ratings

As factional tensions peaked, the dispute took an even uglier turn following an investigative report by Sisa Tapa NEWS. The outlet revealed leaked messages from a private group chat titled the "Lee Jae-myung · Kim Min-seok Party-Gov Team." According to the report, chat participants used extreme language directed at Choi Min-hee, including death threats stating she "should be killed and eliminated," accompanied by graphic sexual insults.

The leak of these abusive messages sent shockwaves through the party, igniting fierce backlash from female lawmakers and pro-Chung members. The revelation that supporters or allies within a leadership-aligned chat room engaged in violent personal attacks against a sitting Supreme Council member has severely compromised the moral high ground of the leadership's disciplinary push, adding a layer of scandal to an already chaotic situation.

Compounding Choi’s political isolation is a separate legal challenge. Outside the party's internal proceedings, civic groups have filed police complaints against Choi over a past controversial remark in which she referred to critics as a "pack of hyenas."

Unsurprisingly, the public fallout from this internal warfare has been swift. According to recent polling reported by MBC News, public approval ratings for the Democratic Party of Korea have plummeted to an all-time low. For a ruling party tasked with driving national legislation and supporting the president's policy agenda, the combination of internal harassment scandals, public shouting matches, and falling voter confidence represents a major political crisis.

What to Watch Next

As the Democratic Party of Korea grapples with its severe internal rift, several key developments over the coming days will determine whether the leadership can restore order or if the factional divide will deepen further:

  • The Ethics Tribunal Ruling: Expected as early as next week, the tribunal's decision on Choi Min-hee’s disciplinary status will serve as a critical bellwether. A harsh penalty could provoke stronger resistance from opposition factions, while a lenient ruling could draw fire from pro-Lee loyalists.
  • The November 18 First-Term Lawmakers Meeting: Party Leader Kim Min-seok’s luncheon with first-term lawmakers will test whether the leadership can quell the signature petition drive led by "The Mincho" and regain control over junior legislators.
  • Investigations into the Group Chat Leaks: Watch how party leadership and law enforcement address the leaked "Party-Gov Team" group chat messages containing death threats and sexual harassment, as failing to address the scandal could further alienate key voter bases and internal blocs.
  • Legislative and Approval Rating Trajectory: With party approval ratings hitting record lows, observers will be watching whether the ruling party can refocus on parliamentary business and government policy, or if prolonged internal strife will continue to paralyze the administration's momentum.
