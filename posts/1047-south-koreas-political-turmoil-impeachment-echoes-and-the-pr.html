South Korea’s Political Turmoil: Impeachment Echoes and the Prosecution’s Budget Scandal

October 13, 2026

The South Korean political landscape is currently dominated by two volatile issues: the renewed calls for presidential impeachment—a term that once again grips the halls of the National Assembly—and a mounting scandal involving the opaque "special activity funds" and operational expenses of the nation's prosecution service. For the average citizen, watching these developments unfold feels like witnessing a repeat of a dark historical cycle. It raises a difficult question: just how far have we drifted from the standard of justice and political integrity that the public demands?

The Impeachment Debate: Radical Calls vs. Cautious Strategy

The Rebuilding Korea Party, a progressive splinter party formed by former Justice Minister Cho Kuk, has ignited a firestorm by actively pushing for impeachment. Within the broader liberal bloc, this move has created a clear tactical divide. On one side, proponents argue that the time for hesitation has passed and that the party must strike hard at the administration. On the other, there is a prevailing "cautionary theory," largely held by the Democratic Party of Korea (the country's ruling party, led by President Lee Jae-myung), which fears that premature impeachment talk could trigger a public backlash.

Behind the Rebuilding Korea Party’s aggressive stance lies a deep-seated anger regarding the era of the previous administration. The public frustration that built up during the tenure of the now-imprisoned and disgraced former leader, Yoon Suk-yeol, has reached a boiling point. The Rebuilding Korea Party sees itself as the vanguard of this sentiment, refusing to compromise. While some view this as reckless, others argue that without such "sharp" political forces, the status quo remains unchallenged.

Conversely, the ruling Democratic Party finds itself in a more complex position. Having successfully ushered in the current administration, they are focused on normalizing state affairs and stabilizing governance. They view the radical, unilateral impeachment rhetoric from their smaller ally as a potential liability. While critics accuse the ruling party of being overly timid, the reality is that a major political party must balance idealistic demands for justice against the practical responsibilities of maintaining national stability. The coming months will be a test of whether the liberal bloc can coordinate these competing approaches or if the internal friction will undermine their long-term political agenda.

The Prosecution’s "Slush Fund" Scandal

While the political drama unfolds in the assembly, a more visceral scandal is brewing within the Prosecution Service. Revelations have surfaced indicating that the prosecution systematically falsified records regarding the number of attendees at official meetings to siphon off "special activity funds" and operational budgets. These funds, intended for sensitive investigative work, were effectively treated as a personal slush fund.

For a public already cynical about the influence of the prosecution, this is an infuriating betrayal. The irony is stark: an institution tasked with upholding the rule of law and scrutinizing the criminal activities of citizens has been caught red-handed engaging in, at best, financial malpractice, and at worst, systematic fraud. The fact that these agencies—which often treat minor procedural errors by ordinary citizens with extreme severity—have been caught manipulating their own ledger books to waste taxpayer money is a blow to the institution's remaining credibility.

What is perhaps most alarming is the lack of a robust, independent investigation into these specific acts of record-falsification, despite the evidence being in the public domain. This apparent "willful negligence" suggests an internal culture that views itself as untouchable. The public is now demanding not just an apology, but a full audit and legal accountability. The era of the prosecution using "investigative secrecy" as a shield to hide the misuse of public funds must come to an end.

The Imperative for Transparency and Accountability

The issues of political strategy and institutional corruption are linked by a single, common requirement: transparency. Whether it is the budget of a powerful law enforcement agency or the strategic maneuvers of political parties, the public’s resources and the power delegated to them must be subject to clear oversight. As long as "special activity funds" remain in the shadows, the risk of abuse will persist.

The Democratic Party faces a heavy burden here. As the governing force, they are now responsible for the very institutions that were previously used to undermine their political rivals. If they fail to reform the prosecution’s opaque spending habits, they risk inheriting the same public distrust that plagued their predecessors. The goal of any reform must be to ensure that no agency, regardless of its importance, is above the fundamental principle of fiscal accountability.

What to Watch Next

  • Prosecution Reform Bills: Keep an eye on the National Assembly for legislative moves to mandate stricter disclosure requirements for the prosecution's special activity funds.
  • Inter-Party Coordination: Monitor the relationship between the Democratic Party and the Rebuilding Korea Party. If the Democratic Party shifts toward the Rebuilding Korea Party's rhetoric on impeachment, it will signal a significant hardening of the political climate.
  • Public Protests: As these scandals gain traction, watch for organized demonstrations. The history of South Korean politics shows that when the system fails to self-correct, the public often takes to the streets to demand it.

Ultimately, the citizens of South Korea are watching to see if their government will prioritize party hegemony or the strengthening of the democratic system. The path forward requires moving beyond simple political posturing and into the difficult, unglamorous work of institutional reform. For the sake of the country's future, that work cannot wait.
