The S-1 Deconstruction: How Anthropic’s IPO Filing Shatters the AI Bubble Myth

October 27, 2026

For months, a quiet panic lingered over Wall Street and Yeouido alike. Following OpenAI’s release of its Astra model in early September, rumors swept global financial markets that Anthropic’s flagship Claude model was losing ground. Industry chatter suggested that Anthropic, facing mounting losses and slumping revenue, would be forced to postpone its anticipated November initial public offering to late 2027 or beyond. Analysts warned that the generative AI boom was degenerating into a classic capital sink—a narrative that weighed heavily on global technology stocks and hit South Korea’s retail investors, who hold immense positions in Big Tech and domestic memory semiconductor titans like SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics.

Then came the counterpunch. Instead of retreating, Anthropic filed a massive, 289-page Form S-1 registration statement with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). The unredacted document laid bare the company’s internal economics, dismantling the narrative of an unsustainable "AI bubble" with cold, hard numbers. Far from a cash-burning project on life support, the detailed financial statements reveal an enterprise generating cash at rates rarely seen in corporate history, backed by an unbelievable gross margin structure.

For international investors—and particularly South Korean market participants accustomed to navigating policy shifts and capital market transparency under the Lee Jae-myung administration after the volatile governance during Yoon Suk-yeol's time in office—the S-1 offers an unprecedented window into the real economics of frontier AI data centers. Here is what the numbers actually reveal once you strip away media sensationalism and accounting noise.

Inside the Numbers: Explosive Top-Line Growth and the 85% Margin Machine

To understand Anthropic’s trajectory, one must look at its quarter-by-quarter top-line evolution. The company’s revenue arc from early 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 reads less like a standard software startup and more like an industrial revolution compressed into eighteen months:

  • 1Q25 Revenue: $370 million
  • 2Q25 Revenue: $780 million
  • 3Q25 Revenue: $1.38 billion
  • 4Q25 Revenue: $2.04 billion
  • 1Q26 Revenue: $4.73 billion
  • 2Q26 Revenue: $11.55 billion
  • 3Q26 Revenue: Approximately $16.8 billion

In total, Anthropic generated $16.28 billion in revenue during the first half of 2026 alone—a more than 14-fold jump from the $1.1 billion recorded in the same period a year earlier. While skepticism flared over third-quarter growth rates, dismissing a company that scaled its quarterly revenue from $370 million to $16.8 billion in six quarters ignores the structural mechanics driving this expansion. Crucially, this revenue is not coming from casual $20-a-month retail subscriptions. The vast majority stems from enterprise B2B customers consuming token API bandwidth at massive scale, integrating Claude directly into corporate workflows.

However, the real revelation lies in the cost of goods sold. Under U.S. GAAP standards, superficial readers saw a modest 8% operating margin in 2Q26, where $10.58 billion in total costs yielded an operating profit of $974 million. Skeptics pointed to SK Hynix—South Korea’s premier high-bandwidth memory (HBM) supplier—enjoying memory-chip operating margins near 80%, asking why a pure AI software firm was netting single digits.

That comparison misinterprets the line items. To generate roughly $16.8 billion in 1H26 revenue from inference and enterprise workloads, Anthropic spent just $2.38 billion in direct cost of revenue (cloud hosting, data center electricity, and token processing costs). Subtracting $2.38 billion from $16.8 billion leaves a staggering gross profit of $14.42 billion—representing a **85% Gross Profit Margin (GPM)**. When serving active enterprise workloads, Anthropic operates as a near-pure cash machine.

Accounting Realities: K-Pop Trainees, Immediate Expensing, and Model Monopolies

If Anthropic generates an 85% gross profit margin on inference, why does its GAAP operating margin appear so slim? The answer lies in how the firm chooses to account for its research and development computing costs.

When building next-generation frontier models, AI companies must commit billions of dollars up front to cluster compute. Anthropic chose an extremely conservative accounting methodology: rather than capitalizing frontier model training costs as long-term intangible assets and amortizing them over time, it expenses 100% of training costs immediately as current-period R&D. In 1H26 alone, Anthropic funnelled $9.7 billion directly into training next-generation models, booking the entire sum as an immediate operating expense.

Analogy: Consider the economics of South Korea’s K-pop entertainment agencies. An agency might spend ₩10 billion (about $7.5 million) over three years training and preparing a new idol group. If accounting rules required the agency to write off that entire ₩10 billion upfront during the training years, the company would look deeply unprofitable. But once the group debuts, zero additional training capital is required, and seven years of high-margin touring, streaming, and merchandising revenue flood the balance sheet. Anthropic is expensing its "trainees" immediately.

Had Anthropic capitalized its frontier training expenses, its reported operating margin would instantly jump to between 60% and 70%. By expensing them upfront, Anthropic depresses current paper profitability while building an unprecedented moat for future earnings.

This dynamic is directly tied to Anthropic’s model pipeline hierarchy:

  • Mythos: The unreleased, top-tier frontier model currently consuming billions in training compute, fully expensed on current balance sheets.
  • Fable: The intermediate model pushing boundary capabilities across complex task automation.
  • Opus and Sonnet: The fully trained, commercially deployed models generating enterprise token sales at an 85% gross margin with virtually no incremental development cost.

Critically, this refutes the long-standing "AI compute inefficiency" myth—the claim that data centers waste 75% of their capacity on training while generating only 25% in commercial inference. As trained models like Sonnet and Opus mature, the revenue derived from inference scales exponentially, while the relative cost burden of training subsequent models trends downward as a percentage of total revenue.

Debunking the $80 Billion Loss and Unpacking Real Governance Risks

Immediately following the S-1 filing, sensationalist headlines dominated financial news feeds: "Anthropic Hits $21B Revenue but Posts $80.7B Net Loss." Uninformed commentators cited this $80.7 billion figure (roughly ₩106 trillion) as evidence of an imminent corporate collapse.

A closer look at the filing reveals that this "loss" is an accounting illusion. Out of the $80.7 billion net loss reported for the first half of 2026, **$72 billion consisted of non-cash mark-to-market accounting adjustments** tied to Convertible Bonds (CBs) and derivative financial liabilities. Under U.S. accounting standards, as Anthropic’s overall corporate valuation soared, the implied value of the stock options embedded within its convertible debt increased proportionally. Consequently, the company had to record that paper valuation gain for debt holders as a "loss" on its own balance sheet.

No cash left the building. In real operational terms, Anthropic posted an operating profit of $974 million in 2Q26. Removing non-cash accounting charges reveals a balance sheet that is exceptionally robust.

However, the S-1 does highlight two genuine, structural risks that every international investor must evaluate:

1. Absolute Founder Control and Dual-Class Governance

Anthropic’s governance structure strongly insulates management from shareholder pressure. Through a dedicated entity called "Founder LLC," the seven co-founders hold Class F super-voting shares granting them **50.1% absolute voting control**. Furthermore, a "Long-Term Benefit Trust" established in 2023 holds the authority to elect a majority of the board of directors. While Anthropic frames this as an ethical safeguard against "existential risks" posed by advanced AI, public equity investors are effectively buying non-voting economic rights without management oversight powers.

2. Over $500 Billion in Non-Cancellable Infrastructure Commitments

To secure compute capacity, Anthropic has locked itself into long-term cloud and hardware contracts exceeding $500 billion with tech hyperscalers including Google, Amazon Web Services, Microsoft, and Broadcom. Crucially, over 80% of these multi-year commitments are non-cancellable. If global demand for AI tokens experiences a sudden macro-structural freeze, these fixed infrastructure obligations would pose an acute solvency challenge.

What to Watch Next: The Hardware Ripple Effect from Silicon Valley to Seoul

Anthropic’s S-1 filing provides crucial reassurance for the global technology ecosystem. The binding $500 billion infrastructure pipeline guarantees a high revenue floor for compute partners and semiconductor supply chains. For South Korea’s equity market—where retail and institutional flows are deeply tied to the memory chip cycle—Anthropic’s 85% gross margin confirms that high-density HBM memory modules are not depreciating sunk costs, but high-margin productivity engines operating 24 hours a day.

As the IPO moves closer to its final pricing and formal listing, investors should monitor three concrete milestones:

  • The Transition Ratio of R&D to Inference: Watch whether 4Q26 R&D expensing slows relative to inference revenue, which will cause GAAP operating profits to surge toward the 30%+ mark.
  • Commercial Deployment of Mythos: Monitor the formal market release of the top-tier Mythos model, marking its transition from a multi-billion-dollar R&D cost center into an active enterprise token generator.
  • Hyperscaler Capital Expenditures: Track quarterly capex guidance from Amazon and Google to ensure their non-cancellable capacity allocations with Anthropic remain on schedule without supply bottleneck delays.

Anthropic’s SEC filing has officially shifted the global AI discussion from speculative hype to audited financial fundamentals. For international markets, the verdict is clear: when frontier AI models hit commercial scale, their profit generation engine is unequivocally real.
