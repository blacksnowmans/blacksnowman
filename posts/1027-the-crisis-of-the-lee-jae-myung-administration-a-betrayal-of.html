The Crisis of the Lee Jae-myung Administration: A Betrayal of Reform

September 23, 2026

Watching the veteran intellectual and former minister Rhyu Si-min deconstruct the current state of South Korean politics recently felt like a moment of clarity. For those unfamiliar, Rhyu is a prominent voice in the liberal camp whose analytical style often cuts to the bone. When he spoke about the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, it mirrored my own growing frustration: the current government, which rode to power on the promise of "prosecution reform," is now presiding over what can only be described as a grand deception of the public. This is no longer a matter of partisan disagreement; it is a fundamental governance crisis that supporters can no longer afford to ignore.

President Lee Jae-myung, the leader of the Democratic Party of Korea (the main liberal opposition-turned-ruling party), recently offered a public apology, citing his own "shortcomings." Yet, the performance rang hollow. It failed to address the systemic disillusionment of his base. Recent polling data highlights a sobering reality: over 26% of respondents cite the watering down of prosecution reforms—specifically the failure to strip the prosecution of its investigative powers—as the primary reason for his plummeting approval ratings. The administration preaches cooperation and unity, but its personnel decisions and legislative maneuvering scream of "naeronambul"—a Korean term for the hypocrisy of "if I do it, it's romance; if you do it, it's an affair."

The Mask of Prosecution Reform

At the heart of the administration's policy platform was a clear, bold promise: to separate investigation and prosecution functions, effectively clipping the wings of the powerful South Korean prosecution service. This was intended to dismantle the so-called "prosecution cartel" that many believe has stifled justice for decades. Instead, we have witnessed the opposite. The number of prosecutors within the Ministry of Justice has actually increased compared to previous administrations, and the promised "separation" of personnel has been abandoned.

The recent push to appoint Kim Ji-yong as head of the newly proposed Serious Crimes Investigation Agency—a man from the prosecution's own ranks—is a masterclass in contradiction. It signals to the public that "reform" is merely a rebranding exercise. When the government plays political games with the structure of these agencies, counting staff reductions by a handful of people rather than tackling the institutional rot, it treats the public like children. This cowardice, combined with a desperate need to maintain high polling numbers, has backfired. The administration’s refusal to either commit fully to their promises or honestly admit their limitations has led to the collapse of their support into the 30% range.

The Rise of "New Lee Jae-myung" and Fanaticism

A disturbing trend has emerged in the digital landscape: the rise of "New Lee Jae-myung" YouTube channels. These platforms have largely abandoned critical discourse in favor of attacking former President Moon Jae-in or the Rebuilding Korea Party (a smaller, progressive party led by Cho Kuk). This is not political support; it is cult-like sycophancy.

True support for the Democratic Party should be rooted in a desire for a healthy, democratic Korea. My own critiques of figures like Lee Jae-myung or his senior aides stem from the belief that if the party is not healthy, the nation cannot be. By contrast, these "New Lee" influencers appear to be nothing more than aspiring insiders, hoping to trade their loyalty for proximity to power. Their viewership is tanking because the public is discerning; they know when content is produced to serve a nation and when it is produced to curry favor with the powerful. The hate and vitriol generated by these channels will vanish like a mirage, leaving nothing behind.

Personnel Disasters: The "Seongnam Line"

In just 15 months, five ministerial nominees have been forced to withdraw. This is not just a failure of vetting; it is a systemic collapse. A persistent narrative within the Blue House is the dominance of the "Seongnam Line"—a group of aides and associates from the President's time as the Mayor of Seongnam. The resignation of Chief of Staff Kang Hoon-sik, coming just as the President departed for an overseas trip, was a transparent signal of deep internal power struggles.

The influence of unvetted, grassroots-level associates on national policy is a classic hallmark of failing administrations. When the President surrounds himself only with those who say "yes," he loses touch with the reality of the electorate. Observers like Park Jie-won, a veteran political heavyweight, have openly questioned why individuals with no professional expertise beyond personal loyalty to the President are being placed in critical positions. This "loyalty-first" hiring policy has replaced the competence needed to manage an economy and a society. When an administration confuses personal loyalty with national interest, the result is inevitably a loss of public trust.

The Path Forward: What to Watch

The President stands at a precipice. The administration’s current trajectory—characterized by economic policies that ignore the reality of rising interest rates and housing costs, and a reform agenda that has been hollowed out—is unsustainable. To avoid a disastrous collapse, the administration must take three concrete steps:

  • Purge the "Seongnam Line": The President must distance himself from the inner circle of cronies whose influence is poisoning the cabinet and the civil service.
  • Deliver on Genuine Reform: Stop the political theater. If the administration promised to separate investigation and prosecution, it must act, even if it is politically painful.
  • End the Sycophancy: Acknowledge that criticism is a survival mechanism, not an act of betrayal. The administration needs advisors who speak truth to power, not influencers who feed the echo chamber.

The coming weeks will be a litmus test for the administration. Will the President heed these warnings, or will he continue to rely on the hollow support of sycophants? If he chooses the latter, the history of his presidency will be written not as a story of reform, but as one of missed opportunity and betrayed trust. We are watching to see if the President truly wants to lead a nation or merely preserve a regime.
