The Silent Toll: Why Global Apathy and Judicial Decay Threaten Us All

September 09, 2026

Inside the United Nations headquarters in New York hangs a tapestry inscribed with a 13th-century poem by the Persian poet Saadi Shirazi, titled "Bani Adam" (Children of Adam). It begins with a haunting reminder: "Human beings are members of a whole, in creation of one essence and soul." It concludes with a stern warning for those who turn away from the suffering of others: "If you have no sympathy for the pain of others, you are unworthy of being called human."

Today, the world seems to have forgotten this lesson. As we scroll through our feeds, the screams echoing from the sands of the Middle East are treated as mere background noise—distant problems to be muted with a click of the remote. Meanwhile, in South Korea, the leaders of the judiciary seem more concerned with political maneuvering than upholding the law. We must ask: how did we become a society that closes its eyes to death and justifies silence as a survival strategy?

The Bloody Contradiction of Modern Warfare

It is a bitter irony that the land of Persia, which birthed such profound humanistic poetry, is now the site of indiscriminate airstrikes and "decapitation strikes" carried out by the United States. Following the collapse of the third round of nuclear talks in Geneva, the Trump administration bypassed diplomatic solutions to launch military actions against Iran. The U.S. demand for total disarmament—including the surrender of enriched uranium and the dismantling of facilities like Natanz and Fordow—was met with force when Iran hesitated. This "might makes right" logic stands in stark opposition to international law, specifically the UN Charter, which prohibits the use of force in international relations.

The human cost of this geopolitical maneuvering was laid bare on February 28, when a U.S.-Israel airstrike leveled the Shajareh Tayyebeh elementary school in Minab, Iran. Relying on outdated intelligence that identified the site as a military facility, the attack killed 170 children and teachers. Amnesty International has labeled this a potential war crime, citing clear evidence of U.S.-manufactured munitions at the blast site. Yet, the Pentagon’s response has been limited to a delayed internal investigation. While Western leaders like French President Emmanuel Macron and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre have voiced criticism, the reality remains: when powerful nations ignore the law, the innocent pay the ultimate price.

The Hormuz Strait and the Illusion of "Peace Through Strength"

The U.S. strategy of "peace through strength" is failing, particularly in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical maritime chokepoint through which about 20% of the world's oil flows. By provoking Iran, the U.S. has effectively handed Tehran the power to throttle global energy markets, causing inflation to ripple across the globe. As the U.S. pushes for a multinational coalition to secure the strait, South Korea faces immense pressure to deploy troops.

However, blind subservience to Washington is a strategic trap. Deploying South Korean forces into the "powder keg" of the Middle East would not only endanger our soldiers but also align us with a military policy that lacks global consensus. Instead of acting as a pawn in an American power struggle, Seoul should advocate for multilateral frameworks, such as the humanitarian corridors proposed by UN Secretary-General António Guterres, to ensure the flow of essential goods like food and medicine. To prioritize short-term political favor over principled diplomacy is to gamble with our national security and our standing in the international community.

The Crisis of Independence in South Korea’s Judiciary

The rot is not just international; it is domestic. The South Korean judiciary, intended to be the final bastion of democracy, is currently mired in a crisis of its own making. Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae has left two seats on the Supreme Court bench vacant for over six months. His recent move to nominate candidates via a "written proposal"—bypassing the traditional consultation process with the presidential office—is a blatant attempt to dodge political accountability and force through his own agenda.

This follows a pattern of troubling silence. During the recent political turmoil surrounding the declaration of martial law, the judiciary’s failure to act decisively left citizens exposed. As outgoing Justice Lee Heung-gu noted in his farewell address, the Supreme Court has failed to communicate its stance during national crises. By prioritizing self-preservation and political convenience, the judiciary has eroded the public’s trust. When the highest court in the land fails to speak when it must, it loses its right to act as the guardian of the Constitution.

The Weight of Our Shared Humanity

The 17th-century English poet John Donne famously wrote, "No man is an island... any man's death diminishes me, because I am involved in mankind." The death of 170 children in an Iranian school is not an isolated event; it is a diminishment of our shared humanity. Every time we remain silent in the face of injustice—whether it is a foreign airstrike or the moral bankruptcy of our own judicial leaders—we contribute to a world that is colder and more dangerous.

The "bell" that Donne spoke of is tolling for us. We have a choice: to succumb to the "survival of the fittest" mentality that leads to destruction, or to restore our dignity by standing up for the principles of human rights and international law. We must reject the calls for blind military involvement and demand that our institutions answer to the law, not to political masters.

What to watch next: Keep a close eye on the parliamentary response to the Supreme Court nominations and any shifts in the government’s stance on the Hormuz Strait. The true test of our democracy will not be in how we align ourselves with global superpowers, but in how we respond to the "toll of the bell" when the vulnerable are under attack. If we do not cultivate the capacity to empathize with the pain of others, we risk losing the very thing that makes us human.
