The Dark Side of the AI "Side Hustle" Boom in South Korea

September 01, 2026

Open YouTube in Korea today, and you are almost guaranteed to encounter a specific, high-energy advertisement. It usually features a relatable office worker claiming they quit the grind after discovering how to use AI to generate "passive income." The promise is tantalizing: spend an hour after work, click a few buttons, and bring home 10 million won (about $7,000) a month. It suggests that AI is a magic wand that turns digital labor into an endless fountain of cash.

For many struggling with stagnant wages and rising costs of living, these vibrant graphs and screenshots of bank account deposits act as a siren song. But beneath the polished marketing lies a much more sobering reality. The industry selling these "secrets" is not creating value through AI—it is monetizing the desperation of people looking for a way out.

The Reality Check

  • Most advertisements for "AI side hustles" are merely funnels designed to sell expensive, low-quality coaching courses.
  • Creating professional AI content is not a "one-click" process; it is labor-intensive, requiring high-level planning, complex prompting, and tedious post-production.
  • The monetization ecosystem for such content is highly unstable, often plagued by platform bans and copyright issues that make the effort-to-reward ratio abysmal.

The Industry of Selling Fantasies

The myth of the "one-click life change" is a modern fairy tale. While AI tools for generating video or music are indeed technological marvels, the leap from "using a tool" to "generating profit" is massive. If you were to calculate the time spent refining prompts, paying for tool subscriptions, and performing manual edits to produce a three-minute video, you would find that you would make more money working a standard part-time job at a local convenience store.

The instructors behind these courses love to showcase the massive earnings of their "successful" students. However, these figures are often based on cherry-picked data, temporary flukes, or outright manipulated dashboards. For the instructor, the business model is simple: sell a course for 3 to 4 million won (about $2,200 to $3,000). By the time the student realizes that the "automatic" system doesn't work, the instructor has already secured their profit.

When students inevitably fail to replicate these results, the companies shift the blame to them, claiming they lacked "effort" or "persistence." It has become a dark joke in the industry: the most profitable AI side hustle isn't using AI—it’s selling a course on how to use AI.

Why This Scam Thrives

Why do so many people fall for these obvious traps? It is fueled by a deep sense of social deprivation. In a society where hard work often doesn't lead to a better quality of life, people are naturally drawn to shortcuts. Scammers weaponize this anxiety. We have created a culture that downplays the value of honest labor in favor of "hacks" and "luck," creating the perfect soil for these fraudulent business models to grow.

How to Approach AI Without Being Used

Content that lacks human soul is quickly sniffed out by audiences. AI can produce sleek visuals and catchy music, but it cannot replicate the unique perspective, emotional depth, or original insight that a human creator brings to the table. We have entered an era where "what" you are saying is infinitely more important than "what" you used to make it.

This is not a call to reject AI entirely. Instead, view AI as a secondary assistant rather than the protagonist of your work. Use it to speed up your editing workflow or to brainstorm ideas, but do not rely on it to be a "money-printing machine." The only way to build a sustainable side hustle is to leverage your own unique experiences and expertise—things that AI cannot replicate.

The Data on Victims

The scale of this issue is becoming impossible to ignore. In South Korea, official reports of damage related to high-priced online side hustle courses jumped from 11 cases in 2024 to 42 cases in 2025—a fourfold increase. This is likely just the tip of the iceberg, as many victims choose not to report smaller losses, effectively subsidizing the expansion of a predatory market that feeds on public anxiety.

The Verdict: Technology is a Tool, Not a Savior

As someone who uses AI daily, I find this entire trend deeply bitter. AI is an incredible technology that democratizes creativity and simplifies complex tasks. Yet, it has become synonymous with "scams" in the public imagination, largely because we have projected our own desire for "lazy wealth" onto it. We wanted a machine to save us from labor, and in doing so, we opened the door to predators.

If you are looking to make money with AI, start by asking yourself: "Is this content more valuable than something a machine could make on its own?" If you cannot answer that, you will likely fail. The course providers will sell you the "how," but they will never teach you the "why," because if you understood the essence of value creation, you wouldn't need their course in the first place.

There is no such thing as a free lunch. No matter how advanced AI becomes, the money in your bank account will always be a reward for the value you provide to others. Stop being a customer of the dream-sellers and start being the architect of your own unique value.
