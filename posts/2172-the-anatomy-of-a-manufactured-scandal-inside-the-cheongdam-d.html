The Anatomy of a Manufactured Scandal: Inside the Cheongdam-dong Drinking Party Investigation Records

November 10, 2026

When legal and investigative documents are finally laid bare, the public narrative we were fed by mainstream media often dissolves like a sandcastle. The investigation surrounding the infamous "Cheongdam-dong drinking party"—a late-night gathering allegedly involving former top officials at the height of Yoon석열 씨's presidency—was never about uncovering the substantive truth. Instead, newly reviewed police and prosecutorial records reveal a textbook case of a scripted investigation, where locations, testimonies, and flimsy physical evidence were forcefully bent to fit a pre-determined conclusion.

How else can one explain why police authorities zeroed in on a cramped room salon where neither CCTV footage nor credit card receipts existed, and where thirty people could not have fit even if they were tightly packed? Today, with Yoon석열 씨 impeached and imprisoned for his abuses of power, this bizarre episode cannot be dismissed as a mere political happening. It stands as a grim indicator of how far investigative agencies could degenerate to protect a targeted nexus of political power.

The Curious Mystery of the Location: Tike versus Ia

The choice of the room salon "Tike" as the alleged crime scene defies basic common sense. According to investigative records, the venue was physically too small to accommodate the roughly thirty people reported to have attended. As local insiders pointed out, claiming thirty people fit into Tike is absurd. Yet, police locked onto this location on November 9, 2022—a full two weeks before formally questioning the key witness, a cellist.

Even more absurd was the state of the venue itself. One of the core elements of the alleged drinking party was musical performances, including a cello and singing. Yet, the piano inside Tike was completely out of tune and unplayable. Still, police pushed forward. A location with zero CCTV records and zero credit card transactions turned out to be an attractive "blank canvas" for investigators. Without physical evidence, they could rely entirely on managed testimonies to lock down the location and sweep the case under the rug.

By contrast, a 2024 court ruling pointed to an entirely different establishment: "Ia," a venue operated by singer Lee Mi-ki. Ia matched every specific condition mentioned in the original leaked transcripts—it possessed a grand piano, could comfortably hold over thirty people, sat in the alley behind the luxury Galleria Department Store, and was run by an owner with an acting background. Yet, police completely bypassed these obvious clues.

Ia operated as an exclusive, members-only space. Had it become the focus of the investigation, the high-profile guest list passing through its doors that night would have been exposed. Choosing to ignore Ia while obsessively pinning the event on the tiny, unequipped Tike was a calculated effort to shield the real trail left by powerful figures.

A Lightning-Fast Investigation and the Hand of Conservative Media

When the police finally called the cellist in for questioning on November 23, 2022, the interrogation began at 2:00 PM and wrapped up in a blistering two hours. A massive national controversy involving the nation's highest offices was supposedly solved in 120 minutes. During this rapid session, officers did not interrogate the witness for truth; they effectively handed her a completed answer sheet to check off.

Records show that police officers openly led the witness, supplying the name "Tike" when the cellist stumbled on details. Rather than testing the consistency of her memory, authorities injected the narrative they wanted. Even more damningly, not a single question was asked about where she sat inside the venue or where the restrooms were located—basic verification steps in any standard probe.

The media choreography that followed was even more revealing. Less than twelve hours after the interrogation ended—before digital forensics reports were finalized and before official records were even collated—the conservative newspaper Chosun Ilbo published an exclusive report labeling the cellist a liar. Such timing was impossible without real-time leaks from inside the police department to favored press outlets, transforming the state into a spin doctor for powerful figures.

Vanishing Call Records and Altered Navigation Data

Perhaps the most glaring hole in the investigative record involves the cellist's communication logs. Between the night of July 19 and the early hours of July 20, 2022, a 7-minute and 42-second block of cell tower location data vanished from her records with KT, one of South Korea's major telecom providers. While the call duration was logged, the specific neighborhood base station data was entirely blank.

In the digital forensics world, such selective data blanks are treated as a red flag for evidence tampering. Right after a parliamentary audit raised questions, three central figures—including political organizer Lee Se-chang and the cellist—simultaneously replaced their mobile phones and changed their numbers, permanently destroying primary evidence.

To fill the vacuum left by missing telecom logs, police relied on a single piece of navigation data pointing toward Tike. Yet, the National Forensic Service noted that this file alone could not confirm actual travel routes. Despite this caveat, police used the file to author a definitive investigative report, which was promptly used by then-Justice Minister Han Dong-hoon to launch a $750,000 (1 billion won) civil lawsuit against opposition lawmakers and journalists. The timeline—conclusion first, lawsuit second, investigation last—reveals a synchronized effort between the accusers and state investigators.

Rewarding the Alibi Providers

The individuals who vouched for the gathering—such as former Korea Freedom Federation official Lee Se-chang and lawyers close to Yoon석열 씨—offered alibis claiming they were indeed at Tike. Yet, none of them were ever subjected to in-person, high-intensity questioning by police; their alibis were rubber-stamped via casual phone calls.

As the political dust settled, these alibi-providers enjoyed remarkable career upsides. Legal counsel tied to the presidential circle was later tapped for high-level administrative posts in the presidential office, while corporate figures claiming to have been at the party joined presidential economic delegations abroad. By contrast, whistleblowers and reporters who pushed for a thorough probe faced crushing defamation suits and financial ruin.
