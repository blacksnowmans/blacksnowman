Apple's September Showcase: Foldables, Soaring Prices, and the Trap of the Ecosystem

September 10, 2026

For anyone waking up to catch Apple’s latest autumn hardware event on their morning commute, the initial rush of excitement is a familiar autumn ritual. Renderings and leaked mockups are quickly forgotten the moment real hardware hits the screen, accompanied by the sinking realization that another wallet is about to be emptied. But this year’s lineup feels different. It is not merely a routine spec bump. Apple has finally entered the foldable market with its first folding device, completely revamped its professional iPhone tier, and packed health-tracking sensors into the latest Apple Watch and AirPods.

The iPhone Duo: Apple's First Foldable Enters the Fray

After years of speculation, Apple's debut foldable smartphone has arrived under the name iPhone Duo. Abandoning the clunky proportions found in early industry mockups, the Duo opts for softer curves that stand apart from competing foldables on the market. When unfolded, it reveals a 4:3 wide foldable form factor featuring a 7.6-inch internal display and a 5.4-inch outer screen, bringing the utility of an iPad directly into a pocket-sized device. Apple has applied a nano-texture finish to the inner display to minimize glare and resist scratches.

Running on the newly redesigned iOS 27 interface, the software has been built from the ground up for the folding screen, adapting the home screen, control center, and multitasking layouts seamlessly. In a first for the iPhone line, the Duo also supports the Apple Pencil via USB-C, opening the door for handwriting and drawing on a larger canvas. However, luxury comes with engineering hurdles. Industry reports noted that the Duo experienced initial production hiccups regarding hinge durability as Apple pushed to meet its strict quality thresholds. While crease visibility has reportedly been tamed to rival or exceed the Oppo Find N6, hinge longevity remains a closely watched variable.

Under the hood, the device packs a 2nm A20 Pro chip alongside a C2 coprocessor, driving a 15% boost in processing speed and energy efficiency. The camera array includes a 48-megapixel fusion dual lens on the back, a 12-megapixel center-stage camera up front, and an under-display FaceTime camera that leaves the screen completely unblemished. Crafted from Grade 5 titanium with IP68 water and dust resistance, the phone delivers impressive battery life—rated at up to 31 hours for internal display use and 44 hours for the outer screen, with fast-charging capable of hitting 50% in 20 minutes.

Yet, practicality takes a hit in the physics department. The Duo weighs a hefty 254g, making it heavier than even the iPhone 18 Pro Max (249g) and the heaviest iPhone in history. Slipping it into a front pocket might feel like carrying a brick. Furthermore, the starting price is staggering: in South Korea, it kicks off at 3.29 million won (about $2,400), with higher storage tiers pushing past 3.9 million won (about $2,850). Despite the eye-watering price tag, market analysts expect Apple to capture significant ground in the high-end folding sector as global shipments ramp up.

The iPhone 18 Pro Series and the Arrival of Korean Siri AI

Apple has streamlined its smartphone lineup this year, entirely dropping standard and "Air" variants to focus exclusively on the Pro and Pro Max. Available in black, silver, glacier, and burgundy, the new generation aims squarely at power users. For users in South Korea, the standout software milestone is the official launch of Siri's Korean AI capabilities. Long criticized for lagging behind local competitors, Siri is finally receiving proper generative AI integration for translation, summarization, and context-aware assistance.

Performance-wise, the Pro series utilizes the same 2nm A20 Pro chip paired with a vapor chamber cooling system to tackle thermal throttling during heavy gaming or video editing. Battery life reaches up to 36 hours for the Pro and 45 hours for the Pro Max, paired with rapid charging capabilities.

The true hardware breakthrough, however, lies in the main camera's new variable aperture. By utilizing a six-blade physical aperture system, the phone overcomes the traditional depth-of-field limitations of mobile photography. Users can manually dial in dramatic background blur for portraits or crisp, deep-focus landscape shots using the Pro Control mode. Externally, the infamous Dynamic Island has been significantly downsized and can now split into three separate modular indicators depending on active background tasks.

Apple Watch Ultra 4 and Series 12: Precision Health Tracking

The Apple Watch continues its evolution from a simple notification tether into a serious medical and athletic companion. The Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 introduce high-frequency health monitoring, checking heart rates every 5 seconds—60 times more frequently than previous generations—while tracking heart rate variability (HRV) every 5 minutes to catch subtle physiological shifts.

New additions include biological age estimation, a redesigned fitness app interface, a daily "Readiness" recovery score, and VO2 Max tracking. Powering these dense background calculations is the new S11 chip. The Series 12 brings back ceramic casing options in pearl white and night blue, alongside aluminum finishes in dark bronze, light gold, black, and space gray. Meanwhile, the outdoor-focused Apple Watch Ultra 4 arrives in black and natural titanium, maintaining a two-day battery life that stretches up to 45 hours even with GPS and sensors running continuously during heavy workouts.

Practical everyday features have also expanded. The watches introduce an "Audio Intelligence" system capable of recognizing critical environmental sounds like sirens or timers and relaying them via haptic feedback. Additionally, a 15-second real-time audio rewind and transcription feature allows users to tap their wrists to check summarized notes from recent conversations.

AirPods 5: Active Noise Cancellation Goes Mainstream

Perhaps the biggest surprise of the hardware cycle is the AirPods 5. Historically, Apple reserved Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) exclusively for its higher-end Pro earbuds. With this generation, ANC comes standard on all base AirPods 5 models.

While retaining the popular open-ear design that many users find more comfortable than silicone-tip in-ear alternatives, Apple claims a 150% improvement in noise suppression compared to older open-ear models. Battery life hits up to 5 hours on a single charge, supported by adaptive audio processing and real-time translation features. Pricing is surprisingly aggressive: the ANC-equipped base model starts at 199,000 won (about $145), with a wireless charging case variant priced at 229,000 won (about $165).

What to Watch Next

Apple’s latest hardware drop showcases undeniable engineering maturity, from foldable glass and variable camera apertures to wrist-based biometric tracking and democratized noise cancellation. Yet, the overarching narrative remains one of aggressive upward price scaling. As global consumers face increasingly burdensome upgrade costs, the friction between cutting-edge innovation and everyday affordability grows sharper.

What to watch for in the coming weeks:

  • Pre-order metrics: Watch how early consumer demand handles the record-breaking 3.29 million won price tag of the iPhone Duo.
  • Real-world hinge durability: Independent reviews will test whether Apple's foldable screen crease and mechanical hinge hold up under daily stress.
  • Korean Siri rollout: User reception of the newly launched Korean-language AI features will determine if Apple can finally close the gap in the local smart assistant market.
  • iOS 27 deployment: Software stability and battery optimization reports will reveal how well the new OS handles the expanded hardware form factors.
