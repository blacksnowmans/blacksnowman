AI Cheating, Budget Battles, and Historic Trials: Inside South Korea's High-Stakes Legislative Push

November 17, 2026

Every mid-November, South Korea slows down for a single day as high school seniors sit for the Suneung, the national college entrance examination that dictates academic standing and career trajectories. But as millions of students prepare for the exam, lawmakers inside the National Assembly in Seoul are grappling with a modern dilemma: how to keep the nation’s most sacred academic test fair in an age where artificial intelligence fits inside a student's pocket.

That challenge was at the top of the agenda for the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) on November 16, as part of a legislative push that spans technology ethics, regional economic preservation, welfare reform, and judicial accountability. As the ruling party under President Lee Jae-myung moves to consolidate its policy priorities, it finds itself balancing everyday governance and public safety against political battles over the national budget and legal reckonings following recent political turmoil.

Cracking Down on AI Cheating and Reimagining South Korea’s Classroom

Expanding Exam Bans and Protecting Academic Fairness

In South Korea, the Suneung (College Scholastic Ability Test) is a high-stakes national event where flight paths are altered and police escorts are provided to ensure students arrive on time. With technological tools becoming increasingly sophisticated, concerns over high-tech cheating using artificial intelligence have escalated dramatically.

To safeguard the test's integrity, the DPK is pushing for aggressive legal amendments to ban anyone caught using AI tools to cheat on the Suneung from retaking the exam for up to 10 years or more. Under current regulations, individuals caught cheating face a five-year disqualification. Doubling this penalty signals an uncompromising stance against digital fraud. Lawmakers are reviewing options, including retroactive legislative provisions, to apply these tougher rules as early as the upcoming exam cycle for the 2026 academic year.

Beyond severe penalties, the legislative effort includes updating digital device protocols inside examination halls and modernizing criteria for detecting advanced technological cheating. For Korean families who invest years of effort and personal sacrifice into exam preparation, maintaining a level playing field is essential to public trust in the education system.

Launching the Education Special Committee for Future Reform

Alongside immediate anti-cheating measures, the DPK formally launched its Education Special Committee to address structural issues within South Korea's school system. Speaking at the launch ceremony, DPK leader Kim Min-seok emphasized the need to redesign educational frameworks for the AI era and build direct lines of communication between classrooms, the party, and the government.

The committee aims to move away from South Korea's traditional emphasis on rote memorization toward personalized, technology-integrated learning. However, lawmakers are proceeding with caution. Primary targets for the committee include narrowing the digital learning gap that could arise during AI integration and alleviating the administrative workload placed on teachers. The goal is to ensure technology enhances instructional quality without widening educational inequality across different socioeconomic strata.

Judicial Accountability for Insurrection and a $590 Billion Budget Clash

Demanding a Strict Verdict in the Han Duck-soo Appeal

While educational reform dominates policy talks, legal proceedings linked to past state disruption continue to cast a long shadow over South Korean politics. The Supreme Court set November 19 as the final verdict date for the appeal of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo, prompting strong calls from the ruling party for an uncompromising judicial ruling.

DPK leaders, including lawmaker Han Byung-do, publicly expressed frustration over the timing of the scheduled verdict, which comes just as the former prime minister’s detention period approaches its expiration. Party leadership argued that the judiciary must fulfill its constitutional duty to strictly penalize those involved in insurrection and constitutional subversion. DPK lawmakers warned that any lenient ruling or political compromise that grants immunity to key actors would undermine judicial justice and trigger public anxiety. For the DPK, holding figures accountable for past attempts to undermine democratic order remains a non-negotiable principle.

The Battle Over Presidential Activity Funds and the National Budget

Simultaneously, tension flared inside the National Assembly Steering Committee over the government's proposed 821 trillion KRW (approximately $590 billion) national budget. The core of the dispute centers on special activity funds (teuksu hwal-dong-bi) allocated to the Presidential Office under the Lee Jae-myung administration.

The main conservative opposition party, the People Power Party (PPP), has demanded a complete zeroing out of these discretionary funds, arguing they lack transparency. The ruling DPK has pushed back, insisting that essential state operations must be funded while offering to conduct reasonable, transparent audits of the line items. The DPK criticized the PPP for resorting to political walkouts and harsh rhetoric, urging the opposition to return to legislative negotiations so that essential public welfare and future-focused budget items can be approved within the statutory deadline.

Protecting Local Tourism in Jeju and Tackling the Aging Crisis

Opposing the Abolition of Jeju’s Visa-Free Regime

Beyond national battles in Seoul, regional issues have sparked local opposition. A recently introduced bill seeking to completely abolish Jeju Island’s long-standing visa-free entry system has drawn resistance from the DPK’s Jeju provincial chapter and the local business community.

Jeju’s visa-free regime was created to accommodate its administrative status as a Special Self-Governing Province and to drive its tourism industry. Local representatives argue that introducing a sweeping ban without consulting island residents or business owners threatens the foundation of Jeju’s regional economy. While acknowledging the need for robust border security and immigration management, the DPK maintains that safety concerns should be met through systematic administrative upgrades rather than dismantling a crucial economic driver.

Increasing Central Funding for Eldercare to 80%

To address South Korea’s rapid demographic shift into a super-aging society, the DPK introduced legislation to increase central government funding for long-term care facilities. The proposed bill raises the national government's share of long-term care expenses to 80%, taking a massive financial burden off municipal governments (jijatche).

Currently, severe disparities in fiscal self-sufficiency among local governments result in uneven quality of eldercare services across different regions. By shifting the financial responsibility primarily to the national treasury, the bill aims to standardize care quality across the country, improve working conditions for care workers, and ensure that stable eldercare remains a guaranteed state responsibility rather than a localized financial hazard.

Upgrading Public Safety: Fire Codes, Crypto Scams, and Military Reform

Enforcing Retroactive Fire Safety and Freezing Scammer Assets

Public safety and financial security also featured prominently in the DPK’s recent legislative agenda. Lawmaker Kim Dong-ah introduced a bill aimed at closing a dangerous regulatory gap in commercial fire safety by retroactively applying upgraded fire safety standards to older logistics warehouses built prior to past legal revisions. Industrial logistics facilities in Korea have suffered catastrophic fires in recent years, and the bill seeks to standardize fire control measures across old and new structures alike.

On the consumer protection front, the party is accelerating legislation to combat modern financial crimes, such as illegal investment chatrooms ("reading rooms") and voice phishing. Given how rapidly illicit funds can be laundered through digital currencies, the proposed law grants authorities emergency powers to immediately freeze cryptocurrency assets linked to financial fraud, significantly increasing the likelihood of asset recovery for victims.

Reforms in Military Education and Expanded Protections Against Relationship Crimes

Structural reforms within the military and criminal justice systems round out the ruling party’s immediate priorities. On November 18, a joint government-party consultation meeting is set to finalize revisions to the integration plan for the nation's military academies. Discussions center on a proposed "1+2+1" joint training structure for the Army, Navy, and Air Force academies, as well as the potential relocation of the Korea Military Academy campus away from its historic Taereung location.

Furthermore, the DPK scheduled a general policy meeting for November 17 to overhaul South Korea's legal response to intimate partner violence. The party plans to amend the Domestic Violence Punishment Act into a broader "Intimate Relationship Violence Act." Key provisions include expanding the maximum distance for court-ordered restraining orders against offenders to 2 kilometers, closing long-standing legal blind spots surrounding dating violence and stalking.

What to Watch Next

As South Korea navigates these overlapping legislative, judicial, and technological challenges, several key events in the coming days will signal the direction of the political landscape:

  • The Supreme Court Ruling on Han Duck-soo (November 19): The final verdict on the former prime minister will set a legal precedent regarding judicial accountability for state disruption.
  • The Military Academy Integration Consultation (November 18): The outcome of the government-party talks on the "1+2+1" military academy restructuring and the relocation of the Korea Military Academy from Taereung.
  • Legislative Progress on AI Cheating Controls: Movement in parliamentary committees on amending the Suneung regulations ahead of the upcoming exam cycle to introduce up to 10-year bans for AI-assisted cheating.
  • The $590 Billion Budget Negotiations: Whether the ruling DPK and opposition PPP can bridge their standoff over presidential discretionary funds before statutory budget deadlines pass.
