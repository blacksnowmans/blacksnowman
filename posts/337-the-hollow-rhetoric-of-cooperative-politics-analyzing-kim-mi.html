The Hollow Rhetoric of "Cooperative Politics": Analyzing Kim Min-seok’s Recent Address

September 07, 2026

When Kim Min-seok, a senior figure in the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK)—the country’s primary liberal opposition party—took the podium at the National Assembly recently, many expected a bold vision for the future. He laid out four core pillars ranging from constitutional amendments to judicial reform. On the surface, the speech was polished. Yet, for many observers, it left an unsettling silence. Behind the grand stage and the carefully crafted rhetoric lies a bitter reality about the state of Korean politics: the gap between performative gestures and actual substance.

"Cooperative Politics" or Image Management?

In his address, Kim called for a "horizontal relationship" between the party and the administration, vowing that the party would act as a source of candid advice to the President. To the casual observer, this sounds like a healthy democratic check. However, in the context of South Korean power dynamics, the phrase "horizontal relationship" is frequently treated with skepticism. Critics argue that when the ruling party—or in this case, the main opposition party acting as a major parliamentary force—uses such language, it is rarely about holding power accountable. Instead, it is often a defensive maneuver designed to protect sagging approval ratings.

True horizontal cooperation is proven through action, not through the theatrical seat-shuffling or performative handshakes often seen in the National Assembly. While Kim advocates for "cooperative politics," critics see it as an attempt to dilute the party's reform agenda. By prioritizing the appearance of bipartisanship with the conservative People Power Party (PPP), the DPK risks weakening its own legislative mandate. When a party holds nearly two-thirds of the parliamentary seats, "begging" for cooperation with an opposition that consistently blocks reform bills can look less like statesmanship and more like a betrayal of the voters who sought decisive action.

The Instrumentalization of Roh Moo-hyun

Perhaps the most contentious part of Kim’s speech was his invocation of the late President Roh Moo-hyun. Kim listed Roh alongside other liberal icons like Kim Dae-jung and the current leader, Lee Jae-myung, as part of a continuous democratic lineage. For many party loyalists, this was seen as a profound insult.

Roh Moo-hyun spent his political life championing the principle of "separation between the party and the government." This was a crucial mechanism intended to prevent the ruling party from becoming a mere administrative rubber stamp for an imperial presidency. By citing Roh to justify a modern "party-government alignment," Kim is effectively using the late president’s name as a tool to rationalize the very power concentration that Roh sought to dismantle. For a politician to reinterpret history to suit their immediate tactical needs is a dangerous precedent that undermines the core values the DPK was built upon.

Data-Driven Resistance Against Alleged Corruption

Beyond the rhetoric of the assembly floor, Kim’s camp faces a concrete scandal: allegations of illegal text message campaigning during the party’s national convention. The backlash was immediate. Following the disclosure of a contact number, over 10,000 messages poured in from citizens asking how their personal information had been obtained for political use. This is no longer just a "political hiccup"; it is a potential breach of the Personal Information Protection Act.

The opposition to this conduct has moved from indignation to systematic documentation. Critics and activists are currently processing these 10,000+ messages into data sets to be used as evidence in legal proceedings. This is a move away from emotional, street-level protest toward a clinical, evidence-based legal battle. By involving legal teams to challenge the validity of the convention and address the broader issue of data privacy, they aim to expose how the party’s machinery may be manipulated by internal factions.

What to Watch Next

As the dust settles on these events, there are three critical areas to watch in the coming weeks:

  • Legal Accountability: The transition of these allegations into formal legal filings will test the independence of the judicial process. Watch for how the party leadership responds to the lawsuits regarding the national convention’s integrity.
  • The "Authenticity" Gap: Keep an eye on whether the DPK leadership continues to prioritize "cooperation" at the expense of its core reform platform. If the party continues to pursue "image politics" while ignoring the demands of its base, internal friction is likely to intensify.
  • Public Mobilization: A major petition involving over 65,000 signatures is set to be delivered to the Blue House (or relevant presidential offices), focusing on concerns regarding media manipulation and journalistic ethics. The government's response to this will be a litmus test for how much pressure the current administration is willing to ignore.

Ultimately, the current political environment in Korea is a tug-of-war between those who treat politics as an image-making exercise and those who demand a return to substantive reform. For the average observer, the lesson is clear: watch the data and the legislative outcomes, not just the speeches on the assembly floor. The truth in politics is rarely found in the rhetoric; it is found in the mechanisms of power and the persistence of those who refuse to let the past be distorted for present gain.
