Apple's September Event Breaks the Mold with the First Foldable iPhone and Major Hardware Leaps

September 10, 2026

Every autumn, the tech world turns its gaze toward Cupertino, California, as Apple rolls out its latest hardware lineup. But this September’s keynote carried a distinctly different weight. For years, tech observers wondered if Apple could truly reinvent categories that competitors had spent years pioneering, or if they would merely refine existing formulas. Watching the announcements unfold, the answer felt definitive: Apple has once again shifted the goalposts, introducing a hardware and software gap that feels remarkably stark.

From the long-awaited debut of Apple's first foldable device to radical shifts in the Pro smartphone lineup, this event was about much more than incremental spec bumps. As the first major product showcase overseen by newly appointed CEO John Ternus, it offered a clear look at where the company plans to take its ecosystem next. The engineering overhaul runs deep, touching everything from silicon architecture and audio processing to industrial design.

iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max: Chasing the Limits of Silicon and Optics

Smartphone performance has long reached a point of diminishing returns for everyday tasks, yet Apple continues to extract massive generational gains. Powered by the new A20 Pro chipset, the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max deliver up to a 50% increase in CPU performance and a 40% boost in GPU capabilities. In practice, the hardware handles heavy on-device artificial intelligence processing instantly, keeping pace with demanding workloads without breaking a sweat.

The camera system receives an equally substantial upgrade. Both Pro models finally introduce a variable aperture to the main wide-angle camera, allowing up to 50% more light into the sensor for low-light and indoor shooting. For videographers, Cinematic Mode now supports 60fps shooting, moving well beyond the previous 30fps ceiling. Minor quality-of-life adjustments round out the device, including a more compact Dynamic Island capable of displaying up to three simultaneous notifications, and staggering battery life—the iPhone 18 Pro Max delivers up to 45 hours of video playback, supported by fast-charging that hits 50% capacity in just 15 minutes.

Pricing reflects these high-end specifications. The iPhone 18 Pro starts at 1.99 million won (about $1,500), while the iPhone 18 Pro Max starts at 2.19 million won (about $1,650). Pre-orders begin on September 12, with official retail availability starting September 18.

AirPods 5: Bringing Active Noise Cancellation to an Open-Ear Design

Wireless earbuds have followed a predictable path, usually forcing users to choose between the secure seal of in-ear canal designs and the comfort of open-ear fits. The new AirPods 5 keep the comfortable open-ear design of their predecessors, catering to users who dislike the pressure of silicone tips, but introduce a major twist: Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) is now built in as a standard feature.

Achieving effective noise cancellation in an open-ear architecture is notoriously difficult, making this a significant technical achievement for Apple. It strikes a balance for urban commuters who want to dampen background city rumble while maintaining situational awareness. Advanced tiers add wireless charging and stem-touch volume controls, keeping user interaction seamless. Priced at 200,000 won (about $150) for the base model and 230,000 won (about $175) for the wireless charging version, the AirPods 5 lower the barrier of entry for premium audio features.

Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4: Refining Health Intelligence

The Apple Watch continues its evolution into a proactive health monitor. The Series 12 features a completely redesigned traffic sensor layout, allowing heart rate measurements every 5 seconds—positioning it among the most precise biometric trackers in the wearables space.

A standout software addition is "Audio Intelligence," which includes a "Live Rewind" feature. By double-clicking the Digital Crown, users can instantly transcribe ambient sound captured 15 seconds prior. Durability also gets a boost with a front-facing Ceramic Shield, improving scratch resistance by 60% over previous glass types. The Series 12 starts at 599,000 won (about $450). Meanwhile, the rugged Apple Watch Ultra 4 targets outdoor enthusiasts with a larger battery providing up to 45 hours of continuous use in extended workout mode, priced from 1.24 million won (about $930).

iPhone Duo: Apple's First Foldable Clears the Crease

The most anticipated hardware of the day was Apple's debut foldable, the iPhone Duo. Early skeptics worried about the durability and visible creases plaguing existing foldable phones, but the physical product aims to dismantle those concerns. Folded, it assumes a passport-sized form factor with a 5.4-inch asymmetric cover screen and curved edges designed for one-handed use.

Unfolded, the 7.6-inch main display nearly erases the central crease. Apple applied a custom "nano-texture finish"—borrowed from its iPad and Mac development—to drastically reduce glare and reflections, giving the screen a matte texture similar to an e-reader. The hinge mechanism utilizes over 100 precision components and a carbon-fiber support plate shaped by AI-driven laser scanning to prevent panel warping over time.

Software is tailored specifically to the form factor. The taskbar shifts to the right side to maximize content space, and multitasking between two apps runs fluidly. Transitioning from streaming Netflix on the cover screen to opening the device maintains playback without interruption, while half-folding the phone props it up like a built-in stand with adaptive controls. It also supports Apple Pencil Pro, though it notably opts for a side-button Touch ID instead of Face ID and weighs in at 254 grams.

Innovation at this scale carries a heavy financial penalty. The iPhone Duo starts at 3.29 million won (about $2,450 / $1,999 in the US) for the 256GB model. Pre-orders open on October 16, with retail shelves stocking the device on October 23.

"Existing foldables often feel like two separate phones awkwardly glued together. The iPhone Duo is the most significant structural evolution for the iPhone since the original model." — John Ternus, Apple CEO

What to Watch Next

As the initial wave of excitement settles, several practical questions remain for the months ahead:

    Supply Chain Realities: Watch for stock shortages and delivery wait times when pre-orders open on September 12 for the iPhone 18 lineup and AirPods 5, and October 16 for the iPhone Duo. Real-time store pickup reservations via the Apple Store app will likely be essential for avoiding long shipping delays.
    Consumer Price Resistance: At over 3.2 million won, the iPhone Duo tests the absolute upper limit of what consumers will pay for a mobile device. Monitor early sales figures to see whether mainstream buyers balk at the price tag or if early adopters establish a new normal for ultra-premium hardware.
    Competitor Response: Observe how rival manufacturers in the foldable space react to Apple's implementation of nano-texture displays and crease mitigation, which will likely dictate the next generation of Android foldables.
