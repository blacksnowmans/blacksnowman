Why Apple’s "Crackgate" Is Handing Samsung an Unexpected Victory

October 26, 2026

Within six hours of receiving his shiny new device, an excited buyer in Australia opened his smartphone fewer than twenty times, only to watch a fracture spiderweb down the middle of the glass. Instead of showing off the latest frontier in consumer electronics to his friends, he found himself driving back to a service center. Headlines quickly flared across tech publications: The Verge bluntly announced, “The first broken iPhone Duos are here,” while Apple tracker MacRumors coined a title that executives in Cupertino undoubtedly dreaded: “Crackgate.”

For years, industry observers wondered what would happen when Apple finally stepped into the foldable arena to challenge Samsung Electronics, the South Korean tech titan that has spent nearly a decade refining the folding screen. When the iPhone Duo launched with an eye-watering price tag of 3.29 million Korean won (around $2,400 to $2,500), consumers expected the polished perfectionism that Apple has spent decades cultivating. Instead, the rocky debut has triggered intense scrutiny over the device's physical resilience, software readiness, and whether entering this market late was a calculated move or a rushed gamble.

The Fragility of First Attempts: Anatomy of "Crackgate"

Foldable devices live and die by their hinges. Apple promoted the iPhone Duo as a triumph of structural engineering, highlighting internal reinforcement plates built with titanium. Yet real-world usage and independent stress tests revealed an unforgiving reality: the physical stress generated by the folding mechanism appears to transfer directly onto the delicate display substrate.

The severity of these structural vulnerabilities became unmistakable when popular tech reviewer Zack Nelson put the iPhone Duo through his signature teardown and durability gauntlet on the YouTube channel JerryRigEverything. The results were devastating. When subjected to standard sand and particulate matter tests, dirt penetrated the hinge housing, preventing the handset from shutting completely. The device’s environmental seals failed to keep ordinary grit from fouling the mechanical gears.

The mechanical stress test went even worse. When bent in the reverse direction, the iPhone Duo’s internal panel failed catastrophically—sprouting vivid green failure lines before shutting off entirely, earning Nelson’s blunt verdict of "Destroyed." Compounding the frustration is the sheer financial risk passed on to early adopters. Apple’s out-of-warranty internal display repair fee sits at $999. In practical terms, breaking the screen once costs an owner nearly a third of the phone’s original retail price, turning a luxury gadget into an anxiety-inducing piece of fragile glassware.

Software in Disarray: Black Bars and Vanishing Buttons

While the hardware flaws have captured headlines, the software execution has drawn equally sharp condemnation. Historically, Apple’s greatest strength has been tight harmony between hardware architecture and operating systems. On the iPhone Duo, that reputation has taken a direct hit.

Japanese tech publication ITMedia Mobile highlighted the problem with a cutting headline: “Is the iPhone Duo Full of Black Bars on LINE and Pokémon GO?” Because third-party applications have not been retrofitted for the handset’s peculiar internal dimensions, popular messaging apps and mobile games fail to adapt dynamically. When launching the ubiquitous messenger app LINE in folded mode, users find a cramped view displaying merely two lines of conversation. Unfold the phone into tablet mode, and the software simply centers the standard phone-sized interface in the middle of the expansive panel, flanking it with massive pillars of dead black space.

“Essentially, a completely new platform was pushed onto developers with barely a month of preparation time.” — Steve Troughton-Smith, software developer

Prominent Apple commentator John Gruber shared a telling vignette about the operating system’s current user-experience woes. While using an app called Unforgetful, an active podcast status bar squeezed the application toolbar so severely that the essential "Save" button was pushed into an overflow submenu, leaving him hunting through settings for a minute just to perform a basic action. On Apple developer discussion forums, engineers reported alarming bugs in early simulators, such as entire columns disappearing from standard data tables. Rather than redesigning the mobile computing experience around a larger canvas, the operating system frequently feels like an awkward compromise that penalizes the user for opening the screen.

The Long Shadow of Samsung’s Eight-Year Grind

The unexpected fallout from Apple's debut has produced an intriguing byproduct: millions of global smartphone users are suddenly revisiting footage of Samsung’s flagship Galaxy Z Fold 8. In the comment sections of Nelson’s durability videos, viewers routinely post variations of the same remark: “Who else came here right after watching the iPhone Duo video?”

When the Fold 8 underwent the identical reverse-bend test on JerryRigEverything, its outer protective glass fractured under extreme torque, but the flexible central display remained operational. Nelson marveled at the unit’s toughness, likening Samsung’s engineering team to mythical "Mithril craftsmen."

That resilience is not accidental. It represents eight generations of trial, humiliation, and architectural reinvention. Korean tech watchers remember all too well the spring of 2019, when Samsung faced international embarrassment after early review units of the original Galaxy Fold experienced screen flickers and peeling display layers, forcing an unprecedented global release postponement. Rather than abandoning the form factor, Samsung engineers worked through nearly a hundred failed structural concepts—eventually adopting microscopic nylon sweeping bristles inspired by domestic vacuum cleaner brushes to lock dirt out of the hinge.

Under the surface of Samsung's Fold 8 lies an intricate sandwich of precision-etched support plates and flexible alloy foils measured in microns, alongside an operating layer that allows users to force custom aspect ratios, tile three apps in a T-shape, and treat the screen as an adaptive workstation. This hardware maturity arrives at a notable domestic moment in South Korea. While the administration of President Lee Jae-myung—led by the governing Democratic Party of Korea—has made technological self-reliance and domestic semiconductor preeminence central planks of its industrial platform, Samsung’s foldable portfolio has quietly become the global baseline against which all foreign rivals are measured.

What to Watch Next

The unfolding drama in the foldable market signals that early-mover advantage in mechanical engineering cannot simply be erased by brand cachet. Moving forward, observers should watch several critical indicators:

  • Warranty Policies and Apple’s Quality Control Response: Watch whether Apple introduces revised hinge tolerances, internal silicone gasketing, or an expanded service program to subsidize the punitive $999 display repair fee for users experiencing spontaneous hinge fractures.
  • iOS Developer Guidelines and Rapid API Patches: Monitor how fast Apple updates its Human Interface Guidelines to eliminate the persistent black pillar-boxing on flagship third-party applications, providing developers with automated responsive-layout tools.
  • Samsung’s Maintenance of the "Super-Gap": Despite its current engineering lead, Samsung faces growing competition from Google and Chinese manufacturers in display crease reduction and slimness. Industry analysts will be tracking whether Samsung lowers repair costs through modular internal layouts and deepens its domestic supply chain alliances—much like the regional strategic infrastructure investments being seen in initiatives like Jeju’s provincial growth funds.

The foldable era has progressed past the novelty phase into unforgiving operational reality. As early adopters weigh the steep price tags of dual-screen hardware, durability and software optimization are no longer optional luxuries—they are the entire foundation of the category.
